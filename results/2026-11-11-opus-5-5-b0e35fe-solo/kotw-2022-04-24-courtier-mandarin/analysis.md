# Courtier / Mandarin kingdom (2 players)

**Board:** This is a money board with good add-ons. Barge is the only real draw, and it's terminal. Steward draws +2 and is also terminal. Village Green draws only one card. Trashing is slow (Steward, Way of the Goat). So the "engine" here is really a good-stuff deck that reaches about $13 at best, while Barge/Courtier money is quick and steady. +Buy comes from Barge, Courtier and Gamble, but no deck here reliably makes more than $13–15. Mandarin and Silk Road are first-edition only (both were removed from Hinterlands 2nd edition).

**Key interactions:**
- **Courtier + multi-type cards.** Courtier gives one choice per type of the card it reveals.
  - Revealing Village Green (Action–Duration–Reaction) gives +Action, +$3 and a Gold, so Courtier doesn't use up an Action.
  - Revealing Barge or Cardinal gives +Action and +$3.
  - Revealing Tunnel (Victory–Reaction) gives +$3 and a Gold, as a terminal.
  - In money, revealing an Estate or a Treasure still gives +$3 or a Gold, which is close to a Gold that also gains you something.
- **Gamble discards the top card of your deck.** If that card is Tunnel, you gain a Gold. If it's Village Green, you can play it, and choosing "next turn" gives +1 Card and +2 Actions next turn (this happened in my test game on turn 11). Barge or Village Green played this way can use the "next turn" option, so they work without spending an Action.
- **Mandarin + Gamble:** Mandarin puts a card from your hand on top of your deck, so Gamble can hit a chosen Tunnel or Barge. That's about +$1 net, plus a Gold or a free Barge next turn. It's cute but small.
- **Displace turns a Gold into a Province** (Gold costs $6, plus $2 is $8), and you still spend the other four cards in your hand. Courtier keeps gaining Golds to feed it. This is a strong greening play late in the game.
- **Cardinal vs Tunnel:** the player being attacked picks what to Exile. If they reveal Tunnel plus another card, they Exile the other card and discard Tunnel to gain a Gold. Cardinal is weak against anyone holding Tunnels.

**Other cards:**
- **Cardinal:** the best target is a money opponent's Silver or Gold. Gold costs $6, so it's in the $3–$6 range. A player gets exiled Golds back when they gain another Gold, so the damage only lasts once they start greening.
- **Silk Road:** it pairs with Tunnels and Duchies. By the endgame a money deck has about 12 Victory cards, so Silk Road is worth about 3 VP for $4.
- **Lurker:** none. Trashing a Courtier from the Supply and gaining it back takes two Lurkers per gain, which is too slow.
- **Steward:** it pairs with Village Green in the engine. In money it's just a flexible terminal.
- **Way of the Goat:** it turns a dead or Gamble-played Action into trashing an Estate.

**Plans considered:**
1. **Barge/Courtier Big Money** (one Barge, one or two Courtiers, Golds; Displace and Tunnels or Silk Roads late). Played in `dominion solo`, it had 4 Provinces by turn 12 on both shuffles. Its weakness is the opponent's Cardinal.
2. **Village Green / Barge / Courtier / Steward good-stuff "engine"** with Gamble. Two runs gave 4 Provinces by turn 14 (a sloppy build) and turn 13. Its first $13 turn came on turn 10. It never reached $16 or more, because there's no draw beyond Barge and thinning is slow.
3. **Tunnel / Silk Road slog.** It's capped at roughly 8 Tunnels for 16 VP plus 8 Silk Roads at 4–5 VP each. That's slower than Provinces and not a real candidate.

**Against their best plan:** In a money mirror, Cardinal and the Displace/Silk Road endgame decide it, and buying one Tunnel protects you from Cardinal. If the opponent builds the engine, money is a turn ahead by T12 (4 Provinces to 3). Cardinal also hurts the engine more, because it exiles the $4–5 pieces it draws next turn.

**Advocate's case:** The engine's best version opens Steward to trash Estates, uses Courtier on Village Green as non-terminal money that also gains Gold, and fires Barge and Village Green for free with Gamble. It still falls behind: on turn 12 it has 3 Provinces to money's 4. Its top turn was $13 on two Buys, not two Provinces a turn, so it never catches up. The solo games had no opponent attacks, and Cardinal would widen that gap.

**Recommendation:** Play **Barge/Courtier Big Money**.
- Get one Barge first, then Courtier, then Golds.
- Add a second Courtier or a Displace around turns 9–11.
- In the endgame, buy Duchy or Silk Road at $4–5 and Tunnel at $3, and use Displace on a Gold to gain a Province.
- Pick up a Cardinal at $4 if the opponent is playing money with no Tunnels.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Silver/Silver. If you're first player and they look like money, Cardinal/Silver is fine.
- **5/2:** Courtier/nothing. Courtier revealing an Estate gives a Gold or +$3 from turn 3. Nothing at $2 is worth it: Lurker does nothing here, and Gamble just cycles a card.

**Buy priorities:**
- $8: Province.
- $6–7: Gold (Courtier + Gamble at $7 once you have a Barge).
- $5: Barge (one), then Courtier (one or two).
- $3–4: Silver.
- Late: Displace at one $5. Start greening at 4–5 Provinces left: Duchy or Silk Road at $4–5, Tunnel at $3–4.
- Add a Village Green only if you end up with three or more terminals.

**Ending:** Most likely the Provinces run out around T16–18. Three piles emptying is unlikely, since there's no junker and no cheap gainer.

**Watch out for:**
- The opponent's Cardinal exiling your Golds late. Buy a Tunnel as cheap insurance.
- Too many terminals colliding (Barge, Courtier, Cardinal, Displace). Keep it to about one per 7–8 cards.

**Least sure of:** whether a better-built Courtier/Village Green engine, using Mandarin/Gamble to set up Barge each turn, could reach two Provinces per turn by about T12. My two runs never got above $13. A third engine run on another shuffle would test it.

**Assumed:**
- Mandarin and Silk Road use the first-edition text given in the kingdom file.
- No Colonies or Shelters (the file says "unknown").
- The player being attacked chooses which card Cardinal Exiles.
- Gamble-played Durations can choose "next turn". The engine allowed this.
