## Beyond the key
- Remodel Gold → Province as a second gain on a one-Buy turn: plausible. Gold costs $6, so Remodel can gain an $8 card.
- Duration cards from last turn (Amulet, Enchantress, Grotto) count toward Peddler's discount, so 4 Actions in play make Peddler $0: plausible. Durations stay in play through the turn they resolve, and Peddler counts Action cards in play.
- Remodeling into Farmhands lets you set aside an Amulet or Gold from the rest of your hand to play next turn: plausible. Farmhands' on-gain sets aside an Action or Treasure from hand.
- Gladiator → Fortune: emptying the 5 Gladiators exposes Fortune, which doubles $ and adds a Buy, so a $10 hand buys 2 Provinces: plausible from the card text, though the Debt makes it slow.
- Enchantress hits only the first Action, so play a Grotto/Amulet/Farmhands before Remodel to absorb it: plausible. That follows from "the first time each other player plays an Action card".
- Transmute is weak because Potion is a dead $4: plausible. Nothing else uses Potion, and Transmute's gains (Duchy, Transmute, Gold) don't help this plan.
- Monastery gets few triggers in a one-gain-per-turn deck: plausible. It trashes one card per card gained this turn.
- A three-pile ending is unrealistic: plausible. No cheap +Buy drains piles, and the key also has the game ending on Provinces.

## Summary
The analysis reads Remodel correctly, including Peddler's $8 printed cost as Province fodder, and avoids Monastery. But it calls the board a money board with no draw. It dismisses Grotto as filler and rejects the Peddler/Farmhands/Foray action deck the winner played, in favor of an Amulet/Gold money deck. A player following it would have the right endgame trick with the wrong deck.
