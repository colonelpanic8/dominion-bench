I've finished the analysis. Of the three plans I played out, money with Hunting Grounds was clearly fastest. The Scrying Pool engine and a deck built around Forge both fell well behind it.

---

**Board:** There's no +Buy anywhere, no Colonies, and the only junk is the Coppers that Noble Brigand hands out. That makes it a one-buy-per-turn Province game. Forge can trash Hunting Grounds together with any $2 card for **Province + Duchy** (9 VP in one Action), which looks like the standout. Turn the board over, though, and the real draw is Hunting Grounds (terminal +4 Cards). The villages are conditional: Encampment needs a Gold in hand, Coin of the Realm works once per copy, and Scrying Pool needs a deck full of Actions. The only fast thinning is Trader. The open question was whether Forge + Hunting Grounds, or a Scrying Pool engine, could beat money with Hunting Grounds.

**Key interactions:**
- **Forge + Hunting Grounds + a $2 card** (Estate, Coin of the Realm, Encampment, Beggar or Scrying Pool): trash $8 worth, gain a Province, and Hunting Grounds' trash trigger adds a Duchy. That's 9 VP for one Forge play. The `dominion solo` engine gained Province + Duchy, as expected. Hunting Grounds + 2 Coppers instead gains a new Hunting Grounds + Duchy and thins 2 Coppers. Gold + Estate, or 4 Estates, also make a Province. **Catch:** you need Forge, Hunting Grounds and fodder in the same hand, with only one Action, so it happens about once a game.
- **Trader + Estate:** 2 Silvers. Trader on Hunting Grounds gives 6 Silvers + Duchy, which is cute but not worth a $6 card.
- **Hunting Grounds → Coin of the Realm → Forge:** call Coin of the Realm after playing Hunting Grounds for +2 Actions, then Forge something the draw found. This fixes the collision, but only when Coin of the Realm is already on the Tavern mat.

**Other cards:**
- **Scrying Pool:** its partners are Encampment and Coin of the Realm (Action density). But nothing cheap thins Coppers, so it bogs down. Its rating (+3.05) assumes trashing and villages this board lacks.
- **Encampment:** stays only if you reveal a Gold. In a money deck that has Golds it's a decent lab-village late, otherwise it's a one-shot.
- **Scholar:** a worse X than Hunting Grounds here, because it discards your hand to reach 7 cards.
- **Mandarin:** a terminal +$3 that collides with Hunting Grounds. Its on-gain effect topdecks Treasures, which is mildly useful at most.
- **Noble Brigand:** best against a Silver-heavy opponent (steals their Silver or Gold), but it's terminal and collides with Hunting Grounds. It's rated deprecated and I agree with that.
- **Beggar:** a reaction to Noble Brigand and Scrying Pool (gain 2 Silvers). Otherwise it adds Coppers, so skip it.
- **Trader's reaction:** it can turn a Copper from Noble Brigand into a Silver. A small bonus.

**Plans considered:**
1. **Money with Hunting Grounds, opening Trader** (one late Forge optional) · first $6 on T5, first Province T8–9, 4 Provinces + Duchy by T15 in both seeds · needs Hunting Grounds by T5–6 · weakness: it's a plain money deck with no ceiling.
2. **Forge / Hunting Grounds combo deck** (keep Estates, 2–3 Hunting Grounds, Forge early, Coin of the Realm) · 17 VP by T15 · needs Forge + Hunting Grounds + fodder together · weakness: they line up too rarely, and kept Estates plus Forge cost economy.
3. **Scrying Pool engine** with Encampment and Forge as payload · T9: 3 Scrying Pools, no Forge or Hunting Grounds, 0 VP gained · weakness: no Copper trashing, no +Buy, payload costs $6–7.

### Build lines (`dominion solo`, 2-player setup, idle opponent)
- **Money with Hunting Grounds, seed 1 (4/3):**
  - T1–2: Trader / Silver.
  - T3: Trader trashes an Estate (2 Silvers), buy Silver. T4: Silver. T5: Hunting Grounds. T6: Forge ($7).
  - T7: Trader trashes an Estate, buy Silver. T8: Hunting Grounds draws to $14, buy Province.
  - T9: Gold. T11: Province. T12: Gold. T13: Province.
  - T15: Forge trashes Hunting Grounds + Estate for Province + Duchy. Total **27 VP on T15**.
- **Money with Hunting Grounds, seed 2:**
  - T1–2: Silver / Trader. T3: Trader, Silver. T4: Silver. T5: Hunting Grounds. T6: Gold.
  - T8: Forge. T9: Province. T12: Province. T13: Duchy. T14–15: Province ×2. Total **28 VP on T15**. Forge never fired.
- **Advocate (Forge/Hunting Grounds deck, seed 1):**
  - T1–5: Silver ×4. T6–7: Hunting Grounds ×2. T8: Forge.
  - T9: Province. T11: Forge trashes Estate + Silver for a Duchy.
  - T12: Province. T13: Hunting Grounds.
  - T14: Hunting Grounds drew Forge + Hunting Grounds + Estate with no Action left.
  - Total **17 VP on T15**.
- **Scrying Pool engine (seed 1):** Potion / Silver, then Scrying Pool, Trader, Silver, Scrying Pool, Scrying Pool, Silver. Still $4 hands on T9 with 0 VP gained, so I stopped there.

**Against their best plan:** the opponent's best plan is the same money mirror. That comes down to Province split, turn order, and the Penultimate Province Rule. Noble Brigand is a possible tie-breaker against a deck full of Silver from Trader. One copy might be worth it when going first, but it collides with Hunting Grounds. The Scrying Pool engine loses the race outright: at T9 it had no payload, while money was already taking Provinces.

**Advocate's case:** "9 VP per Forge play beats 6 VP per Province, so build around it." Played out, it fell behind from T9 onward. By T15 it trailed by about 10 VP (17 vs 27–28). The reasons were a bad Estate-for-Duchy Forge on T11, and a T14 where all three pieces showed up with no Action to use them. It never caught up.

**Recommendation:** Money with Hunting Grounds, opening Trader. Use Forge only as a single late $7 buy, once you have a Gold. Keep one Estate or a Coin of the Realm around so that when Forge and Hunting Grounds collide, you can cash them for Province + Duchy.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Trader / Silver.
- **5/2:** Trader at $5, plus Coin of the Realm at $2 (fodder, and later +2 Actions to play Forge after Hunting Grounds). Nothing better exists at either price.

**Buy priorities:**
- Trader on Estates early (each one becomes 2 Silvers), but keep the last Estate.
- At $6, get Hunting Grounds first, then Gold. Stop at 2 Hunting Grounds.
- At $7: one Forge after the first Gold, Gold otherwise. At $8+: Province.
- Duchies at $5–7 once about 4 Provinces are left.

**Ending:** Provinces run out around T16–18 in the mirror. No pile is at risk of a three-pile ending, since there's no +Buy.

**How the pick loses:** to a better Province split or turn order, or to the opponent's Noble Brigand repeatedly stealing Golds. Beggar or more Hunting Grounds help against that.

**Watch out for:**
- Don't Trader away every Estate. You want fodder for Forge.
- Don't buy a second terminal beyond Hunting Grounds ×2 + Forge without Coin of the Realm or Encampment.
- With 3+ players, the attacks from Noble Brigand and Scrying Pool get stronger, and Beggar's reaction gets better.

**Least sure of:** that the Scrying Pool engine can't be fixed. I played one seed with a mediocre build: no Encampments, and I wasted $2 on one Scrying Pool buy. An Encampment-heavy version with early Gold might be faster. Still, without +Buy or Copper thinning, I doubt it beats T8–9 Provinces.

**Assumed:**
- Forge treats Province as cost "exactly $8" (the engine agreed), and Potion counts as $4 for Forge.
- One seed per alternative plan. Both money games had average draws.
- The idle opponent hides what Noble Brigand and Scrying Pool's attacks would cost.
