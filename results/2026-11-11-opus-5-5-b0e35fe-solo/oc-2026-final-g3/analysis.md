I played four `dominion solo` lines: two engines and two money decks on two seeds. Money with Council Room and the Scrounge Event won clearly on both seeds, so it's my pick, even though the board has villages, draw and +Buy.

# Kingdom analysis: oc-2026-final-g3

**Board:** Council Room, Horn of Plenty, Inn, Patrol, Upgrade, Bandit Camp (all $5), Walled Village ($4), Forager ($3), Golem ($4 + Potion), Overlord (8 Debt), plus the Scrounge Event ($3). Spoils is the only non-Supply pile; Potion is used for Golem. No Colonies, no Shelters, 2 players. The starting deck is normal, so the opening is 4/3 or 5/2.

The board looks like an engine board: three villages, two draw cards, +Buy on Council Room and Forager, and two trashers. But the draw is thin. Council Room (+4) and Patrol (+3) are terminal, and Inn only sifts. The payoff that matters is **Scrounge**. Whenever an Estate is in the trash, Scrounge turns $3 into an Estate plus any card costing up to $5. There are no attacks, and Council Room gives the opponent a card.

**Key interactions:**
- **Scrounge + Estates in the trash.** Option 1 trashes a card from your hand (usually an Estate) for $3. Option 2 takes that Estate back and gains a card costing up to $5. So $3 buys a $5 engine piece early, and late it buys **Duchy + Estate (4 VP)**. With Council Room's 2 Buys, **$6 gets 8 VP, more than a Province.** In the solo lines this showed up on turn 3 (Forager trashes an Estate, then Scrounge gains Upgrade) and on turn 12 of the first money line (two Scrounges for 8 VP).
- **Upgrade or Forager + Scrounge.** Upgrade turns an Estate into a Silver or Forager. Forager trashes it and adds a Buy. Either way the Estate ends up in the trash, ready for Scrounge. Trashed Estates are shared: the opponent can Scrounge yours.
- **Horn of Plenty counts different card names in play.** An engine reaches 8 or more names (village, Council Room, Upgrade, Forager, Copper, Silver, Gold, Horn), so Horn gains a Province. It then trashes itself, so it's a one-shot. Below 8 names it gains a Gold or a $5 card. Play Horn before Spoils, because Spoils returns to its pile as soon as it's played. I lost a name that way once.
- **Walled Village** returns to the top of your deck in light turns. That helps a Council Room money deck more than an engine.

**Other cards:**
- Inn: only sifts, and its on-gain shuffle just recycles Actions. Weak partner here.
- Patrol: pulls Victory cards into hand, which helps Forager or Upgrade find an Estate. Otherwise a weaker Council Room.
- Bandit Camp: village plus Spoils, which adds money and Horn names. Fine in an engine.
- Overlord: copies any $5 Action for 8 Debt. Too slow here.
- Golem: none. The board has few Actions to find, and the Potion costs too much.
- Ratings check: Horn of Plenty, Upgrade and Forager rate highly and do matter, mainly as engine parts. Council Room rates low (−0.94), but its +Buy is what makes double Scrounge work. Scrounge rates low (−0.98), but it carries this board.

### Build lines (`dominion solo`)
- **Engine, seed 1:** opened Silver/Forager. Turn 4: Scrounge → Upgrade. Turn 8: two Scrounges → Patrol and Horn. Turns 9–12 were spent building (Inn, Bandit Camps, second Council Room, Horn). The deck stalled: Council Rooms drawn without villages, $5–7 hands. First greening on turn 13. **9 VP on turn 13, 15 VP on turn 14.**
- **Engine advocate, seed 2:** played tighter. Opened Forager/Silver. Turn 3: Scrounge → Upgrade. Turn 5: Scrounge → Council Room. Then Bandit Camp, Walled Village ×2, Horn and Gold. Big turn on turn 11: Horn gained a Province (9 names) and two Scrounges gave Duchy + Estate twice, for **15 VP on turn 11.** It stalled straight after: $5 on turn 12, $2 on turn 13. **18 VP on turn 13.**
- **Money + Council Room + Scrounge, seed 1:** Silver/Silver, Council Room on turn 3, Silver, Silver. Turn 6: $9, bought Gold and Scrounged an Estate away. Turn 7: Gold. Turn 8: second Council Room. Turns 9–10: $11 each, Province plus Scrounge-trash each turn. Turn 11: Gold. Turn 12: two Scrounges, 2 × (Duchy + Estate). Turns 13–14: Provinces. **20 VP on turn 12, 32 VP on turn 14.**
- **Money, seed 2:** turns 3 and 6–7 bought Gold. Turn 8: $11, Province plus Scrounge-trash. Turn 9: Scrounge → second Council Room. Turn 10: $15, Province + Gold. Turns 11–12: Provinces. **4 Provinces, 27 VP, by turn 12.**

**Plans considered:**
- **Money + 2 Council Rooms + Scrounge** (pick): Gold at $6–7, Province at $8. Use spare Buys to Scrounge Estates away, then Scrounge Duchy + Estate for $3 late. First $8 on turns 6–8, 4 Provinces by turns 12–14. Weakness: Council Room draws the opponent a card.
- **Engine** (Forager/Upgrade, Walled Village/Bandit Camp, Council Room/Patrol, Horn and Scrounge as payload): its first big turn came on turn 11–13 and it couldn't repeat it. The only non-terminal draw is +1 per village, so it's inconsistent. Weakness: too little draw.
- **Good-stuff / Duchy deck:** Forager and Upgrade feeding Scrounge Duchies. It's mostly contained in both lines above. On its own it's slower than Provinces.

**Against their best plan:** In a money mirror, Council Room still helps both players about equally. The Duchy and Scrounge timing decides the game: Scrounge empties the Duchies faster than usual, so start taking 4-VP Scrounges once about 5 Provinces are left. Against an engine, money is about 8 VP ahead on turn 13 in both seeds, before counting the cards your Council Rooms give them.

**Advocate's case:** The fastest engine version uses Scrounge as a discount from turn 3 and a 9-name Horn for its Province. It falls behind on **turn 12–13: 18 VP against 21–26 for money**, and then stalls, because Horn trashes itself and the deck has no repeatable draw. My engine play wasn't perfect, so a stronger player might close a turn of that gap. Closing ~8 VP by turn 13 is unlikely.

**Recommendation:** Big Money with two Council Rooms, using Scrounge both as Estate trashing and as the Duchy + Estate scorer. It won both seeds by a wide margin. The engine's only real payoff, Scrounge, works just as well in the money deck, so the engine gains nothing over it.

**Open:**
- 4/3: Silver/Silver.
- 5/2: Council Room and nothing at $2.

**Buy priorities:**
- Council Room at $5, until you have two.
- Gold at $6–7.
- Province at $8.
- With a spare Buy and $3, Scrounge to trash an Estate in hand.
- From about 5–6 Provinces left: $3 per Buy → Scrounge Duchy + Estate. Prefer two Scrounges (8 VP) over a Province at $6–7, and do both if $11 or more with 2 Buys.
- Avoid the second-to-last Province when the opponent can take the last one and win (Penultimate Province Rule).

**Ending:** Provinces run out, probably around turns 14–17. Duchies can empty early because Scrounge makes them cheap. A three-pile ending is unlikely.

**Watch out for:**
- The opponent Scrounging Estates you trashed. Don't leave Estates in the trash for them late in the game.
- Council Room helping them.
- If the opponent goes engine and its draw comes together (more Council Rooms, Patrols and Bandit Camps), check the race clock each turn.

**Least sure of:** Whether a better-built engine (more Council Rooms and Patrols, Horn used for Golds instead of a one-shot Province) can reach two Provinces per turn by turn 11. Both of my engine lines stalled, and the gap may partly be my sequencing. The test is a third engine line on another seed. Patrol or a Horn as a second X in the money deck was also not tested.

**How the pick loses:** Against a well-built engine that keeps hitting big turns from turn 11, or if the opponent gets more Scrounge value from shared Estates. Both would show in their buys by turns 6–8.

**Assumed:**
- 2 players. With more players, each Council Room gives more cards away, so prefer Patrol as X.
- Scrounge's second option requires an Estate in the trash. `dominion solo` confirmed this.
- The opponent's attacks aren't modelled, and there are none on this board.
