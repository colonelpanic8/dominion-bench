# Kingdom analysis: oc-2026-final-g1

**Board:** Tunnel $3, Council Room $5, Jester $5, Litter $5, Patrol $5, Vineyard $0+Potion, Border Guard $2, Cage $2, Astrolabe $3, Market Square $3. Landscapes are Way of the Squirrel and Seaway ($5 Event). Two players, no Colonies, no Shelters, normal 7 Copper / 3 Estate start (4/3 or 5/2). Debt comes from Litter, a Potion from Vineyard, and Lantern/Horn from Border Guard.

- **What it's about:** You score with Provinces, plus Tunnels (2 VP for $3) and Curses from Jester. There is no real trasher, and almost all the draw and the only village cost $5.
- **Engine pieces:** Litter is a village and draw in one card (+2 Cards +2 Actions, but each play costs $1 of debt). Council Room and Patrol are the terminal draw. Market Square, Council Room and Astrolabe give +Buy.
- **The open question:** whether that engine beats Council Room–Big Money with no trashing and payload that is mostly Copper. In my solo games, it didn't.

**Key interactions:**
- **Cage as a temporary trasher.** Play Cage and set aside up to 4 Estates (later Provinces). They stay out of your deck until you next gain a Victory card. Then they come back to your hand at end of turn, as extra cards on top of the normal 5. Cage itself stays in play, so it takes no deck slot.
- **Cage + Market Square makes Golds.** When you gain a Victory card, every Cage in play is trashed, and each Market Square in hand can be discarded to gain a Gold.
  - The engine checked this: two Market Squares in hand react to one Cage being trashed, and each gains a Gold.
  - So on a green turn, keep your Market Squares in hand instead of playing them: Province + 2 Golds on turn 10 (first game), turn 12 (second game).
  - Cage only triggers on a Victory gain, so this is a payout on green turns, not a way to build.
- **Tunnel + discarding.** Border Guard discards a revealed card, so revealing Tunnel gains a Gold. The opponent's Jester can also discard your Tunnel: you gain a Gold and a Curse.
- **Jester against a money deck.** If it hits Silver or Gold, you take a copy. If it hits a Victory card, they take a Curse. If it hits Copper, you hand them a Copper.

**Other cards:**
- **Patrol:** its best partner is Litter. In the engine it doesn't gift cards the way Council Room does, and late it pulls green cards out of your next draws.
- **Vineyard:** only worth it in a deck with 12+ Actions (4 VP each), and it costs a dead $4 Potion. Usually skip.
- **Astrolabe:** a Silver with +Buy for money decks. Not needed alongside Council Room.
- **Seaway:** $5 for a Market Square or Border Guard plus a +Buy token. Weaker than any $5 card here.
- **Way of the Squirrel:** use it on a spare Action once your deck is drawn, for a 7-card next hand. Minor.
- **Border Guard:** a sifting cantrip. Lantern/Horn are hard to take with this few Actions. Only matters with Tunnels.

**Plans considered:**
1. **Litter engine:** Litter ×3–4, Council Room ×1–2 or Patrol, Market Squares, Cage thinning, Jester, and Golds from Market Square on green turns. Needs about 3 Litters by turn 9. It's weak because there's no trashing, the Estates come back when you green, Council Room feeds the opponent, and results swing with the shuffle.
2. **Council Room–Big Money with Jester and Cage:** one Council Room, then Gold and Province. Add one Jester as a second terminal, and buy a Cage with spare +Buy to take Estates out early. Steady, but caps at about one Province per turn.
3. **Tunnel / Border Guard Golds:** a Tunnel-heavy deck that digs with Border Guards. Not played out. About 40% Gold per Border Guard play doesn't make up for the green cards clogging the deck.

### Build lines (`dominion solo`)

- **Engine, seed 1 (4/3):** Silver / Cage → turn 3 Cage takes 2 Estates, buys Market Square → turn 5 Litter → turn 6 Council Room → turn 7 ($10) Litter, Market Square, Cage → turn 9 Litter, Council Room → turn 10 Province + 2 Golds (Market Squares held) → turn 11 two Provinces ($16, 5 Buys) → turn 12 Province + Gold (4 Provinces) → turn 13 dud reshuffle, $5.
- **Engine refined, seed 1** (Jester and Patrol instead of the second Council Room): Province + Gold on turn 10, 2 Provinces by turn 12, 3 by turn 13.
- **Engine, seed 2:** Council Rooms collided. First Province (+ 2 Golds) on turn 12, 2 by turn 13.
- **Council Room–BM, seed 1:** Silver, Silver, Council Room on turn 3 → Gold + Silver on turn 6 → Provinces on turns 7, 11, 12, 13 (4 by turn 13).
- **Council Room–BM, seed 2:** Province turn 7, then Golds on $6–7 hands, then turns 12 and 13 (3 by turn 13, with $14 that turn).

**Against their best plan:**
- **Money mirror:** Jester decides it. It takes their Golds and Silvers and curses their green cards, while Council Room's extra card helps both sides equally.
- **Against an engine:** money scores 3–5 turns earlier, and the engine needs about two double-Province turns to catch up. It managed one in three seeds. Jester taking their Litters or giving them Curses slows them further.
- **The engine against money:** every Council Room play hands the money deck a card.

**Advocate's case** (for the engine): Litter is a cheap village plus Lab, and Cage thinning stands in for trashing. On a good seed the engine had 4 Provinces on turn 12, one turn ahead of money, with Gold bursts on top.

- **Where it falls behind:** on turn 7 money has 6 VP against the engine's 0. Across seeds, money is level or ahead at turn 13: 24 to 27 VP in seed 1 (only the first engine game had more), 21 to 9–15 in seed 2.
- **Why:** the engine's ceiling is set by its Coppers, since nothing trashes them. And Cage returns the Estates on the first green turn.

**Recommendation: Council Room–Big Money with one Jester and a Cage.** Take a Litter only when you hit $5 and already have Council Room and Jester, so both terminals can be played together.

- **Why money:** played out, the engine never pulled clearly ahead and swung more with the shuffle. Money takes the early Provinces. The attack and +Buy cards here (Jester, Council Room) suit the money deck, and the engine has no Jester-proof payload.
- **Jester as the X:** against a money opponent it is close to a Silver plus a real attack.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Silver / Silver (Silver / Cage is close).
- **5/2:** Council Room / Cage. Going first, Jester / Cage is reasonable.

**Buy priorities:**
- Council Room first, then Jester.
- $8: Province. $6–7: Gold.
- Use spare $2 with a +Buy on a Cage, and play it on your Estates.
- Market Square at $3 only if you plan to hold them for Golds on Province turns.
- Green as in Big Money: Duchy at $5–7 once 4–5 Provinces are left. Late, Tunnel beats Estate at $3–4.
- When you buy a Province with a Cage in play, hold any Market Squares to collect the Golds.

**Ending:** the Provinces run out around turns 16–19. A three-pile ending is unlikely; Curses (if both sides play Jester) plus Tunnels is the only realistic pair.

**Watch out for:**
- **The opponent's Jester:** keep Tunnels in your deck, since a Jester hit on one gains you a Gold.
- **Council Room helps them:** if they build the Litter engine, your Council Room plays feed it. Switch to Patrol.
- **More players:** Jester and Council Room's gift both grow with more players.

**How the pick loses:** the engine strings together 2-Province turns from turn 11 on (as in seed 1) while you stall on $6–7 hands. You can see it coming when they take Litters on turns 5–7. Answer with Jester, which takes Litters from them.

**Least sure of:**
- **The engine verdict.** I played three engine games on two seeds, and one of them was a turn ahead of money. A better engine build (more Cages to take Coppers out, Patrol instead of Council Room) could flip it. Test with more seeds.
- **Jester as the X.** Solo play can't measure an attack, so I assumed Jester beats a second Council Room rather than measuring it.

**Assumed:**
- Rulings shown by the engine: Cage's set-aside cards return to hand after the clean-up draw; several Market Squares can react to one trash; and a Cage played in the Buy phase triggers the same turn.
- The opponent's attacks don't appear in solo play.
- Glicko ratings (Litter 0.54, Council Room −0.94, Jester −1.81, Cage 0.09) roughly agree with the analysis. I rate Jester above its rating because the opponents here play money.
