## Beyond the key
- Tent topdecks itself during Clean-up, so it is in every hand, giving +$2 a turn without diluting the shuffle, but it collides with other terminals: plausible. That follows from Tent's below-the-line text; with no village, a second terminal often dies.
- Cabin Boy can trash itself to gain a Stronghold with no cost cap, once Stronghold is on top of the Forts: plausible. "Gain a Duration card" has no cost limit, and Stronghold is a Duration.
- Reckless Guide is one-shot: doubled on play, called once, then returned to its pile when discarded from play: plausible. Calling isn't playing, so Reckless doesn't double the call, and a called Guide is discarded from play. It doesn't explain how the key sees one or two Guides as useful.
- Exploration is marginal: doubtful. Hill Fort, Cabin Boy and Garrison gains happen in the Action phase, so turns that gain without buying still pay Coffers + Villager. That is the likely reason for Horton's Tent/Exploration opening.
- Ducat thinning strips money faster than gains replace it: plausible. Ducat makes no $ when played, only 1 Coffers, and its on-gain trashes a Copper.
- No +Buy except Ducat, and no way to discount Provinces: plausible from card text.
- A 3-pile ending is very unlikely because Guides return to their pile: plausible.

## Summary
The analysis builds Steward/Tent/Destrier money and explicitly dismisses Garrison, Hill Fort, Exploration and Reckless Guide, the pieces of Horton's playtested line. It never contests the Hill Forts, which is the key's trap. It does read the Forts pile order, Tent's topdeck and the Ducat/Hill Fort→Destrier discount correctly, but it rejects the plan built on them.
