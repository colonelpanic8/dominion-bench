## Beyond the key
- "The deck can't draw itself, so this is not an engine board": doubtful. The net-draw count ignores Tools gaining Lost Cities and Caravans each turn, and Villa gained to hand mid-turn. Both finalists built Tools/Lost City/Caravan/Villa decks that drew well.
- A Villa bought in the Buy phase lets you play more Treasures afterwards, net $3 with +Buy: plausible. Villa returns you to the Action phase, and its +$1 offsets $1 of the $4 cost.
- Tools with a Gold in play gains a Gold, and Tools can copy itself: plausible. Tools gains "a copy of a card anyone has in play", and played Treasures are in play.
- Borrow is +$1 at the cost of a card next turn, worth it only at price points; its +Buy just replaces the Buy it uses: plausible. That follows from the card text and Events costing a Buy.
- On a 5/2, Borrow on turn 2 reaches $3 for Sentinel at the cost of a 4-card turn 3: plausible. $2 + $1 = $3, and the -1 Card token hits the next draw.
- Lighthouse is a good non-terminal $2 buy whose protection is irrelevant (no attacks): plausible. The kingdom has no Attacks.
- Harbor Village is weak, with little that gives +$ after it: plausible. Only Villa, Junk Dealer and Lighthouse trigger its bonus.
- Band of Misfits works as a $5 Villa without the on-gain, or a second Sentinel: plausible. BoM plays a cheaper non-Command, non-Duration Supply Action.

## Summary
The analysis gets Sentinel as the trasher and understands the Villa and Tools mechanics, but it rejects the Tools/Lost City/Caravan build that both finalists and the commentators converged on. It recommends thin money with one or two Lost Cities and dismisses Innovation outright. Its net-draw argument overlooks Tools-driven gaining, which is the core of the experts' plan.
