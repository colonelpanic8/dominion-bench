## Beyond the key
- Lurker trashing Silk Merchant from the Supply, then gaining it back from the trash, gives +1 Coffers +1 Villager each time: plausible. Silk Merchant triggers "when you gain or trash this" with no location limit. Calling each Lurker play "a cantrip" is wrong, though: Lurker gives +1 Action and no +Card.
- After Panic, buy several $0 Coppers each turn as next turn's money (each one is $1 and +2 Buys, then goes back): plausible. Copper costs $0, and Panic gives +2 Buys per Treasure played.
- Panic turns Gold, Potion, Figurine and Tools into one-shots: plausible. All are Treasures, so Panic returns them to their piles when discarded from play. The Potion point is a real cost for any Transmute plan.
- Tools copying itself keeps a Tools in the deck under Panic: plausible. It gains a new Tools while the played one returns.
- Money (Big Money + Council Room/Kitsune) is not viable once Panic fires: plausible. Every Silver and Gold played leaves the deck.
- With 2–3 Highways in play, Provinces cost $5–6 and Estates $0–1: plausible. Highway's reduction stacks per copy played.
- Shop → Council Room "saves a Villager": doubtful wording. Shop spends an Action to play Council Room free, so it saves an Action only next to playing both separately. What it actually adds is Shop's +1 Card +$1 for free.

## Summary
The analysis reads Panic, Kitsune's Curses and Highway's payload well. It even names the Transmute economy that Panic threatens. But it tests Transmute, finds it slow, and builds a Kitsune/Silk Merchant/Lurker engine, while the designer built the board around Transmute as the only self-trasher. A player following it would ignore the card the key centres on, and would miss the Transmute-vs-Panic decision on Coppers.
