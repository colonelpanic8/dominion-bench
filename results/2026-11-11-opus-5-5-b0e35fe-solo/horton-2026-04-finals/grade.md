## Beyond the key
- Governor's Gold mode followed by trash mode turns Gold ($6) into Province ($8) with no Buy: plausible. Trash mode gives "exactly $2 more" for the player who plays it.
- Throne Room on Governor nets +1 Action: plausible. Two Governor plays give +2 Actions for the one Action Throne Room used.
- Way of the Owl on a spare Governor draws to 6 without feeding the opponent: plausible. A Way play replaces Governor's text, so no player gets cards (it also drops Governor's +1 Action, which is why the analysis says to play it last).
- Tactician's discard keeps Governors from being used by Friendly that Clean-up: plausible. Tactician discards the whole hand before Clean-up, but this is no reason to skip Tactician.
- The opponent's Governor trash mode lets you turn Estate into Experiment and gain 2: plausible. $2→$3 is exactly $1 more, and Experiment's on-gain ability adds a second copy.
- Experiment played via Owl doesn't return to its pile: plausible. When a card is played as a Way, its own instructions are skipped.
- Merchant Camp always starts your turn in hand with +2 Actions: plausible. It may topdeck itself when discarded from play.
- Anvil reaches nothing better than Governor: doubtful. Anvil gains Throne Room and Flagship ($4), which matter more in the key's deck.
- A three-pile ending is unlikely because Experiments return to their pile: plausible.
- Opening 5/2 Governor/nothing: plausible but untested by the key. Horton names no opening.

## Summary
The analysis builds a credible Governor/Friendly engine and gets the Governor-flow idea. It dismisses two of Horton's three designed ideas: multiple Tacticians via Throne Room and Way of the Owl, and multiplied Leprechaun for Wishes. Like both finalists, it falls into the key's one trap. A player following it would play the Governor-centered deck Horton saw in the final, not the Tactician deck he designed the board around.
