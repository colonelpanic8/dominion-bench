## Beyond the key
- Panic starts with 5 Suns in 2 players, removed by both Omens (Kitsune, Poet): plausible. Rising Sun's Prophecy setup gives 5 Suns at 2 players, and both cards say +1 Sun.
- Lurker trashing Silk Merchant from the Supply pays +1 Coffers +1 Villager, and gaining it back pays again: plausible. Silk Merchant triggers on "gain or trash this," and Lurker does both.
- A Transmute that trashes Silk Merchant gets the Duchy plus the Silk Merchant tokens: plausible. It is an Action card being trashed.
- Two Highways make every non-Potion card costing $5 or less hit Poet's "$3 or less," and Transmute is excluded: plausible. Highway reduces $ only, and a Potion cost never counts as "$3 or less."
- Prepare stays $3 under Highways: plausible. Highway says "cards cost $1 less," and Events are not cards.
- A prepared Kitsune taking +2 Actions leaves three Actions, and a prepared Council Room draws without using an Action: plausible. Prepare plays them at the start of the turn, before the Action phase, without spending Actions.
- Tools can gain a copy of a played Reckless Transmute: plausible. Transmute stays in play until Clean-up, and Tools gains "a copy of a card anyone has in play."
- Under Panic, Golds from Transmute are one-use bursts, and Potion returns after its first play: plausible. Panic returns played Treasures when they are discarded from play.
- Five Highways make Provinces $3 and Duchies free: plausible arithmetic from Highway's text, though reaching five Highways in a game is optimistic.
- Pile-out example (Curses gone, Lurker takes the last Silk Merchant, Transmute takes the last two Duchies, a 3-pile ending before the Buy phase): plausible. Each step follows card text. Lurker's +1 Action lets Transmute be played too.
- Hand-probability figures for Kitsune (28–33% for one copy, 49–57% for two) in a 15–18-card deck: plausible. They match hypergeometric arithmetic.
- Opening $4/$3 as Silk Merchant/Shop with Shop playing Silk Merchant for $5: plausible. Shop's +$1 plus four Coppers makes $5.

## Summary
The analysis gets the mechanics right: Transmute is the only trasher against Kitsune's Curses, Reckless makes it resolve twice, Lurker/Transmute shapes the endgame, and Highway is a pile threat. But it builds a Kitsune–Highway engine and treats Transmute as a side line. It never frames the Panic-versus-Transmuting-Coppers decision that the designer called central. A follower would play roughly a sensible game but miss the deck-control phase Horton designed the board around.
