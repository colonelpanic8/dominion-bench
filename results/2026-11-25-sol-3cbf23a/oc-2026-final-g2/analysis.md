# Dominion kingdom analysis

## Board

**Build a Livery engine with Rope, Artificer, and Cursed Village. Add Mastermind once it has good targets.** Livery turns gains into draw for the next shuffle, and the Horses make Wayfarer cost $3. That lets you build while taking Colonnade points. The main race is against a player who claims those points early and starts buying Provinces while your draw is still coming together. [Kingdom and card text](kingdoms/oc-2026-final-g2.md)

| Cost | Kingdom piles and landscapes |
|---|---|
| $3 | Chariot Race |
| $4 | Abundance, Rope |
| $5 | Artificer, Corsair, Cursed Village, Livery, Mastermind, Road Network (Project) |
| $6 | Captain, Wayfarer (variable cost) |
| — | Colonnade (Landmark, 12 VP at setup) |

This is a two-player Province game with seven Coppers and three Estates per player. The opening splits are $4/$3 five-sixths of the time and $5/$2 one-sixth. The setup adds a 30-card non-Supply Horse pile and the Hex deck and associated States. The board uses Durations, Command, variable costs, and VP tokens.

Provinces, Duchies, and Estates score 6, 3, and 1 VP. Chariot Race scores 1 VP per win, and Colonnade pays 2 VP per qualifying gain, up to six payouts total. Both let you score while gaining cards you want to play. Cursed Village’s Hexes can add Curses or cost 2–4 VP through Misery. Corsair is the only Attack and targets Silver and Gold. Rope is the only dependable way to trash your own cards. [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf) · [Nocturne rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Nocturne.pdf) · [Seaside rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Seaside2nd.pdf)

## Engine

**Start with one Rope, one Livery, one Artificer, and one Cursed Village.** Rope supplies a Buy and trashes one card on the turn after you play it. Livery supplies $3 and one Horse per qualifying gain. Horses are non-terminal draw, Wayfarer is terminal draw, and Cursed Village provides one net Action and refills a hand depleted by Artificer. Mastermind gives you three plays of a single target at the start of the turn without spending your normal Action. A second Cursed Village or a second Mastermind makes the deck more reliable. Get the Village if terminals are colliding and the Mastermind if you already have targets you regularly draw together.

A favorable **$4/$3 build** looks like this.

| Turn | Worked line |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Open Rope and Silver. |
| 3–4 | Rope and four Coppers buy Livery. On turn 4, Rope draws and trashes an Estate, and Silver plus three Coppers buys Artificer. |
| 5–6 | A $7 Livery hand with Rope buys Cursed Village and gains a Horse. On turn 6, Rope trashes another Estate, and Artificer plus five Coppers buys Mastermind. |
| 7 | Cursed Village gives you slots for Livery and Mastermind. The Horse helps find Rope and Silver. Livery, Artificer, Rope, Silver, and three Coppers make $10 before discards. Discard the three Coppers to topdeck Chariot Race, then spend the remaining $7 on Abundance and a $3 Wayfarer. The Horse from gaining Abundance sets that price. Chariot Race is now a guaranteed Mastermind target for turn 8. |

I’d expect the first full draw around **turns 8–10** and the first strong scoring turn around **turns 9–11**. A Masterminded Wayfarer and one Horse draw 11 cards between them. On a turn when Mastermind instead triples Livery, you need Horses and well-timed Cursed Village refills to reach the payload. A single Mastermind, a turn with few qualifying gains, or several terminals drawn before a Village can each cost you a full turn. Keep enough draw to survive those gaps and the first green cards.

Two ordinary Liveries, Artificer, Rope, and five Coppers make **$13 and two Buys**, enough for Province plus Duchy. Mastermind on Livery with that same Artificer, Rope, and Copper gives **$16 and two Buys**, enough for two Provinces and six fresh Horses. Both turns work through Corsair. An Abundance triggered by an Action-phase gain adds another $3 and Buy. Those totals are achievable payload, with the $16 turn depending on lining up Mastermind and Livery. ## Key interactions

- **Livery and Wayfarer.** Gain a $4+ card with Livery active, gain the Horse, then use a spare Buy for a $3 Wayfarer. A Rope and Wayfarer can therefore cost $7 together and come with a Horse. Wayfarer can also set its own price to $3 by gaining Silver. With Artificer and enough Buys, gaining Copper for zero discards can set up a $0 Wayfarer. Recheck the price after any intervening gain. [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf) · [Adventures rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Dominion-Adventures-Rules.pdf)
- **Artificer and Cursed Village.** From a six-card hand after Artificer’s draw, keep Cursed Village and discard the other five to topdeck a $5 piece. Play the Village to draw back to six, starting with the card you just gained if it stayed on top. With Livery active, that gain also produces a Horse. On a bigger draw turn, Artificer can discard eight cards to gain a Province without spending a Buy. Work out what money survives the discard before committing. [Adventures rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Dominion-Adventures-Rules.pdf) · [Nocturne rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Nocturne.pdf)
- **Mastermind and starting-hand order.** Triple Wayfarer draws nine cards. Triple Horse draws six and gives three Actions, even though the Horse returns on the first play. Triple Livery gives $9 and three Horses per qualifying gain. Resolve Rope first if its draw can find the target. When Mastermind triples another Mastermind, the following turn can triple three different Actions if you have the targets in hand. [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf)
- **Abundance and Colonnade.** With Chariot Race or a $3 Wayfarer in play, buying its duplicate for $3 can collect 2 VP and trigger a waiting Abundance, restoring the $3 and the Buy. An Artificer Action gain can instead trigger Abundance before you shop. Artificer’s gain happens in the Action phase, while Colonnade checks Buy-phase gains. A Mastermind, Corsair, or Captain still in play from last turn can qualify its own duplicate. [Plunder rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf) · [Empires rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/DomEmpires2021.pdf)

## Other cards and the ratings check

- **Chariot Race** gets better with Artificer and Mastermind. A first Race reveals the opponent’s top card and leaves it there. Artificer can then put a card of known higher cost on your deck for another Race. Mastermind compares three draws against the same opposing card, for up to $3 and 3 VP. Ties lose, and a Wayfarer in either deck uses its current cost. [Empires rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Dominion-Empires-Rules.pdf) · [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf)
- **Captain** normally borrows Chariot Race here. If an earlier gain makes Wayfarer cost $4 or less, Captain can borrow that for three cards. At the start of a turn, an earlier Mastermind target can set the price first, for example by playing Wayfarer and taking Silver. Captain’s borrowed card stays in the Supply, so Colonnade sees Captain in play. An emptied Chariot Race pile makes Captain much less dependable. [Captain rulings](https://www.riograndegames.com/games/dominion-captain-promo/) · [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf)
- **Corsair** is worth one copy if the opponent is building around Silver and Gold. They still get the money on the turn you trash a Treasure, so the payoff comes on later shuffles. Stagger two copies if you need continuous pressure. Replaying Corsair on the same turn adds money and next-turn draw, with the attack still hitting the first qualifying Treasure. [Seaside rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Seaside2nd.pdf)
- **Road Network** is a late consideration against a player already greening. An opposing Province plus Duchy gives you two extra cards before your next turn, and an Artificer gain of a Victory card also triggers it. The $5 competes with too many build pieces early. [Renaissance rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Dominion-Renaissance-Rules.pdf)

The community ratings put Captain at **+3.8** and Rope at **+3.6**. Rope lives up to that rating here because it handles both thinning and +Buy. Captain has few dependable targets. I’d raise Livery (**+1.3**), Artificer (**+1.7**), and Abundance (**−0.5**) because this board gives them cheap follow-up gains, immediate draw, and a way to turn extra Buys into points. Corsair (**−2.8**) is a matchup buy with real upside against money. [Card ratings](context/card-ratings.md)

## Plans considered

| Plan | Speed and requirements | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Livery engine** | First $6 around T3–6 and first spendable $8 around T6–8. Start with one of each core piece and add a second Village or multiplier as needed. A Corsair is optional. | The $5 buys are crowded. A missed shuffle can delay the Village or strand Mastermind without a target. |
| **Chariot Race and Colonnade tempo** | Open Rope/Race or Abundance/Race on $4/$3. Get a playable Race and at least two early duplicates, then add Artificer or Mastermind. Expect the first $6 around T5–7 and a Province-capable $8 around T7–9. Aim for four of the six Colonnade payouts, plus Race wins. | The other player can claim the same tokens while building a better deck. After Colonnade empties you need Race wins, Provinces, or a winning pile-out. |
| **Big Money + Livery** | Rope/Silver into Livery on $4/$3, Livery/pass on $5/$2. One Livery and one Rope are enough to start. Livery plus three Coppers reaches $6 for Gold as early as T3 on $5/$2. A Horse can help reach $8 around T5–7 on that split or T6–8 on $4/$3. A Corsair pivot trades some draw tempo for attacks roughly once per shuffle with one copy. | Corsair can erode the Treasure base. This deck also has a harder time claiming Colonnade and sustaining multi-gain turns. |

**Against their best plan.** In the engine mirror, take useful Colonnade duplicates early, especially Livery, Artificer, and discounted Wayfarer. If the opponent goes for Race and Colonnade, keep building while buying the cheap duplicates that fit your deck. Then use Province plus Duchy or double Province to get ahead before the third pile goes. Against the Race runner-up, I’d play that same Livery engine and contest the cheapest Colonnade payouts. Against straight money, I’d keep enough Copper to hit prices through Corsair and start taking Provinces as soon as I can continue gaining draw alongside them.

## Opening and buy priorities

- **$4/$3 — Rope and Silver.** The first $5 matters more than an early Race. Enumerating the second shuffle gives Rope/Silver about an **82%** chance to present at least $5 on turn 3 or 4 before opposing effects. Rope/Race reaches about **77% even if its first Race wins**, and about **36% if it loses**.
- **$5/$2 — Livery and pass.** Livery plus two Coppers buys a $5 and gains a Horse. Even the $4 Livery hand can buy Rope and gain a Horse. Artificer is the alternative if you want a guaranteed choice of topdecked piece when it appears and can afford to spend a hand gaining it.
- **Second shuffle.** Get your first Livery or Rope, then Artificer or Cursed Village according to whether you need gains or terminal slots. Trash Estates first and keep enough Copper to pay for your build. If the opponent opens Corsair, prioritize Livery and lean on Rope, Abundance, and Artificer for economy before adding more Silver.
- **Third shuffle.** Add Mastermind once you have multiple targets worth tripling. Add a discounted Wayfarer, a second Rope or one Abundance for Buys, and a second Village when you regularly draw terminals together. Take Colonnade points on pieces you already want. Start greening when you can buy a Province and keep the deck drawing, often around T8–10. In a fast mirror, take an earlier Province if waiting risks losing control of the ending.

## Ending and what could change the pick

I’d expect a **Province ending against money** and a meaningful **three-pile threat in the mirror**. Chariot Race and Wayfarer are the first cheap piles to count. Duchy, Estate, Cursed Village, and Artificer are plausible third piles depending on what both players buy. Count Artificer gains and extra Buys before leaving an opponent a winning turn. Colonnade’s depletion matters for scoring, and Horses sit outside the Supply. If Provinces split 4–4, the token margin and the Estates you trashed can decide it.

The strongest challenge is an early **Chariot Race and Abundance** opening that takes four Colonnade payouts before your engine scores. Two waiting Abundances and one Race in play can turn **$6 and one Buy into three Race purchases for 6 VP**. Artificer and Mastermind give that deck ways to keep scoring afterward. I still prefer Livery because it can contest those payouts with good gains of its own and has the better Province turn. The recommendation loses when the opponent gets the token lead, your Village or multiplier misses a shuffle, and they can end on piles before your first big turn. If both cheap piles are already low and the opponent leads on tokens, bring forward Duchies and Provinces.

With more players, I’d secure the first Village and Livery sooner. Key piles are more contested, Corsair hits more opponents, and Road Network becomes more attractive once several players start greening. Your individual share of Colonnade is harder to control.

**Least sure of:** how reliably one early Livery feeds enough Horses to start the engine by turn 9. The test is your second reshuffle. If you repeatedly miss Livery or gain only one Horse per cycle, add draw or a second Livery before another multiplier. If you’re already drawing through the deck and have two Buys, start scoring.

**Assumptions:** Standard two-player rules and the card text in the kingdom file. The early price odds come from enumerating opening shuffles. The build and scoring windows are estimates from worked hands.
