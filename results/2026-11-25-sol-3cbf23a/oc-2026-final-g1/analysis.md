## Recommendation

Build a **Litter engine with Council Room or Patrol**, use **Cage** to park starting Estates, and turn the first Victory gain into Gold with **Market Square**. Open **Silver + Astrolabe on 4/3** and **Jester + Cage on 5/2**. Aim for two Litters, enough draw to play most of your Treasures, and one Jester if you can support it. Provinces are the primary scoring plan. Tunnels help you score on smaller turns, and Vineyard is a strong pivot once you can reach 12 Actions and draw a Potion repeatedly.

## Board

This is a two-player Province game with seven starting Coppers, three Estates, and opening splits of 4/3 or 5/2 in either order. The $5 slot is crowded, and Potion is the only card at $4. Cage can take Estates and Curses out of circulation for several shuffles. Litter is the sole source of spare Actions, so access to it determines how easily you can combine draw, Jester, and Way of the Squirrel.

| Cost | Piles and landscapes |
|---|---|
| $2 | Border Guard, Cage |
| $3 | Astrolabe, Market Square, Tunnel |
| $4 | Potion, a Basic Treasure added by Vineyard |
| $5 | Council Room, Jester, Litter, Patrol, and the Event Seaway |
| Potion | Vineyard |
| — | Way of the Squirrel, plus the Horn and Lantern artifacts from Border Guard |

Scoring comes from Provinces, Duchies, Estates, Tunnels, and Vineyards. Curses subtract a point each. Every scoring gain adds a card, and Cage is the only card that trashes. Its trashing removes the Cage itself. Litter incurs 1 Debt per play. Council Room, Astrolabe, Market Square, and Seaway supply extra Buys. Jester can gain a copy of an opponent’s discarded card without spending a Buy, or give them junk. A Victory discard gives them a Curse.

## Engine and worked line

**Two Litters are the core.** Each draws two cards and adds one net Action, so two Litters give you three terminal slots. Council Room gives you four immediate cards and a Buy, at the cost of giving your opponent a card. Patrol gives three immediate cards, pulls green cards and Curses out of its next four, and lets you order the rest for a subsequent draw or Border Guard. Take Council Room when you need raw draw and Buys. Patrol gains value as you green, especially when you can follow it with another draw card. Add a third Litter if you regularly have more terminals than slots or need a better chance of finding a Litter in your opener.

A plausible **4/3 build** goes like this. The prices are what the turn needs to produce after paying Debt.

| Turn | Gain and purpose |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Silver and Astrolabe for early $5s and extra Buys |
| 3–4 | Council Room at $5, then Cage + Border Guard at $4 with two Buys |
| 5–6 | Litter + a second Border Guard on a $7 turn, Gold on a $6 turn. Park two Estates if Cage meets the draw. |
| 7–8 | Jester + Market Square, then second Litter + another Market Square, each on a turn with $8 and two Buys |
| 9 | Buy a Province if the race calls for it. Keep one or both Market Squares in hand if Cage is waiting to turn them into Gold. |

That’s a favorable sequence. An early miss at $5 or a stranded Litter can push it back a shuffle. Around **turns 8–11**, a seven-card Squirrel opener, two Litters, and Council Room see 15 cards before sifting. Two Border Guards can inspect four more positions. That can cover most or all of the circulating deck while Cage is holding Estates. Once those Estates return and you add green cards, a second strong draw card matters. A hand that misses Litter can still take a one-terminal draw turn or use a cheap Action as Squirrel to set up the next hand.

A full payload turn with seven Coppers, Silver, two Golds, Astrolabe, and Jester produces **$16 after two Litter Debt**, with **five Buys** if Council Room and both Market Squares are played. Missing a Gold or declining to play Jester brings that to $13–14. **Province + Duchy at $13** is the more useful benchmark. A third Gold makes double Province possible even with a couple of Coppers missed or sifted away.

## Key interactions

- **Cage + Market Square.** “The next time you gain a Victory card, trash this” lets you park Estates early and use your first Province, Tunnel, or other Victory gain to trigger the trash. Each Market Square **still in hand** can react for one Gold. Two Squares mean two Golds from the same Cage. A fresh Cage can set aside zero cards and still trigger, so a later $2 Cage can pay off if you expect another green turn with a Square in hand. The parked cards return after the normal five-card cleanup draw. [Plunder rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf)
- **Border Guard + Tunnel.** Discarding a Tunnel from the top of your deck gives you a Gold and leaves the Tunnel’s 2 VP intact. Lantern lets you inspect three and discard two, potentially triggering two Tunnels in one play. With two Tunnels among 18 circulating cards and two Border Guards in hand, four otherwise random inspected positions contain about half a Tunnel on average. Lantern raises that to about three-quarters before accounting for sequencing. One or two Tunnels can pay their way. Loading up early makes the deck depend on finding Tunnel in the draw pile instead of in hand. An opposing Jester that hits Tunnel gives you the Gold opportunity as well as the Curse. [Renaissance rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Dominion-Renaissance-Rules.pdf), [Cornucopia rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/CornucopiaGuilds.pdf)
- **Horn + Way of the Squirrel.** With a spare Action, use Border Guard as Squirrel, then use Horn at cleanup to put that Guard into your next hand. You start with seven cards including the same Guard and can repeat the setup. This gives up Border Guard’s immediate sift and uses a terminal slot. It’s especially good on a turn where you’ve already hit the price you need. Lantern is the better artifact when immediate selection and Tunnel discards are worth more. [Renaissance rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Dominion-Renaissance-Rules.pdf), [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf)
- **Jester + Litter.** Jester makes $2 while copying a useful opposing card or handing over junk. A copied Litter, draw card, or Gold can save an entire purchase. Against an untouched opening deck with two non-Victory buys, its first random hit is roughly 25% Estate, 58% Copper, and 17% one of those buys. Caged Estates reduce the early Curse rate. Once opponents green or accumulate Curses, the attack gets better at draining the Curse pile. Use Squirrel on Jester when the next hand is worth more than the attack and $2. [Cornucopia rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/CornucopiaGuilds.pdf)
- **Vineyard + cheap Actions.** Border Guards and Market Squares develop the deck and count toward every Vineyard. At 12 Actions a Vineyard scores 4 VP, and at 15 it scores 5. With three Vineyards and 11 Actions, the next $2 Border Guard is effectively a 3 VP buy. One Potion can buy one Vineyard each time you draw it and can be paired with a coin purchase if you have another Buy. Pay off Litter Debt before buying.

**Seaway** can put its token on Border Guard or Market Square, and the token also pays when you use that card as Squirrel. A $5 turn is usually better spent on Litter or draw because the deck already has several cheaper sources of +Buy. **Astrolabe** earns its first slot by smoothing the early $5s and making $4 and $7 turns productive. Additional copies compete with Silver for immediate spending power. The repository ratings understate Litter here because it fills both the draw and village roles, and understate Cage because it has both an early thinning job and a later Gold payoff. [Adventures rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Adventures.pdf), [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf), [Card ratings](/tmp/claude-1000/-home-imalison-projects-dominion-arena/f8629424-2e36-48be-8522-e621f2b4ebe7/scratchpad/sol-bench/sb/oc-2026-final-g1/context/card-ratings.md)

## Plans and matchups

| Plan | Rough line and main weakness |
|---|---|
| **Litter engine with Cage and Market Square** | Look for $6 around turns 4–6 and $8 around turns 6–8. The worked build starts threatening $13–16 around turns 9–12. Secure two Litters, a strong draw card, and enough economy to keep buying $5s. One Jester can attack on most successful draw turns from the midgame. Missing Litter or drawing through the first green shuffle can cost a turn. |
| **Patrol money** | Open Silver/Astrolabe on 4/3 or Patrol/Cage on 5/2. Add Gold and one or two Border Guards, with Council Room as the faster draw option if giving the opponent a card is affordable. Look for $6 around turns 4–6 and $8 around turns 6–8, then start buying Provinces. The core plan has zero attack plays. It gets on the scoreboard early and has trouble matching repeated Province-plus-Duchy turns. |
| **Cheap-Action Vineyard build** | Start with the same economy, then buy Border Guards and Market Squares in pairs where possible. One illustrative line gets Litter and two Guards by turn 4, adds a Square and another Guard on turn 5, buys Potion around turn 6–8, and reaches roughly 9–12 Actions around turn 9–11 with another draw card. Three Vineyards at 12 Actions yield 12 VP, with Tunnels adding points and occasional Gold. A dedicated version can skip Jester, or add one once Litter supports it. Potion can miss the shuffle, and an early Province lead can force you to score before the Action count pays off. |

The strongest response to the recommended engine is **another Litter build that contests the pile and greens first**. If the opponent gets the better Litter split, pivot toward one-terminal draw turns and cheap VP while you still have pile control. Against Patrol money, get the first Jester working when you can, then aim to catch up with multi-buy scoring. If you’re the money player facing the engine, take Provinces on your first good hands and pursue Tunnels or a three-pile finish while you’re ahead. Against a Vineyard-heavy opponent, track their score at the **next** Action threshold as well as their current one.

## Opening and buy priorities

- **4/3 — Silver + Astrolabe.** Before interference, this hits $5 on turn 3 or 4 about **81%** of the time. Double Silver reaches about **91%** and is defensible if the first $5 is paramount. Astrolabe pays back the difference through build turns such as $4 for Cage plus Border Guard or $7 for Litter plus Border Guard.
- **5/2 — Jester + Cage.** Jester gives immediate economy and a chance to junk or copy an opener. Cage lets you park the Estates Jester is looking for in the mirror. Council Room + Cage is the draw-first alternative if you value a faster build and can afford the card you give away. Litter gets stronger once there’s a terminal worth playing beside it.
- **First two or three shuffles.** Secure a strong terminal and the first Litter, then prioritize the second Litter or more draw according to what stalled your last turn. Buy one early Cage and one or two Guards on spare Buys. Add Market Squares ahead of the intended Cage release. Take a Gold when money is the bottleneck. Start greening around turns 8–10 on a sound engine line, earlier if Provinces are moving quickly. Check which Estates or Curses return from Cage before committing to the next shuffle.

## Ending and uncertainty

Expect a **Province ending** if either player builds dependable $13–16 turns. In mirrors, track **Border Guard, Market Square, and Tunnel** together. An empty Curse pile can replace any one of them, and Vineyard joins the count if both players buy Potion. A player ahead on cheap VP can finish by buying Actions even on a turn that falls short of Province money. Before buying the penultimate Province, count the opponent’s likely cheap gains and score as well as their chance of reaching $8.

The highest-ceiling alternative is the Vineyard build. Its best case combines **Horn and Squirrel for larger starting hands, Astrolabe for spare Buys, and one well-timed Potion**. I’d switch earlier if I could see a path to 12 Actions and several Potion plays while the Province pile was still healthy. The recommended build loses when its second Litter or first Gold misses a shuffle and the opponent converts that window into Provinces or a cheap three-pile lead. **The least certain judgment is how often the engine reaches a second big scoring turn after releasing Cage.** The first green shuffle tells you more than the first $16 hand.

The turn ranges are estimates from the supplied card text and worked purchase lines. With more players, Jester and Council Room affect more opponents, Litter is more contested, and I’d green sooner when cheap piles start dropping.
