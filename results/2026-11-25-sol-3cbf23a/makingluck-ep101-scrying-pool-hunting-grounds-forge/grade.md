## Beyond the key
- Forge trashing Hunting Grounds to regain one, plus the Duchy/3 Estates trigger and a bought Province, for a 9 VP turn. With an Estate added, Province + Duchy + Province is 14 VP net: plausible arithmetic from the card text.
- Forge ignores Potion in trashed costs and can't gain a Potion-cost card: plausible. It matches Forge's "exactly equal ... in $" wording and the standard ruling.
- A $0 Forge (only Coppers) still must gain Curse or Copper: plausible. The Curse pile is always in the Supply.
- Playing the last exposed Encampment without an enabler exposes Plunder for that turn's buy or gain: plausible. Encampment returns to the pile only at the start of Clean-up.
- A Brigand buy can't trigger Beggar: plausible. Beggar reacts to Attacks being played.
- Trader on Hunting Grounds gives six Silvers plus the Hunting Grounds trigger: plausible.
- Trader's exchanged Silvers for Beggar's Coppers go to the discard pile: plausible, per the exchange ruling.
- Probabilities: 82% to Pool on turn 3/4 after Potion/Silver, 71% to see one of 3 starters, 31% for a single Brigand. All check out.
- Mandarin 5/2 opening (an extra $5 turn from the topdecked Coppers): plausible by the rules. Whether it beats an earlier Potion is untested, and the analysis says so.
- Three Hunting Grounds trash triggers can drain the 8-card Estate pile for a three-pile ending: plausible.
- Opening Potion/Silver as the default: doubtful against the hosts' conclusion that Silver/Silver reaches the $6 Hunting Grounds and $7 Forge sooner.

## Summary
The analysis picks the Scrying Pool/Encampment deck, one of the two decks the hosts found competitive. Forge is central to it, and the Pool-into-Brigand Gold snipe is identified, so the plan broadly matches. It loses credit for opening Potion/Silver instead of Silver/Silver, for keeping the Coin of the Realm/Hunting Grounds deck only as a fallback, and for offering only Beggar, not the Trader Silver flood, as the counter to Pool/Brigand.
