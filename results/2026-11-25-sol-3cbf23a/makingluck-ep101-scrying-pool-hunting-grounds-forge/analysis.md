# Kingdom analysis

**Build around Scrying Pool, but buy enough conventional draw and money to make Forge work.** Open Potion and Silver on 4/3. On 5/2, use Mandarin’s on-gain effect to get an extra $5 turn and open with Mandarin, Potion, and Encampment in the first three turns. Aim to buy Provinces as soon as you can sustain $8, then use Forge and Hunting Grounds to score alongside the buy. ## Board

Two players start with seven Coppers and three Estates. The opening splits are 4/3 and 5/2, in either order. There are no landscapes, Colonies, Platinum, Shelters, or non-Supply piles. Potion is a $4 Basic Treasure, and Coin of the Realm uses the Tavern mat.

| Cost | Kingdom pile |
| --- | --- |
| $2 | Beggar, Coin of the Realm, Encampment |
| $2 + Potion | Scrying Pool |
| $4 | Noble Brigand, Trader |
| $5 | Mandarin, Scholar, Plunder once exposed |
| $6 | Hunting Grounds |
| $7 | Forge |

Encampment and Plunder share one pile, with five copies of each. Silver is the only $3 purchase. The important ordinary prices are $5 for draw or economy, $6 for Gold versus Hunting Grounds, and $7 for Forge. Mandarin and Noble Brigand are first-edition-only cards.

Scrying Pool supplies nonterminal draw and attacks the opponent’s next draw. Hunting Grounds supplies unconditional terminal draw, and Scholar can reset a bad hand. Encampment supplies a spare Action even on the turn it returns, while a banked Coin supplies two Actions after you play an Action. Forge is the only mass trasher. Trader removes one card at a time and turns an Estate into two Silvers, which helps early buying power but makes later Pools weaker.

There’s one Buy per turn. Provinces, Duchies, and Estates score from the deck, and Plunder scores 1 VP token per play while producing $2. Noble Brigand can steal Silver or Gold and can junk with Copper. No card hands out Curses, although the Curse pile is available to a $0 Forge gain. The likely finish is Provinces, with Forge making Estates, Duchies, and Hunting Grounds relevant to a three-pile ending.

## Engine

Start with **one Potion, two Scrying Pools, one Encampment, one Gold or Plunder, and one Hunting Grounds**. Add a third Pool or another Encampment if the deck stalls, and get one Forge online. A banked Coin is especially useful when Hunting Grounds is the first Action in hand or when you need Hunting Grounds and Forge on the same turn. One retained Encampment can support both terminals. Keep enough draw to leave a Hunting Grounds in hand for Forge. A copy you played for cards is already in play.

A worked 4/3 line, assuming favorable shuffles and no interference:

| Turns | What the deck can do |
| --- | --- |
| 1–4 | Open Potion/Silver. On turn 3, Potion, Silver, and two Coppers buy the first Pool. On turn 4, $3 buys Encampment. |
| 5–6 | On turn 5, Pool can pick up Encampment and a Copper, bringing a three-Copper-and-Silver hand to $6 for Gold. Leave Encampment in hand for the next shuffle. On turn 6, two Coppers and Potion buy Pool number two. |
| 7–9 | On turn 7, Encampment can draw into the Pools while Gold keeps it in the deck. Gold, Silver, and three Coppers make $8 for Forge. Turn 8 can buy Coin. On turn 9, Encampment and the Pools can find Forge and all three Estates. Forge the Estates into Hunting Grounds and use Gold, Silver, and a Copper to buy a second Hunting Grounds or another Gold. |
| 10–12 | The first full-deck turn is plausible around turn 10–11 on this line. Allow until around turn 12 if the key gains miss a shuffle. The next priority is proving that the deck can draw, leave a Hunting Grounds unplayed, and still make $8. |

Early Pool alone is unreliable. In a shuffled 16-card deck with two Pools and one Encampment, a five-card hand contains at least one of those three about 71% of the time. That measures access to a starter. Forge thinning, Hunting Grounds, and a spare village are what let the deck keep drawing after that first play. A built turn can make **$8–$10 and one Buy** with something like two Golds and a Silver, or two Golds and two Plunders. The latter also scores 2 VP per turn. Once you reliably reach $8, buy additional payload only if it adds VP, replaces vulnerable money, or keeps Encampment in the deck.

## Key interactions

- **Forge + Hunting Grounds.** Hunting Grounds says, “When you trash this, gain a Duchy or 3 Estates.” Forge can trash a $6 Hunting Grounds, gain another copy from the Supply, and take 3 VP from the trigger. With $8 left for a Province, that’s a **9 VP turn** before any Plunder tokens. It can repeat while Hunting Grounds and scoring cards remain and the deck can absorb the new green.
- **Forge + Hunting Grounds + Estate.** $6 + $2 gains a Province, and the Hunting Grounds trigger gains a Duchy or three Estates. With $8 left for another Province, that’s **15 VP gained, 14 VP net** after losing the trashed Estate. Save this for when you can afford to lose the draw or when it ends the game. A spare Encampment or obsolete Scrying Pool can also supply the $2. Forge counts only the coin part of a cost and cannot gain a Potion-cost card. [Prosperity rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Prosperity2nd.pdf)
- **Forge as a builder.** Three Estates become Hunting Grounds or Gold, two Estates become Noble Brigand or Trader, and an obsolete Potion plus an Estate becomes Hunting Grounds or Gold. Add spare Coppers to any useful batch without changing the price. A batch of only Coppers still requires a $0 gain if available.
- **Encampment + Gold or Plunder.** Draw the enabler before resolving Encampment’s reveal when you can. If you play the last exposed Encampment without an enabler, Plunder is available to buy or gain that turn, before Encampment covers it again at Clean-up. The split pile counts as empty only when both halves are gone at the end of the turn. [Empires rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Dominion-Empires-Rules.pdf)
- **Scrying Pool + Noble Brigand.** Pool can leave a revealed Silver or Gold on top for Brigand to steal that turn, or clear a weak top card to give Brigand two fresh looks. A $4 Brigand buy can do this immediately. You get the +$1 only when you play Brigand. [Hinterlands first-edition rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/Freigabe-PDF_Dominion_Hinterlands_US_Rule.pdf)

## Other cards and rating check

| Card | Best use here |
| --- | --- |
| Beggar | A reasonable $2 hedge if the opponent commits to Pools. Discarding it for two Silvers accelerates money, although Pool can discard the topdecked Silver and a played Brigand can steal it. A Brigand **buy** cannot trigger Beggar. |
| Coin of the Realm | Its strongest pairing is Hunting Grounds into Forge. Bank it in advance so you can lead with Hunting Grounds and then call Coin for two Actions. One copy can bridge a poor Encampment split despite Coin’s −0.4 rating. |
| Mandarin | The first gain can repeat a $5 opening hand. Later plays put a spare Pool or village on top for next turn. Buying Mandarin with Plunders in play can line them up again for VP. Play only the Treasures you need if topdecking more would crowd out your Actions. |
| Scholar | A fallback draw card when the opening stalls or Encampments are scarce. Play it early with Actions to spare. Its hand discard can throw away the Forge targets, village, and payload you already found. |
| Trader | Trashing Hunting Grounds gives six Silvers **and** the 3 VP trigger. That’s a possible money or pile-out play. For the Pool deck, one early Estate conversion is usually enough. Trader can exchange Beggar’s gained Coppers for Silvers, but those Silvers go to the discard pile. [Hinterlands second-edition rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Hinterlands2nd.pdf) |
| Plunder | The best extra payload once $8 is stable. Each copy enables Encampment, earns VP without adding a Victory card, and survives Brigand’s trashing effect. Prefer Gold when its extra dollar is what gets you to $8. |

Pool’s **+3.0** and the Encampment/Plunder pile’s **+3.0** fit their roles here. Forge (**−0.8**) and Hunting Grounds (**+0.6**) are the biggest upgrades over their general ratings because together they supply scoring beyond the single Buy. Mandarin’s rough first-edition **−3.2** also understates its opening value on this particular 5/2.

## Plans considered

| Plan | Timing, requirements, and weakness |
| --- | --- |
| **Pool → Hunting Grounds → Forge** | First $6 around turns 5–7 and first $8 around turns 7–10. Wants two Pools, one durable Encampment or a Coin, one Forge, and preferably two Hunting Grounds. Both players want Encampments. A slow first shuffle or stolen Gold can delay the whole chain. |
| **Big Money + Hunting Grounds** | Open Silver/Silver on 4/3, or use the Mandarin opening with Silver in place of Potion on 5/2. Two Silvers and two Coppers can hit $6 on turn 3 or 4. On a later Hunting Grounds turn, two Silvers and four Coppers reach $8. First Province is plausible around turns 5–8, with four Provinces around turns 14–17 as an unattacked benchmark. One Brigand can punish exposed Gold and Silver, but a single copy in a 16-card deck is in only about 31% of five-card hands before draw. |
| **Encampment/Coin → Hunting Grounds → Forge, skipping Potion** | Start from the money opening, take Gold or an exposed Plunder to retain Encampment, then buy Hunting Grounds and Forge. A good $6 turn around turns 3–5 and $8 turn around turns 5–8 can put all the pieces in the deck early. It needs a village and ideally two Hunting Grounds. Scholar can bridge a bad shuffle, but as a terminal it competes for the Actions that the Forge turn needs. |

**Recommendation and matchup.** Take the Pool line. The best opposing response is to contest the Encampments, steal an enabling Gold with a well-timed Brigand, and start buying Provinces while you assemble Forge. Against that pressure, buy your first sustainable Province and use Coin to cover a lost village. Against straight money, you can afford one more draw gain if it makes the Forge turn repeatable. If you’re the money player facing a working Forge deck, buy Provinces early and use Duchies or a timely pile-out to make your lead hold. A 4–4 Province split can easily favor the Forge player.

## Open and buy priorities

- **4/3 or 3/4 — Potion and Silver.** With an undisturbed first shuffle, this buys a Pool on turn 3 or 4 about 82% of the time. Take the first Pool at $2 + Potion, then find an Encampment and an enabler. Take the second Pool at the next good opportunity.
- **5/2 or 2/5 — Mandarin, Potion, and Encampment across turns 1–3.** Buy Mandarin on the $5 hand. Its gain puts the five played Coppers back on top, so the following turn is also $5. Buy Potion on that turn and Encampment on the $2 turn. If $5 comes first, the sequence is Mandarin, Potion, Encampment. If $2 comes first, it’s Encampment, Mandarin, Potion. This delays the first Potion shuffle by a turn compared with Potion/Encampment and gets Mandarin into the same shuffle for economy. [Mandarin on-gain ruling](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/Freigabe-PDF_Dominion_Hinterlands_US_Rule.pdf)
- **Next two shuffles.** Take Gold at the first $6 if keeping Encampment is the bottleneck. Take Hunting Grounds if you already have an enabler or need unconditional draw more. Take Forge on a productive $7 when you can reach it with trash targets and preserve enough money afterward. Use a spare $2–$4 buy on Coin if terminals are colliding, or on Brigand if you have a strong immediate target. Forge away Potion once you have enough Pools.

## Ending and what could change the pick

Track **Provinces, Estates, Duchies, and Hunting Grounds** closely. Three Estates at a time can drain the eight-card Estate pile in three triggers. If one Hunting Grounds and three Estates remain and you’re ahead, trashing a Hunting Grounds can gain the last Hunting Grounds and empty Estates in the same Action. Your Buy can then empty a third pile. Before taking the penultimate Province, count the opponent’s potential Forge gain as well as their Buy.

The strongest challenge to the Pool line is the **no-Potion Forge deck**. Gold, Encampment, and Hunting Grounds give it a real shortcut, especially with a fast Mandarin opening. It can win by landing its first Forge conversion while your Pools are still drawing one card at a time. If you’re near turn 7 with one Pool, no retained village, and steady $6 hands, lean into Hunting Grounds, Coin, and money. With more players, the five Encampments get scarcer, more attacks can hit you, and pile endings are harder to control. Value Coin sooner and green earlier.

**Least sure of.** The close call is whether Mandarin’s extra opening purchase repays the later first Pool on 5/2. I favor Mandarin for the early $6–$7 hands. The comparison that would settle it is which opening more often draws both Forge and an unplayed Hunting Grounds with $8 still available by turns 10–11. The turn ranges above are worked estimates using the supplied card text and ordinary two-player setup.
