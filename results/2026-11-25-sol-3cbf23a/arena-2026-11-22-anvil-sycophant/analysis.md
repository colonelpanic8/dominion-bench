# Kingdom analysis

**Open Temple–Anvil on $4/$3 and Temple–Sycophant on $5/$2.** Build a thin Blessed Village–Mapmaker deck, then add one Expand. The repeatable scoring turn is **Province plus Duchy for $8 and a Sycophant**. Anvil replaces the Sycophant, and the gain and trash together earn 4 Favors toward the next Mountain Folk draw. When the game is about to end, Expand can turn a Gold into a second Province instead.

## Board and setup

| Cost | Kingdom piles and landscapes |
|---|---|
| $2 | Sycophant, Avoid (Event) |
| $3 | Scheme, Fool, Anvil |
| $4 | Mapmaker, Temple, Blessed Village, Crucible, Diplomat |
| $7 | Expand |
| — | Way of the Horse, Mountain Folk (Ally) |

Two players, eight Provinces, no Colonies, Platinum, or Shelters. Each player starts with six Coppers, Lucky Coin, three Estates, and one Favor. Lucky Coin still makes $1, so the opening splits are $4/$3 and $5/$2 in either order. Play it on the opening turn it appears to put a Silver into the first shuffle. Fool brings Lost in the Woods and the Boon deck, which can supply Will-o’-Wisps from outside the Supply. Way of the Horse returns the Action you played to its own pile.

Five Kingdom piles cost $4, so an early Anvil can build almost the entire deck while your buys go toward Gold and Expand. Temple supplies thinning and VP tokens, and Provinces and Duchies do most of the scoring. There are no Attacks. Mapmaker makes the opponent’s greening an opportunity to improve your next hand. The Forest’s Gift is the only source of an extra card buy. Avoid spends a Buy and gives it back.

## Engine and timing

**Start with one Temple, one Anvil, two Blessed Villages, two Mapmakers, and a Scheme.** Temple takes an Estate and a Copper together, and can take a Sycophant as the third name. Keep enough Copper to feed Anvil while Lucky Coin builds your Silver supply. Two Blessed Villages give three terminal slots, enough for two Mapmakers and Temple during the build, then two Mapmakers and Expand when scoring. Scheme lets you start the next turn with the village or draw card you were missing.

A favorable build shows how the pieces arrive without relying on a particular Boon:

| Turn | Line |
|---|---|
| 3 | With Temple, Anvil, two Coppers, and an Estate, trash one Copper and the Estate. Discard the other Copper to Anvil for Blessed Village. |
| 4 | Lucky Coin, the opening Silver, and three Coppers make $6. Buy Mapmaker and take the Silver from Lucky Coin. |
| 5–6 | Use Village–Temple to thin again. A turn with Mapmaker, Anvil, Lucky Coin, and two Silvers can find two Coppers, feed one to Anvil for the second Village, and spend $7 on Gold or Expand. Take Gold here if it is your first. |
| 7–9 | Use Anvil to add the second Mapmaker. Buy Scheme on a $3 turn and Expand once you can support it. A Favor draw or a spare Action played as a Horse can bridge the first turn that needs to find everything. |

I’d expect the first near-full-deck turns around **turns 8–10**, with repeatable scoring around **turns 9–12** if Anvil connects early. Two Mapmakers can put four selected cards into hand, but they can also pass over a piece you need. When you need almost every remaining card, an expendable Action played as a Horse gives straight draw and preserves your Action. Scheme a Blessed Village when terminal collisions are the problem, or a Mapmaker when you already have Actions or expect the opponent to green.

## Key interactions

- **Anvil, Expand, and Sycophant.** Expand turns a $2 Sycophant into a Duchy and earns 2 Favors. Anvil discards a Treasure to gain a replacement and earns another 2. If your remaining Treasures make $8, that is **9 VP from a Province and Duchy, plus 4 Favors**, with one Buy. Five completed loops fund four Mountain Folk activations. Each activation adds three cards at the start of a turn. Before Expand arrives, gaining and trashing one Sycophant with Temple or Crucible gets you from the starting 1 Favor to 5.
- **Expand turns Gold into a finishing burst.** Two Golds, two Silvers, and Anvil represent $11. Expand one Gold into a Province and the remaining Treasures make exactly $8 for the second. With an extra Copper to feed Anvil, you can also gain an Estate for **13 VP**. Preserve Gold while the Duchy line is scoring and use the conversion to win the Province split or end the game.
- **Anvil and Mapmaker give you draw during Treasure play.** Gain an Estate with Anvil while holding Mapmaker. The Estate triggers Mapmaker before your first purchase, so you can still play the Treasures you find. Expand can trigger Mapmaker earlier by gaining a Victory card in the Action phase. An opponent’s Victory gain gives you the same selection for your next hand and can start a chain if the first Mapmaker finds another. Check their hand before giving them an Estate trigger. [Rio Grande Games’ Plunder rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf)
- **Anvil can collect the Temple bank immediately.** Play Temple for 1 VP and put another on the pile, then use Anvil to gain Temple and collect the bank before your opponent can take it. A bank of 2–3 VP can justify the gain even after thinning. The extra Temple can become Horse draw or fuel for Crucible or Expand. Returning a Temple with the Way can also reopen an empty pile. [Rio Grande Games’ Empires rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Dominion-Empires-Rules.pdf), [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf)

## Other cards and landscapes

- **Crucible** keeps the opening economy moving. An Estate is $2, and a spare $4 card gained by Anvil is $4 on a later turn. Crucible can trash Sycophant for $2 and 2 Favors without using an Action. Play Lucky Coin for Silvers early. Once the influx starts to dilute your draw, Crucible can cash the Coin out for $4.
- **Diplomat** is useful as a third draw card, especially when it can follow Temple or Expand with four or fewer cards in hand. Played straight from five cards with two available to draw, it leaves six in hand and gives no Actions. Favor draw makes its village condition harder to meet at the start of a turn. Its Attack reaction has no target here.
- **Fool** is a cheap Anvil gain when you have a spare terminal slot. Three Boons and Lost in the Woods can pay off early, especially while you have Estates to discard. If the opponent leaves you holding the State, you can turn Fool into a Horse on its next appearance and keep the State. Blessed Village is the more dependable way to tap Boons. Save a revealed Forest’s Gift for a scoring turn unless you can use the extra Buy immediately. [Rio Grande Games’ Nocturne rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/DomNocturne.pdf)
- **Avoid** is good when you have $2 left over and know you will shuffle in Clean-up. Keep up to three green cards out of that shuffle to protect the next turn. Leave enough cards to draw a full hand. [Rio Grande Games’ Plunder rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf)
- **Will-o’-Wisp** is a welcome Boon gain while Copper and Sycophant are common. It can also draw Estates, so its value as extra draw falls as the cheap cards left in the deck become mostly green.

The ratings check points to the right question here. Blessed Village and Way of the Horse already rate well. Temple (−1.6), Anvil (−1.2), and Mountain Folk (−3.7) do more on this board than their averages suggest. Temple has a clean exit once it finishes thinning, Anvil reaches every Kingdom pile except Expand, and the scoring line replenishes Favors while gaining points.

## Plans considered

| Plan | Opening and thresholds | What it needs and how it loses |
|---|---|---|
| **Thin Expand engine** | Temple–Anvil or Temple–Sycophant. First $6 around T4–6 and first $8 around T6–9. | Aim for two Villages, two Mapmakers, and one Expand. An early trashing turn can leave you short of $4, and late draw leaves Gold and Expand in different hands. |
| **Crucible–Anvil tempo** | Crucible–Anvil or Crucible–Sycophant. First $6 around T3–5 and first $8 around T5–7. | Trash Estates for money, use Anvil to gain Mapmaker and Temple, and start buying Provinces sooner. One Crucible and one Anvil suffice. It can pivot into Expand, but a larger deck makes the scoring loop harder to repeat. |
| **Big Money with Mapmaker** | Mapmaker–Silver on $4/$3, or Crucible–Sycophant on $5/$2 and buy Mapmaker at the first $4. First $6 around T3–5 and probable first $8 around T5–7. | One or two Mapmakers, Lucky Coin Silvers, and early Gold. A strong opening shuffle can hit $8 even earlier. One Buy usually means one Province, even on the turns Mapmaker finds plenty of money. |

**Against their best plan.** In a mirror, contest Blessed Village and Mapmaker early and watch who can collect the Temple bank. Against a faster Crucible opening, judge the race by Provinces remaining. The engine can catch a one-Province lead with two Province–Duchy turns or one double Province turn. If your opponent has three Provinces while your Expand still misses its targets, take the points you can reach. Against the Crucible plan, a clean engine has the stronger late turn, so the tempo player should press their lead and threaten a three-pile finish when they can.

## Opening, buys, and ending

- **$4/$3 → Temple and Anvil.** Temple can remove two different starters on its first play. Anvil can gain a $4 piece even on a turn that leaves little money to spend.
- **$5/$2 → Temple and Sycophant.** There is no $5 Kingdom card. Gain and trash the Sycophant for the first Mountain Folk activation, or use it as Horse draw if that saves a turn. Crucible is the faster alternative at $5 if you intend to race.
- **First two shuffles after the opening.** Get the first Mapmaker and Blessed Village, then add the second Village as terminal collisions appear. Take Scheme on a spare $3. Start trimming Copper once Silver can carry your buys, and keep a cheap Treasure available for Anvil. Get one Gold and one Expand before adding more economy. A second Anvil is worth considering once you regularly have two Treasures to discard.
- **Green when you can follow up.** A repeatable $8 plus Expand is enough to start. Green sooner if your opponent is already taking Provinces or threatens to empty piles. Watch **Temple, Sycophant, and Blessed Village or Mapmaker** in an engine mirror. Estates and Duchies can also fall quickly once Anvil and Expand start gaining points. Count the opponent’s potential buys and gains separately before leaving two Provinces or a three-pile finish. Track Temple tokens when judging a 4–4 split.
- **With more players**, Village and Mapmaker get more contested, and green gains give Mapmaker more opportunities to react. Expect less control over the ending. Take an earlier scoring turn when multiple opponents are draining the same piles.

**What could flip the pick.** Crucible–Anvil is the closest alternative. A hand with those two cards, an Estate, Copper, and Lucky Coin can trash the Estate, discard the Copper to gain Temple, and still spend $4 on Mapmaker while gaining a Silver. That is a full turn of development and the strongest reason to open Crucible. I favor Temple because removing a Copper and an Estate together makes the weak draw work earlier. If Crucible buys the same draw pieces while reaching Gold a shuffle sooner, its Province lead can force the Temple deck to green before Expand pays off.

**Least sure of.** The timing of the first reliable Expand turn. The turn ranges are estimates from the worked lines, with normal reshuffles and variable Boons. By turn 8, check whether you can find a village and two draw effects while retaining either $8 after feeding Anvil or $11 before converting a Gold. If Provinces are already falling and both lines are still a shuffle away, favor immediate points over another round of engine building.
