# Kingdom analysis

**Recommendation.** Choose **4/3 and open Exploration → Steward**. Build around **Hill Fort, Destrier, and Ducat**, using Garrison to bridge the early draw gap. Aim for two Hill Forts if the split allows it. Once you have enough gaining, use Tent to expose Strongholds when you have a Cabin Boy ready to convert. Your first big scoring turn will usually be **Province + Duchy**. A second Gold or banked Coffers can turn that into two Provinces.

## Board and setup

| $2 | Ducat, Ratcatcher |
| $3 | Tent, Steward, Reckless Guide, Wishing Well, Harbinger |
| $4 | Garrison, Cabin Boy, Exploration |
| $5 | Hill Fort, Mystic |
| $6 | Stronghold, Destrier |
| Trait | Reckless on Guide |

Forts is one 16-card Supply pile, with four each of Tent, Garrison, Hill Fort, and Stronghold in that order. You can gain only the exposed card. The two-player tournament setup uses seven Coppers and three Estates, arranged for a 4/3 or 5/2 opening. It uses ordinary Provinces and Estates, with zero added piles. Reserve calls, Durations, Coffers, and Villagers matter here. This is an attack-free Province race with **eight extra VP in Strongholds**. Duchies and Estates also score, and Curses carry the usual penalty. All scoring comes from cards. Steward removes two starting cards per trashing play, Ratcatcher handles the awkward last Estate, and Ducat can replace an unplayed Copper with a Treasure that banks $1 and adds a Buy. The strategic question is how quickly you can secure Hill Forts and enough draw to use those Buys.

## Engine and key interactions

- **Hill Fort + Exploration.** One Hill Fort can gain a Silver, Ducat, or Reckless Guide and replace itself. If you skip card gains in the Buy phase, Exploration still gives **one Coffer and one Villager**. Spend the Villager to play Garrison *before* Hill Fort on a subsequent turn. Hill Fort and two purchases then give that Garrison **three cards next turn**. Garrison only counts gains after it is played. [Allies rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Dominion-Allies-Rules.pdf), [Renaissance rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Dominion-Renaissance-Rules.pdf)
- **Hill Fort + Ducat + Destrier.** Gain one card with Hill Fort, then buy a $2 card and Destrier for $4. That is **three gains from $6 and two Buys**. With two gains already made in the Action phase, a later Hill Fort can gain Destrier directly and even put it into hand if you have an Action left. A Cabin Boy conversion at the start of the turn can supply one of those earlier gains. When you gain Ducat in the Action phase, you can trash a Copper then and put the Ducat into hand for this turn’s Buy phase.
- **Reckless Guide + terminal Actions.** Playing Guide gives **+2 Cards and +2 Actions** for one Action spent, then parks it on the Tavern mat. That lets you play Steward and Tent together early, or Garrison before Hill Fort later. Its call gives one redraw. The Guide returns to its pile at clean-up after the call, so continued use takes fresh gains. When a starting hand needs a reset, call Guide before taking Garrison’s or Stronghold’s extra cards. [Plunder rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf), [Adventures rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Adventures.pdf)
- **Tent + Cabin Boy + Stronghold.** Topdecking Tent lets you play it again next turn and choose when to rotate the exposed block. Cabin Boy can become a Garrison early or a **2 VP Stronghold** later, provided the target is exposed at the start of your turn. A Tent played that turn comes too late for that conversion. Stronghold supplies either **+$3 now** or **+3 cards next turn**, so you can green while adding playable payload. [Allies rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Dominion-Allies-Rules.pdf), [Plunder rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf)

**Worked build.** This is a favorable shuffle line with the necessary Forts still exposed. It shows what the opening can convert into.

| Turn | Play and gains |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Buy Exploration for $4, collect its opening Coffer and Villager, then buy Steward for $3. |
| 3–4 | Steward trashes two Estates. Two Coppers plus the opening Coffer buy Tent. A four-Copper hand buys Cabin Boy. |
| 5–6 | Play Cabin Boy and Tent, rotate to Garrison, and spend the Villager to trash two Coppers with Steward. Tent’s $2 buys Ducat while you hold a third Copper to trash on gain. Topdeck Tent. Next turn, convert Cabin Boy into Garrison **before** playing Tent to expose Hill Fort. Tent and three Coppers buy Hill Fort. |
| 7 | Hill Fort gains a Ducat and takes the cantrip mode. Play Garrison, then use three Coppers and the first Ducat’s Coffer to reach $6. Buy Ratcatcher for $2 and Destrier for $4. Garrison draws two next turn from those purchases. |
| 8–9 | On the seven-card start, Destrier and Hill Fort can find three Coppers, both Ducats, and Tent while Hill Fort gains Silver. That gives $7 for Silver and a second Destrier at $4. On turn 9, Tent, both Silvers, one Copper, and a Ducat reach $8 if the Destriers connect. |

Aim to see most of the deck around **turns 8–10**. One Hill Fort is enough to pursue the line, and two speed up the build. Aim for **two to three Destriers, one or two Ducats, and a source of opening-hand draw**. A lone Garrison stays out of your shuffle on its draw turn, so a second Garrison or a draw-mode Stronghold helps cover the gaps. Even with three Destriers among 16 randomly ordered available cards, a five-card start misses all three about 29% of the time. An eight-card start cuts that to 10%, before counting other ways to reach draw.

A built turn with **Garrison, Mystic, Gold, two Silvers, and two Ducats** reaches **$13 and three Buys**, including the Ducats’ Coffers. Add a second Gold to reach **$16**. That puts Province–Duchy in reach around turns 9–11 on a good build, with double Province more plausibly around turns 10–12. Keep enough draw to absorb the green cards.

## Other cards and ratings

- **Harbinger** can recover a newly gained card or set up a guaranteed Mystic hit. Mystic then gives +$2 and replaces itself. This is most valuable when you have enough draw to see both regularly.
- **Wishing Well** improves with a thin, tracked deck and can reveal a card for Mystic to name after a miss. It draws once before naming, so a single Harbinger topdeck is consumed by the mandatory draw.
- **Ratcatcher** complements Steward when there is only one junk card you want to trash. Call it after a Guide redraw or Duration draw if you want to choose from the larger hand.

The local ratings support Steward (+3.0) and Destrier (+2.6) as priorities. Hill Fort (+0.8), Exploration (−1.4), and ordinary Guide (−1.8) are stronger here because of the gain timing, the limited terminal slots, and Reckless. Mystic (−4.0) earns a place when you need non-terminal money and can hit its guess often. ## Plans and openings

| Plan | Race and matchup |
|---|---|
| **Hill Fort engine** | First $6 around turns 5–7, first $8 around turns 8–10. The strongest reply is a mirror that takes two or three Hill Forts and controls the next rotation. Secure your first Hill Fort before adding luxury draw, and take a second when you can. |
| **Mystic–Destrier without Hill Fort** | Open Mystic–Ducat. A Mystic and four Coppers hit $6 on turn 3, and a good Mystic–Ducat hand can fund Ratcatcher plus discounted Destrier for $7. Add Steward, two to three Destriers, and Gold. Look for $8 around turns 6–8. This is the best fallback when Fort access closes. It can green first, but the Hill Fort deck can gain draw and money while buying points. |
| **Big Money + Garrison** | Open Mystic–Ducat, add one Garrison if exposed, then prioritize Silver and Gold. Expect $6 around turns 3–5 and $8 around turns 5–7 on a favorable Gold shuffle. Garrison with two gains gives a seven-card start, but adding weak cards for the draw slows the deck later. The Fort engine’s best reply is to keep building through the first opposing Province and overtake with multi-green turns. |

- **4/3:** Exploration → Steward. Spend the first Coffer on a useful threshold after trashing. Buy Tent if you need to open Forts yourself. If the opponent already has that covered, put the buy toward Cabin Boy, Ducat, or the exposed Fort you need.
- **5/2:** Exploration → Steward also works by spending the opening Coffer to raise $2 to $3. That spends a Coffer the 4/3 keeps, so choose 4/3 for the engine. For the faster Mystic line, open Mystic → Ducat. Both Coppers must be played to buy the opening Ducat at $2, leaving zero Copper in hand to trash.

**Buy priorities and ending.** In the first shuffle, trash Estates and establish access to Forts. In the next, take Hill Fort, your first Ducat, and discounted Destriers. Add Silver as a gain and Gold once draw can carry it. Green when you can repeat $8 or make a $13 turn, and earlier in a close mirror if the next Province or Stronghold changes the endgame. Provinces are the likely finish. Watch **Ducat, Destrier, and Duchy** as three-pile candidates, with Cabin Boy or Forts joining the count if heavily contested. Rotating past a block leaves those cards in the Forts pile. At a 4–4 Province split, one Stronghold is a two-point swing, so count Estates and Duchies before exposing the final Province.

**Challenge and uncertainty.** The Mystic–Destrier line can beat the recommendation if it buys draw on turn 3, starts taking Provinces by turn 6 or 7, and the Fort player misses the first Hill Fort window. A Stronghold pivot can also swing a mirror, especially if the opponent has Cabin Boys in play when you rotate. Its four copies supply eight points total, so I would use that pivot to complement Province scoring. The closest call is opening Exploration versus opening Ducat or Guide for immediate tempo. If Exploration’s early Coffer and Villager fail to turn a trashing hand into a gain or an extra terminal play, the faster opening has the edge. With more players, the same four Hill Forts and four Strongholds are harder to count on, making the Mystic–Destrier fallback more attractive.

**Assumptions.** The tournament rule arranges only the starting hands. Later shuffles are random, and turn estimates come from worked hands. I used the file’s wording for Exploration, which checks for gains during the Buy phase. The publisher’s rulebook describes the check in terms of purchases. The lines above work under either wording.
