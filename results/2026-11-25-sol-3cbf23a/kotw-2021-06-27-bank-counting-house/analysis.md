# Bank, Counting House, Travelling Fair, and Tower

## Board

Build around **Counting House + Travelling Fair**, then add **Bank** to turn the Copper stockpile into multiple Provinces. The crucial move is to buy and topdeck a fresh Counting House while leaving enough cards on your draw pile to get through Clean-up without shuffling. **Enchantress** can blank the next Counting House, **Jester** can force the shuffle you were avoiding, and **Tower** can make the Copper split worth more than a Province split.

Two players, seven Coppers and three Estates each, with 4/3 or 5/2 openings in either order. Settlers and Bustling Village share one pile, with five of each and Settlers on top. The setup uses the first-edition-only Counting House, Farming Village, Horse Traders, and Talisman. Enchantress and Haunted Woods are Durations. There are no Colonies, Platinum, Shelters, or extra piles. The crowded price is $5, where the combo piece competes with both major attacks and, once uncovered, Bustling Village. Gold is the $6 stepping stone to Bank. [Rio Grande Games’ Empires rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Dominion_Empire_rule.pdf)

## Key interactions

- **Counting House + Travelling Fair.** At $7, a Fair and a fresh House leave one more Buy for a Copper. Topdeck the House, then topdeck only as many other gains as you need to fill the next hand. The Coppers you played go to the discard at Clean-up, where the new House can pick them up. Each additional Fair costs $2 and adds one net Buy. From an empty draw pile, four Fairs, a House, and four free Coppers cost $13 and give exactly five cards to draw. That line consumes a House from the Supply every turn and leaves no buffer against Jester. [Rio Grande Games’ Adventures rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Dominion-Adventures-Rules.pdf)
- **Talisman + Fair.** One Talisman turns each Copper buy into two gains. Two Fairs, a House, and two Copper buys then make the five-card stack for $9, provided Talisman is already in play. An extra Talisman, Silver, or Bank can be worth topdecking to improve the next hand. Spare Coppers can go straight to the discard. [Rio Grande Games’ Prosperity rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/Dominion-Prosperity-Rules.pdf)
- **Counting House + Bank.** Play the Bank last. Eight Coppers and a Bank make $17, enough for two Provinces with Horse Traders’ extra Buy if you can keep those Treasures. Nine Coppers and a Bank make $19, enough for Fair and two Provinces. Seven Coppers and two Banks make $24 before any other payload. Before spending the whole turn on green, count the cards you can leave for the next hand.
- **Horse Traders + a village.** Farming Village leaves two Actions, enough for Horse Traders and Counting House. Horse Traders can discard Coppers before House retrieves them, supplying $3 and a Buy without losing those Coppers. Its Reaction also brings Horse Traders back and draws one card at the start of your turn. That extra draw helps find the pieces when you have a buffer and can force a shuffle when you have none.
- **Attacks against the loop.** Use Settlers or Raze to absorb Enchantress before playing House. Budget a card for the replacement draw. Jester can force an immediate shuffle if you left zero cards after drawing your hand, so leave one spare Copper per likely Jester, plus enough cards for your own start-of-turn draw. Haunted Woods can recycle junk into your next hand, but you can sometimes use its forced topdeck to preserve the Copper discard. Buying Fair itself leaves your hand alone. ## How fast it can go

This is a favorable, legal **5/2** line without opposing attacks. It shows why the combo is worth pursuing even before you have Bank. Turn numbers are your own.

| Turn | Money | Purchases and next hand |
|---|---:|---|
| 1–2 | $5 / $2 | Buy House and use Fair to topdeck two Coppers. |
| 3 | $5 | Draw those two Coppers and three more. Buy Fair, topdeck Silver, and put the gained Copper in the discard. |
| 4 | $10 | Draw House, Silver, two Coppers, and an Estate. House retrieves six Coppers. Buy two Fairs, House, and two Coppers. Topdeck the three gains and draw the two remaining Coppers ahead of two Estates. |
| 5 | $12 | House accesses all twelve Coppers. Buy three Fairs, House, and three Coppers. Topdeck the four gains, drawing one of the Estates and leaving the other. |
| 6 | $15 | Buy three Fairs, House, Talisman, and two Coppers. Topdeck all four gains to go with the last Estate. |
| 7 | $18 | Buy three Fairs, House, Bank, and two Coppers. Talisman makes those two Copper buys four gains. Draw House, Bank, and three Coppers, leaving the fourth Copper on the deck. |
| 8 | $41 | Play twenty Coppers and Bank. Three Fairs and four Provinces cost $38. If four Provinces leave you exposed, take fewer and buy enough useful cards to set up another House turn. |

That line uses **five Houses by turn 7**, including the opener. In an attacked or contested game, I’d budget for a first useful Fair–House turn around **turns 5–8** and a first multi-Province turn around **turns 8–12**. The worked line is an upside case. With only two or three Houses available, use shorter bursts and expect to rebuild after a shuffle.

The strongest conventional build uses **Farming Village** to see past green cards, **Settlers** as a cantrip that can also recover one Copper, and **Bustling Village** to support several terminals and recover a discarded Settlers. **Raze** is best at removing Estates early or Curses later. It can trash itself for a two-card look when its job is done. **Jester** is worth more here for forcing a shuffle or copying an opposing Bank or House than for its early Curse rate. Take a revealed Copper yourself if the opponent is stockpiling them. The repository ratings put Counting House, Bank, and Talisman below average. Fair gives House repeat access to the discard, House gives Bank unusually large Treasure counts, and Tower gives Talisman’s extra gains a scoring route. Those are the reasons to buy them here.

## Recommendation and openings

- **4/3 or 3/4 — Horse Traders + Silver.** On an early $5, take House and use the spare Buy for Copper when you have Horse Traders. At $6, Gold is a good bridge to $7 if your draws cannot yet maintain a Fair chain. Fair plus Talisman is stronger when you can topdeck Talisman into a useful next hand.
- **5/2 or 2/5 — Counting House + Fair for two topdecked Coppers.** When the $2 comes first, those Coppers replace two of the five still in your draw pile, so the second turn still makes $5. With either order, turn 3 starts with two known Coppers and draws three more cards from the first shuffle. An early House is strongest after a hand of Coppers has gone through Clean-up.

On the **first shuffle**, secure a House and enough economy to reach $7. On the **second**, buy Fair before the gains you want to topdeck, track the exact draw-pile count, and prefer Talisman when it saves a Fair or supplies the buffer. Add the first Bank when a forthcoming House hand can find it. Start greening as soon as you can take two Provinces and keep a credible next turn, or earlier if the opponent is in range of ending. With Fair alone, Province–Duchy costs $15 and double Province costs $18.

A strong opponent can **mirror the combo, contest Houses, and add one timely attack**. In that matchup I’d take the first large scoring turn sooner and buy the last available House when it denies their continuation. Against the Duration engine, pressure Provinces before its draw lines up. If you’re the Duration player, Enchantress or Jester needs to buy time for your next scoring turn. Merely building a larger deck gives the combo time to reload.

## Ending, risks, and uncertainty

Provinces, Duchies, and Estates score the usual 6/3/1. Tower adds a point per owned non-Victory card from an empty Supply pile, with no additional card gain at scoring. Curses are worth −1 each until their pile empties and Tower offsets the penalty. Emptying the five Settlers only exposes Bustling Village. Watch **Counting House, Raze, Enchantress, Copper, and the full split pile**. With two piles down, count the opponent’s Fair Buys and Talisman copies before leaving a third pile in reach. The Copper pile starts at 46 in this setup. If it empties, a 24–17 Copper split is worth seven points, enough to overturn a one-Province lead. [Rio Grande Games’ Empires scoring rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Dominion-Empires-Rules.pdf)

I rechecked the Duration engine with staggered Woods, Talisman-assisted village gains, and Horse Traders for payload and +Buy. It can win if an early attack breaks the first Fair chain and the House buyer spends several turns recovering. That is also how the recommended plan loses to money. It holds unscored Copper, gets its House blanked or its discard shuffled, and watches the opponent take the last Provinces. At three or more players, expect fewer Houses per player, more attacks between turns, and faster pile depletion. Plan for shorter bursts and count Tower earlier.

**Least sure** is how often the 4/3 opening can establish a second consecutive House turn through early disruption. At the first $7, count next-hand cards, any forced draws, likely Jester plays, and the Houses left in Supply. If that count points to an immediate reset, take a shorter Bank burst or pivot to Haunted Woods money. The timing ranges are estimates from the supplied card text and worked hands.
