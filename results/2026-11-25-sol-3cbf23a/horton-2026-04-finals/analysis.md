# Horton 2026-04 Finals kingdom analysis

**Choose 5/2. Open Governor and pass at $2.** Use Friendly to get a second Governor early, then add Forager, Merchant Camp, and a replay. The deck wins by drawing enough to gain Gold, turn Gold into Provinces, and buy Victory cards on the same turn. In the mirror, the first player to do that twice has a substantial advantage.

## Board

| Cost | Kingdom piles |
|---|---|
| $3 | Anvil, Experiment, Forager, Leprechaun, Merchant Camp |
| $4 | Advisor, Flagship, Throne Room |
| $5 | Governor with **Friendly**, Tactician |

This is a two-player Province game with seven Coppers and three Estates per player. The tournament rule lets you arrange those cards for 5/2 or 4/3 in either order. The $2 opening buy is a pass. At $4 you have three useful engine pieces, and at $5 Tactician competes directly with Governor. [Tournament setup and card text](kingdoms/horton-2026-04-finals.md)

**Way of the Owl** is terminal draw available on every Action. Merchant Camp supplies spare Actions and can put itself into the next hand. Replayed Governors, Experiments, and Foragers can also leave extra Actions, and Tactician supplies one on the following turn. Forager is the repeatable thinner and the immediate source of +Buy. Governor upgrades starting Estates into $4 pieces and $3 cards into more Governors or Duchies.

Provinces, Duchies, and Estates are the scoring cards. Leprechaun can introduce Curses and the Miserable states, which can cost VP without adding a card. There are no Attack cards or dedicated junkers. Advisor lets the opponent discard your best reveal, and every Governor mode gives the opponent something. Leprechaun’s Hexes affect its owner. Wish is outside the Supply.

## Engine and opening

| Split | Opening | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **5/2** | **Governor / pass** | You enter the first shuffle with access to all three Governor modes and can gain a second copy without spending a Buy. When the first Governor appears, compare holding it for a guaranteed Friendly gain with playing it to draw into a $5 buy. |
| **4/3** | **Flagship / Experiment** | Flagship plus three Coppers reaches $5. The Experiment pair helps find that hand, and a Flagship left pending can turn the next Experiment into +4 Cards and +2 Actions. Flagship/Forager is the slower opening if you value permanent thinning over the first $5. |

**First two shuffles.** Get two Governors, then aim for three or four. Hold a spare for Friendly while the pile is contested. Take one Forager early and trash a Copper when the immediate +$1 preserves a key price. Upgrade Estates to Throne Room or Flagship with Governor. Get a Merchant Camp before adding several terminals, and topdeck it whenever the next hand needs an extra Action. A second Camp is useful when you regularly want both Flagship and Throne Room in the same turn.

**Third shuffle onward.** With three Governors, a replay, and a Camp, one clean draw turn can build the next one. Buy an Experiment pair when you need to bridge a shuffle or reach newly gained Gold. Prefer another replay or Advisor when you need draw that persists. Add a second Forager when extra Buys are useful and you still have expendable cards. Three Governors is a practical target in the mirror. With two, you need more support from replays, Advisor, or recurring Experiment gains.

**Worked fast line.** This is one favorable shuffle sequence from the 5/2 opening, with no help from the opponent’s Governor modes.

| Turn | Build |
|---|---|
| **3** | Governor draws to five Coppers. Buy Governor. |
| **4** | Governor draws to five Coppers again and finds the other Governor. Buy Forager and hold the spare Governor for Friendly. You now own three. |
| **5** | One Governor draws into Forager and a second Governor. Forager trashes Copper, giving $1 and +Buy. The second Governor turns an Estate into Throne Room. Two Coppers plus the Forager money buy Merchant Camp. |
| **6** | Two Governor draws find Camp, Forager, Throne Room, and the third Governor. Camp gives $1 and the spare Action. Forager trashes an Estate for $1 and +Buy. Throne Room plays the third Governor for Gold and then draws the last three cards, including the remaining Coppers. Six Coppers plus the two Action dollars buy Governor and Experiment. |
| **7** | A topdecked Camp, a doubled Governor draw, and the Experiment pair get through the deck. One Governor gains a second Gold before the final Experiment triggers a shuffle and draws it. Another Governor turns one Gold into a Province. Six Coppers, the other Gold, Camp, and Forager give $11 and two Buys. Buy a second Province and a $3 card if the score and piles favor it. |

I’d plan on drawing most or all of the deck around **turns 7–9** and first scoring twice around **turns 8–10**. The worked line gets there early because the first two Governor draws both hit $5 and the first replay connects immediately. The reliable version can start from a topdecked Camp plus draw, has a backup draw card or Experiment, and keeps some draw unused until after gaining Gold. A built turn can reach **$16 and two Buys after one Gold upgrade** with three other Golds, a Silver, two Coppers, Camp, and a $2 Forager. That can take three Provinces. Two Provinces, or a Province plus a Duchy, are more useful planning targets.

## Key interactions

- **Friendly + Governor.** At the start of Clean-up, a Governor still in hand can gain one more Governor. A $5 buy or a Silver upgrade plus Friendly takes two copies in one turn. The copy you play is already in play, so keep one in hand when denial matters. Friendly gains during Clean-up can also empty the final pile. [Plunder rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf)
- **Governor + replay.** Two Governor plays can draw six cards, gain two Golds, or mix modes. Gain Gold before the draw that will reshuffle it, then turn it into a Province with a later Governor. Watch the reciprocal upgrades. Your opponent can turn an Estate into a $3 engine piece, a $4 card into Governor or Duchy, or trash a Copper outright with the $1 slot empty. A three-Province turn that invokes several upgrades can also hand them points. [Governor card notes](https://www.riograndegames.com/games/dominion-governor-promo/)
- **Merchant Camp + Flagship + Throne Room.** Camp gives enough Actions to play Flagship and then Throne Room. Flagship pays $2 and replays the Throne Room, which can pick a different target on its second resolution. Doubling Governor and Forager can mean two Governor modes, two trashes, and two extra Buys. When Flagship carries over, a Camp that takes the replay gives +4 Actions and $2 and still topdecks as a single card. [Plunder rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf), [Allies rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Dominion-Allies-Rules.pdf)
- **Experiment + replays or Anvil.** One gain usually takes two Experiments. Throne Room or Flagship gets +4 Cards and +2 Actions from one Experiment, which returns once. Anvil can turn a spare Copper into an Experiment pair and leave the Buy available. Every Experiment played as Owl stays out of the Supply, so you can use Owl to keep the pile empty when going for the finish. [Renaissance rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Dominion-Renaissance-Rules.pdf), [Menagerie rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf)
- **Tactician + replay + Owl.** On the first play, discard your hand for Tactician. On the second, use Owl to draw back to six. You keep the money and Buys already generated by Actions, can still play the Treasures you redraw, and get +5 Cards, +1 Action, and +1 Buy next turn. Two Flagship triggers allow **Tactician → Owl → Tactician**, which can set up +10 Cards, +2 Actions, and +2 Buys next turn. With a fourth play, Owl can refill again. This is the strongest reason to buy a Tactician once the replay deck is working. [Menagerie rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf), [Seaside rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Seaside2nd.pdf)

### Other cards and modes

- **Advisor** adds permanent nonterminal draw. Governor can gain it from an Estate, and it is best when several revealed cards would keep the turn going. If there’s only one key card among the three, expect the opponent to discard it.
- **Anvil** is a midgame builder when your Governors are scarce and you still have spare Coppers. It can gain Throne Room, Flagship, Advisor, or the Experiment pair. Count the discarded Treasure against the turn’s purchasing power.
- **Leprechaun** is an optional payload card once seven is easy to count. For example, play Camp, Forager, three physical Governors, and then Throne Room on Leprechaun. Leprechaun is the seventh card, and both plays see seven, gaining two Golds and two Wishes. Count carried Durations and exclude returned Experiments. Leave draw after Leprechaun if you want to use the Wishes this turn. Wish can gain a Governor, Gold, or Duchy directly to hand. Use Owl on Leprechaun when the count is wrong and the Hex could spoil the turn. [Nocturne rulebook](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Nocturne.pdf)
- **Forager** can pay $2 on every play once Copper and Silver are in the shared trash. A trashed Anvil adds a third name. Use Owl when you need the cards more than the money and want to keep everything left in your hand.

The [local ratings](context/card-ratings.md) put Governor at +5.5, Forager and Throne Room at +2.7, and Merchant Camp and Tactician at −2.4 and −1.9. The low-rated pair gains the most here. Camp guarantees access to spare Actions on the next turn, and Owl lets a replayed Tactician set up a big turn while still buying this turn. Friendly’s −0.1 average also understates its value on a pile both players want repeatedly.

A worked line gains Gold and buys Silver on turn 3, buys Silver on turn 4, then draws Gold, Silver, and three Coppers for $8 on turn 5. Expect $6 around turns 4–5 and $8 around turns 5–7. | **Tactician replay engine** | Use the same Governor opening for the first $6 and $8. Add one Tactician once a replay and a spare Action are available, then aim for a powered turn around turns 8–10. On the worked turn 6, buy Tactician and Experiment instead of the fourth Governor. No candidate has an attack to sustain. **I’d play the Governor replay engine and let the draw dictate how far to build.** Its key pieces improve ordinary turns, and a single replay can supply the extra draw or upgrade that makes a scoring turn. Tactician earns its slot when the deck already has the pieces to replay it. With only two Governors and poor access to replays, favor Advisor or Experiment for draw and take good scoring hands as they arrive.

## Ending, risks, and what would change the pick

- **Start greening** once you can regularly combine a Gold upgrade with a Province buy, or reach $13 with two Buys and a useful Duchy. Against an opponent already greening, take a clean double Province when it gives you a meaningful lead. Recheck the remaining Gold after every conversion.
- **Count the three-pile finish every turn once Governor runs low.** Governor, Experiment, and Duchy are the main candidates. A contested Throne Room or Merchant Camp pile can take Duchy’s place. Count your opponent’s gains as well as their Buys, including Friendly in Clean-up and two Experiments per gain. When ahead, use Owl to hold Experiments out for the ending. When behind, returning them can reopen the pile.
- **Check both players’ Governor options before the last Province.** The opponent can turn a $4 into a Duchy on your upgrade play. On their own turn, $8 plus a Gold in hand and a Governor can take the last two Provinces with one Buy. In a close game, count Estates removed and the fewer-turns tiebreak alongside the Province split.
- **Reassess after the split.** A 4/3 opponent who misses $5 gives you time to secure extra Governors. An opponent who gets four or five quickly makes contesting the last copies and greening earlier more valuable. With more players, Governors are tighter and each mode helps more opponents. Cheap replays and earlier points gain value.

**Challenged pick.** The Tactician build has the highest draw ceiling. It can spend on the setup turn by playing Forager and coin-producing Actions first, then using Owl on the replay. A second pending Flagship can make the next turn even larger. I still prefer a Governor-first build because those same replay slots can score immediately and deny copies during the opening. The recommended deck loses when it gives away tempo in the mirror by playing every Governor it sees, feeding the opponent useful upgrades, and adding support after it could already score.

**Least sure of.** The timing of the first Tactician buy is the closest call. With enough Governors already secured, the test is whether the next shuffle is likely to bring Tactician, a replay, and enough Actions together while still producing a buy on the setup turn. If it does, Tactician can be the best next $5.

**Assumptions.** The tournament rule fixes the starting order only. Later shuffles are random. The worked line assumes favorable draws and no incoming Governor effects, and the turn ranges are estimates from the build sequence.
