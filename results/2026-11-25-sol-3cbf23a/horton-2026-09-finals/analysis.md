# Horton 2026-09 finals kingdom analysis

**Recommendation.** Choose **4/3 and open River Shrine / Silver**. Build a Hostelry–Artificer engine with Witch’s Hut and Weaver, then turn the gains into **Triumph** points. Take an Alchemist through Change to make the engine more reliable, and keep Stonemason available to turn an Alchemist into two Vineyards or a Gold into two Duchies. I’d aim for a first substantial scoring turn around **turns 8–10**, with the Estate and Hostelry counts determining how much longer to build. ## Board and setup

Two players start with seven Coppers and three Estates and can choose either opening split. The extra Supply pile is **Potion** at $4. **Horse** costs $3 and is outside the Supply. Footpad’s Action-phase gain draw applies from turn one, and **Flourishing Trade** starts with five Suns. River Shrine is the only Omen. The fifth Shrine play activates the Prophecy immediately for both players. [Cornucopia & Guilds rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/CornucopiaGuilds.pdf), [Rising Sun rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/DomRisingSunRules.pdf)

| Opening cost | Kingdom piles |
|---|---|
| $0 + Potion | Vineyard |
| $2 | Stonemason |
| $3 + Potion | Alchemist |
| $4 | River Shrine, Hostelry, Change, Weaver |
| $5 | Witch’s Hut, Footpad, Artificer |

The other landscapes are **Exploration** at $4 and **Triumph** at 5 Debt. The opening $3 buys Silver among the coin-only cards. At $4 you can thin, gain, or add a village, while $5 is contested by draw, attack, and Artificer. Flourishing Trade drops each card’s coin cost by $1, including cards being gained or trashed. Exploration and Triumph keep their prices. Extra Buys come from **unused Action plays**, so every extra terminal you play can cost you a scoring purchase. [Rising Sun rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/DomRisingSunRules.pdf)

Provinces, Duchies, Estates, Vineyards and Triumph all score, while Curses subtract points. Triumph adds VP tokens and an Estate. River Shrine can trash two cards per play, which is enough to handle an isolated Witch’s Hut, but using a terminal slot to clean up every turn slows the build. Expect a three-pile threat before the Province pile empties in an engine mirror.

## Engine and worked opening

The smallest promising build is **one River Shrine, two Hostelries, one Witch’s Hut, one Weaver and one Artificer**, with Silver and surviving Coppers for economy. A third Hostelry and either an Alchemist or more reliable Horse gains make the difference between drawing once and doing it under Footpad. Add the first Footpad when you can play it alongside your draw. Add a second Hut if you have spare Actions and still need draw. Alchemist and Horse each grow the hand by one without consuming an Action slot. Witch’s Hut also grows it by one and sees four fresh cards, with Weaver making the discard far more productive.

| Turn | A favorable 4/3 line |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Buy River Shrine and Silver. |
| 3 | With Shrine, two Estates and two Coppers, trash the Estates. Skip the Buy-phase gain, take Hostelry in Clean-up, and discard the two unplayed Coppers for two Horses. |
| 4 | Silver, three Coppers and the last Estate give $5 for Witch’s Hut. |
| 5 | Hostelry and a Horse can find Hut. With a favorable shuffle, use the draw to play Shrine again, trash the last Estate and a Copper, buy Exploration, and gain Weaver in Clean-up. This line retains three Coppers and Silver after trashing and discarding. |
| 6 | Hostelry, the second Horse and Hut can reach Weaver. Discard Weaver to gain a second Hostelry and turn two Coppers into Horses. The three gains draw three cards, and a timely reshuffle can recover the discarded Coppers. The six Coppers and Silver make up to $8 before the banked Coffer. Buy Artificer. |

That line can draw nearly the whole deck on turn 6 with a favorable shuffle. I’d budget **turns 8–10** for repeatable strong turns. By then, aim for three Hostelries, Artificer, Hut and Weaver, plus one or two Alchemists or a steady supply of Horses. A Footpad hit complicates the start. In a 16-card deck with three Hostelries, you open at least one in about **71%** of five-card hands and **49%** of three-card hands, before counting cantrips or other recovery. Save an Exploration Villager for a hand where you need to lead with Hut or another terminal to find the village.

With Trade active, **three Hostelries, one Hut and one Footpad** leave two unused Actions and therefore **three Buys**. A useful built turn has **$7–12 plus any banked Coffers**, typically enough for a Province with another gain or a pair of Triumphs when you can leave the final Debt outstanding. The score depends more on how many cards you gained before buying than on the raw money.

## Key interactions

- **Artificer, Footpad and Hostelry.** Artificer puts its gain on the deck and Footpad’s global rule immediately draws it. After Trade, discard three to gain Hostelry or four to gain Artificer, Hut or Footpad. An Action-phase Hostelry gain with two Treasures discarded gives **three gains and three draws**, including the Horses. Buying Hostelry can also generate Horses if you leave Treasures unplayed. On a finishing turn, Artificer can discard zero, gain and draw a Copper, and add one to each later Triumph. Seven discards can gain a Province after Trade. [Adventures rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Dominion-Adventures-Rules.pdf), [Cornucopia & Guilds rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/CornucopiaGuilds.pdf), [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf)
- **Witch’s Hut and Weaver.** Discard Weaver and another Action to play Weaver for free and hand out a Curse. Weaver can gain **two Silvers and draw twice**, or gain Hostelry for more draw and Actions. Artificer can also discard Weaver for a free play. When the opponent plays Footpad, discarding Weaver can refill your hand before your turn. A Curse gained from Hut also draws its recipient a card under Footpad’s setup rule, so if you play both attacks, **play Hut before Footpad**. [Cornucopia & Guilds rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/CornucopiaGuilds.pdf)
- **Change, Alchemist and Stonemason.** Change can turn an Estate into an Alchemist for **1 Debt**, before or after Trade. Its coin-only comparison lets it ignore the Potion symbol. One Potion later lets you topdeck every Alchemist you played, which is particularly good insurance against Footpad. When it’s time to score, **Stonemason can trash an Alchemist for two Vineyards**, with no Potion purchase required. Going from 13 Actions to 12 in that exchange makes the two Vineyards worth **8 VP**, and the gains draw two cards. A Gold can similarly become two Duchies for **6 VP** and two draws. Change can turn a Gold into a Province for **2 Debt**, and a subsequent Change pays $3 if you leave Debt outstanding. [Rising Sun rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/DomRisingSunRules.pdf), [Alchemy rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/Dominion-Alchemy-Rules.pdf), [Cornucopia & Guilds rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/CornucopiaGuilds.pdf)
- **Gains into Triumph.** Weaver gains two Silvers and Artificer gains Hostelry plus two Horses. That’s **five gains** before buying. Two Triumphs then score **6 and 7 tokens plus two Estates, or 15 VP**. A spare Buy spent on Copper first raises that to **17 VP**. You need $5 available after clearing prior Debt to buy both and leave the second 5 Debt outstanding. Horses count even if you played and returned them. On a bigger turn, add Stonemason trashing Gold for two Duchies and the same two Triumphs score **25 VP total** before any other buys. Buy Triumph after the gains you want it to count. [Empires rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Empires.pdf)
- **River Shrine, Exploration and overpay.** Buying Exploration on a Shrine turn gives **one Coffer, one Villager and a Shrine gain**. The project pays on the turn you buy it if you gained no cards in the Buy phase. After Trade, Shrine can gain any of the original $5 Actions in Clean-up. It gains too late to feed Triumph, and the Estate from a successful Triumph shuts off both Shrine and Exploration for that turn. Stonemason overpay gives three cards for one Buy. After Trade, **$4 total** gets Stonemason plus two Hostelries or other original $4 Actions, **$5** gets Stonemason plus two original $5 Actions, and **$3 + Potion** gets Stonemason plus two Alchemists. [Rising Sun rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/DomRisingSunRules.pdf), [Renaissance rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Renaissance.pdf), [Cornucopia & Guilds rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/CornucopiaGuilds.pdf)

The local ratings put **Weaver at +0.6, Witch’s Hut at +0.4 and Alchemist at −1.1**. All three play above those ratings here. Weaver’s free plays produce immediate draw, Hut supplies the discard and becomes a much more reliable curser in an Action-dense deck, and Change bypasses Alchemist’s usual opening cost. Exploration at −1.4 is worth buying when Shrine can supply the card you would otherwise have bought. ## Plans considered

| Plan | Pace, requirements and matchup |
|---|---|
| **Hostelry–Artificer into Triumph** | My pick. The worked line reaches $6–8 on turn 6. Aim for two Hostelries by the third shuffle, then a third for spare Buys. One Hut attacks about once per early cycle and can attack most turns once the deck draws. The strongest response is a mirror that contests Hostelry and gains Estates before you can cash in. A first Footpad helps win the build race, but every extra terminal competes with a Buy. |
| **Alchemist–Vineyard engine** | The runner-up. Start with the same strong opening, then use Change or a Potion to get two or more Alchemists. A $6 hand can come around turns 5–7 from retained Copper and Silver. An $8 hand can follow around turns 7–9 with Gold or Footpad Coffers, though the scoring plan can work without it. At 12–15 Actions, three or four Vineyards give **12–20 VP**. It can win on piles when the mirror spends down Estates. Its weak point is converting Alchemists while you still need them for draw. |
| **Big Money + Footpad** | Open Footpad / pass, buy early Gold, and add a second Footpad if its attacks are buying time. Footpad plus four Coppers hits $6 as early as turn 3, and Footpad plus Gold and three Coppers hits $8 on a favorable turn 5–7. One Footpad appears in about 45% of opening-shuffle hands. Two in a 17-card deck appear in about half of five-card hands. A Hut can help recover against opposing Footpad, but it competes for the terminal slot. Money’s best chance is an early attack that denies the engine’s first $5, followed by fast Provinces and a pile threat. Weaver and Alchemist make sustained handsize pressure less effective as the engine develops. |

## Open, build and finish

- **4/3 → River Shrine / Silver.** Choose this split. Take Hostelry with the first Shrine when you can make Horses or already have a terminal to pair with it. Take Weaver once Hut or Artificer can discard it. A $5 buy takes priority over a routine Clean-up gain when you need your first Hut or Artificer.
- **5/2 → Artificer / pass.** From an uncut five-card hand, Artificer can discard four to gain and immediately play Shrine, or discard all five to gain and play Hut. Prefer Shrine when it can start thinning or gain Hostelry with a Horse. Stonemason is the $2 option, but its early play typically trashes one Copper or turns an Estate into two Coppers. Footpad / pass is the pressure opening if you choose to race, especially from first seat.
- **Second and third shuffles.** Trash Estates first. Keep enough Copper to hit the first $5 buys and fuel Hostelry. Prioritize the first Hut and Artificer, then the second and third Hostelry. Use Change for an Alchemist early if it will improve your next shuffle. Take one Potion when it can anchor several Alchemist plays or secure Vineyard points. Buy Exploration when Shrine or Action-phase gains already cover development.
- **Greening.** Around turns 8–10, look for a Triumph that scores at least **6–7 total VP** while your Action-phase gains still improve the deck. Green earlier in a mirror if Estates are falling or two piles are low. Save the Alchemist-to-Vineyard conversion for the turn it secures the lead, preserves a VP breakpoint, or ends the game.
- **Ending.** Watch **Hostelry, Stonemason and Estate** first. Curses, Artificer and Vineyard can replace any of them. Stonemason overpay can remove two cards from a contested pile in one Buy, and multiple Shrines can empty a pile in Clean-up. Against money, count the Province clock as well. Track both players’ VP tokens, Vineyard breakpoints, available Estates, Coffers and spare Actions before giving the opponent a turn. With more players, expect more incoming attacks and tighter Hostelry competition. Trade needs more Suns, though more players can contribute Shrine plays, and the pile-ending threshold rises to four at five or six players. [Rising Sun rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/DomRisingSunRules.pdf)

**What could flip the pick.** The Vineyard line is the strongest alternative. If Trade comes on early, Stonemason can overpay for two Alchemists with one Potion, while Change supplies another without one. That gives a real route to accumulating draw and then converting it into points in pairs. I’d pivot toward it if Estates are being drained before my first big Triumph or if my Action count is already at 12–15 and Vineyard is a viable ending pile. The Triumph plan loses most readily when it builds for one extra turn and the opponent takes the last Estates or closes three piles with Vineyards.

**Least sure.** The 4/3 opening’s edge over **5/2 Artificer / pass** is the closest call. Artificer can assemble Shrine plus another piece in one early turn, while Shrine / Silver has a better direct shot at thinning two Estates and buying a $5 card on separate turns. I’d judge the opening by whether it delivers usable draw and a second Hostelry by the third shuffle, especially through an early Footpad. The turn ranges above are worked estimates under the supplied tournament setup.
