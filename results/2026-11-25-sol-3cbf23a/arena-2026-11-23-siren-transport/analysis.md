# Siren / Transport kingdom analysis

**Recommendation.** Open double Transport for a guaranteed turn‑3 Siren on $4/$3. Use the eight-card follow-up to buy into Wharf, then build with Destrier, a cheap Hamlet or a City, and enough Treasure to reach $13–$16. A second Siren is valuable while Curses remain, especially in the mirror. Once the Curse pile is nearly empty, prioritize draw and points.

## Board

Siren sets the early pace, Wharf and Destrier sustain the engine, and the first empty pile turns City into draw and village in one card. The main question is whether the engine reaches two scoring buys before the opponent can use Curses and two other piles to end the game.

| Cost | Kingdom piles | Events |
|---|---|---|
| $2 | Farrier, Hamlet, Jewelled Egg | |
| $3 | Siren | Transport |
| $5 | City, Cursed Village, Den of Sin, Wharf, Wheelwright | Trade |
| $6 | Destrier, discounted by $1 per card you’ve gained that turn | |

Two players start with seven Coppers and three Estates each, split $4/$3 or $5/$2 in either order. There are eight Provinces, eight Duchies, eight Estates in the Supply, and ten Curses. There are no Colonies, Shelters, Heirlooms, split piles, or added Supply piles. The setup uses the non-Supply Loot pile, the Hex deck and its States, and Exile mats. Night, Durations, and overpay matter. There is no $4 Kingdom card, $5 is crowded, and $6 is the first natural Gold or undiscounted Destrier price.

Provinces, Duchies, and Estates supply all positive VP. Siren gives Curses for −1 VP, and Misery can impose −2 or −4 VP without adding a card to the deck. Trade can recover a point by trashing a Curse. There is no VP-token or alternate scoring engine. Expect the Curse pile to empty in a Siren mirror, making a three-pile finish a credible alternative to eight Provinces. ## Engine and key interactions

- **Transport gets Siren online on turn 3.** Siren says, “When you gain this, trash it unless you trash an Action from your hand.” Transport’s two modes move the card from Supply to Exile and from Exile to your deck. Neither move is a gain. Two $3 Events secure the turn‑3 attack without sacrificing an Action. Later, one Transport can bank a Siren, then an ordinary gain of another copy can release the banked one to your discard pile even if the newly gained copy trashes itself. That spends two Sirens from the pile for one playable copy. [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf), [Plunder rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf)
- **Siren, Wharf, and Den of Sin make the next turn.** Resolve Siren’s start-of-turn draw to eight before Wharf or Den’s fixed +2. Siren plus either makes ten cards, and Siren plus both makes twelve if enough cards are available. Den enters your hand when gained and can be played that Night. One Siren can attack at most every other turn because it stays in play through the following turn. Two can alternate once the deck is drawing well. Stagger copies when possible so their refills land on different turns. [Nocturne rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Nocturne.pdf), [Seaside rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/Dominion-Seaside-Rules.pdf)
- **Wharf and Destrier do most of the repeatable work.** Wharf gives two cards and a Buy now and again next turn. Destrier gives two cards while preserving your Action. Hamlet is the cheapest way to play Siren and Wharf together. Discard an Estate or Curse for the extra Action, and use a separate discard if you need its Buy. City provides that spare Action unconditionally and draws two once a pile is empty. Cursed Village is strongest after Hamlet or Wheelwright has shrunk your hand, or on a turn that starts small. With an eight- or ten-card start, it will often give you Actions alone.
- **Wheelwright and extra Buys accelerate Destrier.** After one gain, a later Wheelwright can discard a $5 card to gain Destrier. A Wheelwright can also discard a full-price Destrier to gain another copy, or turn a spare $5 card into Wharf, City, or a Den you can play that Night. With two Buys, $7 buys Farrier first and then Destrier for $5. At $9, Trade can turn two junk cards into Silvers and leave $4 for Destrier. Each actual gain counts, including a Siren that subsequently trashes itself. Buying Transport does not advance the discount. [Hinterlands rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Hinterlands2nd.pdf), [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf)
- **Trade turns an Egg into payload.** Hold Jewelled Egg in hand while making $5 with other cards. Trade an Egg and an Estate or Curse for one Loot and two Silvers. The deck grows by one, but two weak cards become three payload cards. Those three gains also make Destrier cost $3, so the whole package costs $8 with two Buys. Trading two Eggs produces two Loot and two Silvers if both piles have enough cards, but lining up both Eggs and $5 is a much narrower opportunity. Prioritize Curses when trading ordinary junk. [Adventures rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Adventures.pdf), [Plunder rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf)

**Worked build.** Double Transport leaves Siren and four starting cards in your turn‑3 hand. Three or four Coppers appear two-thirds of the time, enough for Silver. On a clean shuffle, that Silver line guarantees at least $5 on turn 4. A plausible sequence is Wharf on turn 4, Gold on a $6 Wharf turn 5, then replay Siren and buy Den on turn 6. On turn 7, Siren plus Den starts at ten cards. A Wharf play can get you to twelve cards seen and two Buys, enough in this line to add City and a discounted Destrier on $10. Incoming Curses and the shuffle can move any of those purchases back a turn or two.

The first near-full draw is plausible around turns 7–9. I’d expect the first reliable big scoring turns around turns 9–11, with heavier junking pushing them later. Aim for one or two Sirens, two Wharves or a Wharf and a Den, two or three Destriers as the deck grows, and enough Hamlet or City plays for the terminals you actually draw together. A built turn with six Coppers, two Silvers, a Gold and a Sword makes **$16**. One Wharf and Sword give three total Buys, enough for two Provinces. Even without Loot, five Coppers, three Silvers and two Golds make $17. The risk is the turn between refills. Keep a source of fixed draw for it and buy a spare village if terminal collisions are costing attacks or Wharf plays.

## Other cards and the source-game order

- **Farrier** is the default spare $2 buy. It supplies +Buy without consuming a terminal slot and can be held to pay for a directly gained Siren. Overpay when it will improve a weak next hand. Extra cards drawn during cleanup often just replace draws Siren would have made anyway. [Cornucopia & Guilds rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/CornucopiaGuilds.pdf)
- **Jewelled Egg** is worth taking when you can plausibly hold it and pay for Trade within a shuffle. In the source game, the first player to trash one gets **Sword**, a $3 Treasure with +Buy that cuts the opponent to four cards. One possible line after double Transport is turn‑3 Egg on three Coppers, then turn‑4 Trade if Siren’s refill finds the Egg and a fifth Copper. That can turn a $5 Trade into a $7 Sword-plus-Silver hand with two Buys on turn 5. Siren’s refill absorbs the hand-size attack on covered turns. Sword is more punishing on the gaps. Other Loot can change the plan too, especially Prize Goat for repeatable trashing and Insignia for topdecking a gained Siren before it can trash itself. [Plunder rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/DomPlunder.pdf)
- **Cursed Village** has a useful Transport route when you need the village and want to avoid the gain Hex, though it costs two Buys and $6 in total. The source game’s first Hex was **Delusion**. If you receive it by buying Cursed Village, it restricts Action purchases on your *next* turn. You can still buy Den, Treasure, Victory cards, or either Event, and Wheelwright can still gain Actions. [Menagerie rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Dominion-Menagerie-Rules.pdf), [Nocturne rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Dominion-Rules-Nocturne.pdf)
- **City** is worth buying ahead of the Curse pile emptying if you need a village anyway. With one empty pile it is already a strong engine piece. With two it adds $1 and +Buy as well. If you have the Actions to play Siren first and it hands out the last Curse, City gets the better effect that same Action phase. [Prosperity rules](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Prosperity2nd.pdf)

The community ratings line up with Wharf as the premium enabler (+6.3) and Farrier as a strong cheap buy (+4.4). Siren (+2.4), City (+1.9), and Jewelled Egg (about 0) gain the most from this particular board. Transport bypasses Siren’s normal entry cost, Siren empties a pile for City, and Trade gives Egg a reliable trash outlet. Trade’s −2.3 still reflects a real cost here. Every ordinary Trade spends $5 and a Buy to replace cards one-for-one. ## Plans considered and matchup

| Plan | Rough race | What it needs and how it loses |
|---|---|---|
| **Siren–Wharf engine** | First $6 around turns 4–6, first $8 around turns 6–8, first big scoring turn around turns 9–11. Turn‑3 Siren on $4/$3, with repeat attacks as it cycles. | Needs one early Siren, at least one early Wharf, and follow-up draw. Can lose to an attack-heavy mirror that drains Curses and takes a points lead while you spend $5 buys on infrastructure. |
| **Two-Siren tempo and pile pressure** | Same turn‑3 attack. A $6 refill can buy Gold or fund two Transports for Siren #2 if you have two Buys. A Gold and five Coppers give the first $8, plausibly turns 6–8. Attack on most turns if the copies stagger. | Needs two Sirens and enough +Buy to pressure the piles. Strong when the opponent delays their own Siren or overbuilds. Its extra attack investment stops paying once Curses run out, and a City–Wharf deck can then outscore it. |
| **Wharf Big Money** | Silver/Silver on $4/$3, or an early Wharf or Den on $5/$2. First $6 around turns 3–5, first $8 around turns 5–7 on good draws. Expect zero Siren plays in the baseline. | Needs one or two Wharves and early Gold. Trade plus Egg is its strongest accelerator if the draw lines up, especially for the first Sword. It can take early Provinces, but Curses drag down the turns between Wharves and leave it vulnerable to a bigger scoring turn. |

The best response to the recommended engine is a **Siren mirror that contests Wharf and greens promptly**, especially if the opponent secures Sword. Match the early Curse pressure, then use Hamlet for a cheap terminal slot or City as the Curse pile gets low. Den and Destrier let you keep building if Wharf is contested. The best response to the two-Siren tempo plan is to get your own Sirens going and build Cities for the moment the Curse pile empties. At that point, the better draw deck has the advantage unless tempo already has a winning pile threat.

## Openings and buy priorities

| Opening order | Recommended buys |
|---|---|
| **$4 → $3 or $3 → $4** | **Transport Siren to Exile, then Transport it onto your deck.** Play it on turn 3. Default to Silver at $3–$4, Farrier at $2, and pass at $1. Take Egg over Silver when you want to race for the first Loot and accept the risk of missing the Trade. |
| **$5 → $2 before Night draw** | **Den of Sin, then Transport Siren to Exile.** Play Den immediately. Its two draws are both Coppers, so turn 2 actually has $4. Turn 3 has at least $3 from the three remaining Coppers, so the second Transport guarantees turn‑4 Siren. If you favor build speed over that guarantee, turn‑2 Farrier with $2 overpay gives a seven-card turn 3 and a shot at a direct Siren purchase. |
| **$2 → $5** | **Farrier, then Den of Sin.** Den gives seven cards on turn 3 and Farrier appears in seven of the eleven available positions. If it appears, leave it in hand to secure Siren. If it misses, take Wharf at $5 or make an economy buy and reassess. |

- **First shuffle.** Get Siren online and buy the first Wharf. Prefer Den when it rescues an otherwise weak next hand, when Wharf would collide with Siren too often, or when Deluded is active.
- **Second and third shuffles.** Add Hamlet when you have two terminals worth playing together. Take City as Curses dwindle. Add the second Siren while several Curses remain, then use fixed draw and discounted Destriers to smooth the gaps. Use Trade for an Egg or a strong two-junk hand. Once you’re drawing most of the deck, buy Gold if it moves you toward $13 or $16.
- **Greening.** In the mirror, start taking Provinces as soon as you can keep the next turn functional, especially when the Curse pile is nearly empty. Against money, you can invest longer while the piles are safe. At $13, Province plus Duchy gives 9 VP. At $16, two Provinces give 12 VP. Take the line that leaves the opponent without a winning reply.

**Ending.** Count Curses, Farriers, and Sirens first, then Duchies and any heavily contested engine pile. Wheelwright can remove a final cheap Action without using a Buy, and Transport can empty an Action pile straight into Exile. If two piles are already empty, $11 and two Buys can buy a Province and exile the last Siren in the same turn. Check the score after Curses, Estates trashed to Trade, and Misery. Four Provinces apiece is often an unequal score here.

**What would change the pick.** A fast second opposing Siren raises the value of your own second copy and of a City timed for the Curse pile. An early Sword raises the value of covering every start of turn with Siren, Den, or Wharf. With more players, each Siren hands out more total Curses, incoming attacks stack, and key piles are more contested. Get a functional draw package sooner and green earlier when the table can end on piles.

**Challenge and least certain point.** The strongest rival is a fast Sword opening that uses Trade for Silver, then goes straight into Wharf and Gold. It can beat a Siren engine that spends too many turns on Transport or buys villages before it has draw worth supporting. I’d still start with the guaranteed Siren on $4/$3. The closest call is the turn‑3 Egg over Silver when Sword is first. Silver is the steadier route to a turn‑4 $5 purchase. Egg wins the comparison when it converts early enough that Sword attacks before the opponent has regular Duration coverage.

**Assumptions.** The turn ranges are hand-worked estimates. Delusion and Sword are treated as known first cards for the source-game branch. In an ordinary setup, their order is hidden.
