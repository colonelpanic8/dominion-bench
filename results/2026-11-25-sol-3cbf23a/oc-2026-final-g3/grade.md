## Beyond the key
- Golem is a skip: Potion costs a gain and a deck slot, Golem can discard the Horn or Spoils you wanted, and the deck already has villages: plausible. This is the reasoning the grader note asks for.
- Spoils returns to its pile on play and an Overlord'd card stays in the Supply, so neither adds a Horn name (Overlord does): plausible. This follows directly from both cards' text.
- Upgrade is clean Copper thinning because no card costs exactly $1: plausible. No Supply card costs $1 and Overlord costs $0 plus Debt.
- A Horn trashed for scoring adds a Treasure name to the shared trash for Forager on both sides: plausible. Forager counts differently named Treasures in the trash.
- Scrounge for Duchy plus Estate is 4 VP, and $11 with two Buys makes Province plus Scrounge for 10 VP: plausible. Scrounge gains the Estate from the trash and then a card up to $5.
- An Inn gained to the discard pile can shuffle itself in with chosen Actions: plausible. It is an Action card in the discard pile when its on-gain ability resolves.
- A 16-card deck with three villages and two draw cards gives a five-card hand with both about 35% of the time and neither about 11%: plausible. The hypergeometric arithmetic checks out (0.353 and 0.106).
- "Opening Overlord alone uses all seven opening coins and still leaves one Debt": doubtful. It treats Overlord as a whole opening, but the winner's Upgrade/Overlord on 5/2 just carries the Debt into turns 3–4.
- The Patrol Big Money line hits $7 around turn 5 and $8 around turns 7–8: plausible. It is untested and not part of the key.

## Summary
The analysis understands that Horn wants many differently named cards and that draw must keep up. But it plays Horn as a side payload of one or two copies on a Bandit Camp/Council Room Province engine, not the six-to-seven-Horn megaturn that won. It also opens the Forager the commentators disliked and wants a second Bandit Camp where the key prefers Walled Village.
