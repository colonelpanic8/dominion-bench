## Beyond the key
- Cage + Market Square Gold loop: plausible. A Victory gain trashes the Cage in play, and each unplayed Market Square in hand can be discarded in reaction to gain a Gold. Cage may set aside 0 cards, so it can be played just for this. Neither finalist used it. It costs the held Market Squares' draw and +Buy, and needs a $2 Cage rebuy each turn, so the claimed 2–3 Golds a turn is optimistic.
- Cage's set-aside cards return after the Clean-up draw and add to the next hand: plausible. "At end of turn" comes after Clean-up.
- Nothing in the Kingdom costs $4: plausible. The costs are 0P/2/2/3/3/3/5/5/5/5.
- Tunnel gains a Gold when Border Guard discards it, or when the opponent's Jester does: plausible. Both discards are outside Clean-up. A Jester hit on Tunnel also gives the Tunnel's owner a Curse, since Tunnel is a Victory card.
- Way of the Squirrel's +2 Cards at the end of the turn makes the next hand 7 cards: plausible. "End of this turn" comes after the Clean-up draw.
- Litter's Debt must be paid before you buy: plausible. This is the standard Debt rule.
- Jester can gain copies of the opponent's Golds or Litters off their top card: plausible. That is Jester's text.
- Seaway gaining a $3 Market Square for $5 is poor: doubtful. It is cheap to dismiss from card text alone, but putting the +Buy token on Market Square is exactly what the finalists did to drain piles. The analysis allows it only "if Buys are short".
- Each Market Square can react to a trash only once, because it is discarded: plausible.

## Summary
The analysis has the right parts: Litter as village/draw, Patrol over Council Room, Cage early on Estates, and Potion for Vineyards. But it aims them at a slow Province engine built around an untested Cage/Market Square Gold loop. The game was a fast Action-dense Vineyard race that three-piled on turn 14. It undervalues Border Guard and Jester, and expects Provinces to end the game.
