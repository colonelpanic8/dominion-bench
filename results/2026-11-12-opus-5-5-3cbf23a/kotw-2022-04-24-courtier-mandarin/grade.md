## Beyond the key
- Barge and Cardinal are two-type reveals for Courtier (+1 Action and +$3): plausible. Barge is Action–Duration, Cardinal Action–Attack.
- Several Courtiers can reveal the same card in hand: plausible. Revealing doesn't move the card.
- Courtier revealing Tunnel gives +$3 and a Gold: plausible. Tunnel is Victory–Reaction.
- Tunnel is "the only green card that still pays Courtier": doubtful. Any Victory card gives one choice; Tunnel is just the only one giving two.
- Displace exiles a Gold to gain a Province without a Buy, and a later Gold gain lets you discard the Exiled Golds back: plausible. $6 + 2 = $8, differently named; Exile returns copies on gaining that card.
- Gamble flipping Tunnel gains a Gold, flipping Village Green plays it (choose next-turn mode): plausible. Both react to discards outside Clean-up, and Village Green's duration mode applies from the Buy phase.
- Gamble can play Barge in the Buy phase for next turn's +3 Cards +1 Buy: plausible. Barge is an Action and has a next-turn mode.
- In a Gold-heavy deck Gamble nets about +$1 and is Buy-neutral: plausible. It costs $2, gives +1 Buy, and a flipped Gold gives $3.
- Way of the Goat makes Lurker a $2 trasher and lets a dead Courtier or Barge trash an Estate: plausible. Any Action can be played as the Way.
- Cardinal's discards trigger the opponent's Tunnel and Village Green: plausible. Cardinal discards the non-Exiled card outside Clean-up.
- Cardinal-exiled Courtiers come back on a later Courtier gain: plausible. Exile's gain rule.
- Displace on an Estate gains Village Green or Cardinal at 5/2: plausible. $2 + 2 = $4, differently named.
- Silk Road is often worth 3 VP for $4: doubtful. That needs 12 Victory cards, and its plan trashes Estates with Steward early.
- Lurker trashing Village Greens from the Supply for denial or a three-pile: plausible. Lurker can trash any Action from the Supply.

## Summary
The analysis builds around Courtier and multi-type reveals, finds the Village Green three-choice reveal and Mandarin's topdeck of it, and covers +Buy and Steward thinning, so most rubric items land. But it misses the heart of the expert plan: Mandarin's first-edition on-gain topdecking all Golds in play to replay them every turn, which it treats as a one-off, and Mandarin never enters its build. It also opens Cardinal, which both commenters called useless, and keeps a Displace the expert called not economical.
