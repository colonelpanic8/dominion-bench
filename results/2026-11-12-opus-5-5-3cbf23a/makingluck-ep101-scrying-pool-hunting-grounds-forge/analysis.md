**Board:** A Prosperity/Hinterlands/Dark Ages/Alchemy mix with no +Buy and no cheap trasher. Hunting Grounds is a strong draw card. It is also a $6 card that pays out a Duchy (or 3 Estates) when trashed, and Trader and Forge both trash it. So the board is a money-with-draw deck that turns cheap cards into VP through Forge's "trash $8 worth, gain a Province" and the Hunting Grounds trash bonus. The open questions are whether Scrying Pool, Encampment and Coin of the Realm can carry a real engine, and how hard Noble Brigand hits a deck full of Silvers.

**Engine:** The pieces are weak for an engine.
- **Draw:** Hunting Grounds (+4, terminal), Scholar (+7, terminal) and Scrying Pool (non-terminal, but only in an Action-dense deck).
- **Actions:** Encampment works only if you reveal a Gold or Plunder. Otherwise it is a one-use village that goes back to its pile. Coin of the Realm gives +2 Actions once per shuffle per copy.
- **Thinning:** only Forge, at $7 and terminal.
- **+Buy:** none. The payload beyond one buy is extra Forge plays, each needing $8 of trashable cards in hand, plus Plunder's VP.
- **Plunder** sits under 5 Encampments that bounce back to the pile, so in practice it is out of reach.
- **Speed:** with a Potion, $6–7 parts and no +Buy to speed the build, a deck that draws itself arrives around turn 12 or later. Its built turn is about one buy plus one or two Forge conversions, which the money plan below also gets.

**Key interactions:**
- **Forge + Hunting Grounds + any $2 card (Estate, Coin of the Realm, Encampment):** the trashed cards total exactly $8, so you gain a Province. Trashing Hunting Grounds also gains a Duchy. That is 9 VP from one Action, and you still have your Buy. It works from about turn 9, once Forge and Hunting Grounds are both in the deck.
- **Forge converts other junk too:** Gold + Estate, Duchy + Silver, or Silver + Silver + Estate gains a Province. Silver + Silver gains a Gold or a Hunting Grounds. Forge is a remodel to Province that also cleans out Estates.
- **Trader + Hunting Grounds:** trashing Hunting Grounds gains 6 Silvers and a Duchy. Trading Gold also gives 6 Silvers. With Forge (2 Silvers → Hunting Grounds) this is a loop that adds 4 Silvers and a Duchy per Trader-plus-Forge pair. It bloats the deck, so treat it as a late-game Duchy source, not a plan.
- **Trader + Estate:** 2 Silvers on turn 3 or 4. This is the economy opener.
- **Beggar + Trader in hand:** Beggar's 3 Coppers can each be exchanged for a Silver.

**Other cards:**
- Coin of the Realm: the best village here. It is also $2 of Forge fodder, so Coin + Hunting Grounds trashes into a Province.
- Encampment: works only next to Gold, so it's a marginal village. Its split pile keeps Plunder effectively unavailable.
- Scrying Pool: needs Action density this board can't thin into. Its attack is minor. Skip it, along with the Potion it needs.
- Scholar: an acceptable money draw card, but worse than Hunting Grounds and it collides with it.
- Mandarin: a +$3 terminal that competes for the same terminal slot. Only an option on 5/2.
- Noble Brigand: steals the opponent's Silvers and Golds. It hurts a Trader deck a little, and it's weak as payload.
- Beggar: counters Noble Brigand (discard it to gain 2 Silvers). Otherwise none.

**Plans considered:**
1. **Hunting Grounds money (baseline):** open Silver/Silver, Hunting Grounds at $6, Gold, Province at $8. First $6 on turn 3–5, first $8 around turn 7–9, 4 Provinces around turn 14–15. Weakness: plays only one terminal per turn.
2. **Trader / Hunting Grounds / Forge money (pick):** open Trader. Add 1–2 Hunting Grounds, 1–2 Forges and 1–2 Coins of the Realm, plus Golds. Each Forge play turns Hunting Grounds + a $2 card into Province + Duchy, or junk into a Province, on top of a normal buy. It needs Forge by about turn 8. Weakness: Forge, Hunting Grounds and Trader are all terminals, so it collides without Coins of the Realm.
3. **Scrying Pool / Encampment engine:** draws itself around turn 12 or later and has no +Buy. Rejected.

**Recommendation:** Plan 2. Worked line:
- **Turns 1–2:** Trader/Silver.
- **Turns 3–4:** Trader is drawn about 5 times in 6 and trashes an Estate for 2 Silvers. The deck becomes 7 Copper, 2 Estates, 3 Silvers and Trader. Buy Silver, or Hunting Grounds at $6.
- **Turns 5–7:** about $1.2 per card makes $6 common. Buy Hunting Grounds or Gold, and Coin of the Realm on a $2–3 hand.
- **Turns 7–9:** Forge at $7, Province at $8.
- **Turns 9–15:** most Forge turns turn junk into a Province while you still buy, about 9 VP when Hunting Grounds is the fodder.
- **Result:** about 4 Provinces and 3–4 Duchies by turns 14–15. This beats plan 1, because Forge adds VP without using the Buy and also removes Estates.

**Against their best plan:** The opponent's best plan is the mirror or Hunting Grounds money. Against Hunting Grounds money, Forge's extra 6–9 VP per play beats one draw card. In the mirror, Coins of the Realm (fewer collisions) and the Penultimate Province Rule decide it.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Trader / Silver.
- **5/2:** Trader / Coin of the Realm. Mandarin or Scholar plus Coin is a weaker alternative.

**Buy priorities:**
1. Province ($8).
2. Forge ($7): the first one, and a second once the deck passes about 20 cards.
3. Hunting Grounds or Gold ($6): first Hunting Grounds, then alternate with Gold.
4. Silver ($3–5), Coin of the Realm ($2–3): 1–2 copies.

When to green:
- **Duchies:** from about 4 Provinces left.
- **Late Trader plays:** use them on Hunting Grounds for a Duchy and Silvers.
- **Forge late:** use it on Duchy + Silver → Province.

**Ending:** Provinces run out, probably around turns 14–16. Duchies drain fast because of Hunting Grounds trashes. A three-pile ending (Duchy, Estate, Hunting Grounds) is possible but unlikely.

**Watch out for:**
- **The Penultimate Province Rule with Forge:** with Forge, Hunting Grounds + Estate and $8, the opponent can take the last two Provinces in one turn. Count their Forge plays before leaving 2 Provinces.
- **Noble Brigand:** if they buy several, stop relying on Silvers. Pick up 1 Beggar and use Forge more than Trader.
- **Terminal collisions:** if they happen often, add another Coin of the Realm before another terminal.

**Challenged:** I re-tested the Scrying Pool / Hunting Grounds / Coin of the Realm engine, using Forge to thin and as payload. Even when built it gets one buy plus at most two Forge conversions, and fodder limits those. That is about the same output as plan 2, delivered 3–4 turns later. My pick loses if Forge arrives late and the deck greens into collisions while a Hunting Grounds money opponent hits $8 steadily.

**Least sure of:** That Trader beats a Silver/Silver opening. The extra Silvers from trashing an Estate are good, but Trader takes a terminal slot later. Test: compare Province counts on turn 12 against Silver/Silver into Hunting Grounds.

**Assumed:**
- Forge's total counts only $ cost, so a Potion counts as $4 and Scrying Pool can't be gained by Forge.
- Gaining a Copper is mandatory when the trashed cards total $0.
- Trader can be revealed for each gain, and the Silver goes to the discard pile.
- Mandarin and Noble Brigand are first-edition only (Hinterlands).
- Ratings check: Trader (−4.0) and Forge (−0.8) are rated well below my view, because of the Hunting Grounds trash bonus. Scrying Pool (3.0) is rated above my view because this board has no cheap thinning.
