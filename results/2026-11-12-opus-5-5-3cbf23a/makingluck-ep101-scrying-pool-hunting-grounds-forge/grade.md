## Beyond the key
- Forge on Hunting Grounds + a $2 card gains a Province, and Hunting Grounds' on-trash gains a Duchy: plausible. $6 + $2 = $8, and Hunting Grounds gains a Duchy or 3 Estates when trashed.
- Forge on Gold + Estate, Duchy + Silver, or Silver + Silver + Estate gains a Province, and Silver + Silver gains Gold or Hunting Grounds: plausible. The totals are exact by the costs.
- Trader on Hunting Grounds gains 6 Silvers plus a Duchy: plausible, from the text of both cards.
- Encampment is a one-use village unless you reveal Gold, and the bouncing Encampments keep Plunder out of reach: plausible. Encampments without a Gold return to their pile, so the top of the split pile rarely runs out.
- Forge can't gain Scrying Pool, and a trashed Potion counts $4: plausible. Forge needs an exact $ cost, and Potion costs $4.
- Penultimate Province Rule is sharper with Forge, since an opponent can take two Provinces in a turn: plausible. A Forge conversion plus an $8 buy does it.
- Beggar counters Noble Brigand by turning into 2 Silvers: plausible, from Beggar's reaction, though the key rates it weaker than a Silver flood.
- The Scrying Pool engine draws itself only around turn 12 or later and its attack is minor: doubtful. Playtesting found the Scrying Pool deck about as strong as the Coin of the Realm/Hunting Grounds deck, mainly because of its attack.
- The Trader + Forge + Hunting Grounds loop as a late Duchy source: plausible but marginal. The analysis says itself that it bloats the deck.

## Summary
The analysis gets the core right: Forge is the thinning and the only way past one Province a turn with no +Buy, and its deck is essentially the experts' Coin of the Realm/Hunting Grounds/Forge build. It opens Trader/Silver instead of the tested Silver/Silver, and it dismisses Scrying Pool, missing the Scrying Pool/Noble Brigand line that playtesting found equally strong. Its advice against Noble Brigand runs opposite to the expert's Silver-flood counter.
