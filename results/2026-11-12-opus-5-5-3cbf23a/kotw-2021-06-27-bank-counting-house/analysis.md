**Board:** The board turns on **Tower + Copper**. Tower scores 1 VP for every non-Victory card you have from an empty Supply pile, and Copper is a Supply pile with 46 cards left. Talisman gains a copy of every Copper you buy, because Copper costs $0 and isn't a Victory card. Travelling Fair supplies cheap Buys. Together they can empty the Copper pile by about turn 11–13, and then every Copper you own, the 7 you started with included, is worth 1 VP. Counting House and Bank turn a deck full of Coppers into big turns. Everything else is support or a counter.

**Engine:** No engine here can draw its whole deck.
- **Villages:** Farming Village, and Bustling Village, which only becomes available once the 5 Settlers are gone.
- **Draw:** Haunted Woods (+3 next turn) and Enchantress (+2 next turn). Both are terminal Durations.
- **Thinning:** only Raze. Trashing Coppers also throws away Tower points.
- **Payload:** Horse Traders, Bank and Travelling Fair give money and Buys.
- **Speed:** a Duration good-stuff deck like this first plays most of itself around turn 11–13 and stalls often. Its ceiling is about 1–2 Provinces a turn, which is too slow against the Copper plan.

**Key interactions:**
- **Talisman + Travelling Fair + Copper → Tower.** With k Talismans in play and $X, buy Travelling Fair n times (total cost $2n). That leaves n+1 Buys, so spending them all on Coppers gains (n+1)(k+1) Coppers.
  - A $5 hand with 2 Talismans buys Fair twice for 9 Coppers.
  - At the realistic 5–8 Coppers a turn from turn 7, the 46 Coppers are gone around turns 11–13, opponent's buys included. That's roughly +35–40 VP that the opponent can't take back.
- **Talisman doubles itself.** Each Talisman bought with one in play is two Talismans. That raises k and empties the Talisman pile, which is a second pile and scores under Tower too: roughly 6–8 VP from holding most of the 10.
- **Counting House + Copper flood.** About halfway through a shuffle the discard holds 15–25 Coppers. A Counting House hand then makes $20+, which pays for 2–3 Provinces through Fair, or about 9 Fairs and 20 or more Coppers. Travelling Fair can put a newly gained Counting House or Bank on top of your deck to set up the next turn.
- **Bank in a Treasure-only deck.** Bank plus 4 Coppers is $9, so Bank is how a deck of $1 cards reaches Provinces.
- **Curses under Tower.** Curse isn't a Victory card, so if the Curse pile empties, each Curse is −1 + 1 = 0 VP. That makes a Curse-pile ending (10 Curses at $0) an option for the third pile.

**Other cards:**
- **Jester:** the best counter to the plan. When it reveals a Copper or Talisman from the flood player's deck, the Jester player can take the copy (VP for them, and it still drains the pile). It only hands out Curses when it reveals a Victory card.
- **Enchantress:** Talisman doubles it at $3. Its attack does almost nothing to a Treasure deck, apart from blanking a Counting House played as your first Action. Play Settlers or Raze first to absorb it.
- **Haunted Woods:** its attack is close to useless against the flood, because the hand is empty after you play your Treasures. It's a decent BM+X card for the opponent.
- **Horse Traders (1st ed.):** Talisman doubles it, and it gives +Buy and $3. Its discards go to the discard pile, where Counting House can take the Coppers back that turn. Optional.
- **Settlers:** a cantrip that takes back a Copper, so a cantrip worth $1 here. Coppers are better because they score.
- **Raze:** a $2 pile that Talisman doubles, so it's the easy **third pile** to end the game. Don't trash Coppers with it.
- **Farming Village:** only matters to the rejected engine.
- **Ratings check:** the ratings undervalue this board's key cards (Talisman −1.5, Counting House −3.1, Bank −1.6, Fair 0.5, Tower 0.7). The reason is board-specific: Tower turns the $0 pile into the main source of VP.

**Plans considered:**
1. **Talisman/Fair Copper flood (Tower), with Counting House and Bank as payload.**
   - Line: Talisman turn 1 or 2, then 6+ Talismans by about turn 6. Copper floods from turn 7, Copper pile empty around turn 11–13. Counting House and Bank turns take Provinces.
   - Needs: more than half of the Talisman pile.
   - Weakness: an opponent who takes Coppers and Talismans too, by buying them or with Jester.
2. **BM + Haunted Woods / Jester, with Bank.** First $6 around turn 4–5, first $8 around turn 6–7, and about 4 Provinces by turn 14–17. Haunted Woods is in hand about 1 turn in 3. It can't score fast enough to beat 40+ Tower points.
3. **Farming Village / Duration-draw engine with Horse Traders, Bank and Fair.** Draw is weak and thinning is poor, so it first plays most of its deck around turn 12+. It has no answer to the Tower points.

**Recommendation:** Play the Copper flood. Its VP comes from a pile the opponent can only share in, not stop. My Treasure deck ignores Enchantress and Haunted Woods. I also control the ending: Copper, then Talisman, then Raze, Curses or Enchantress as the third pile.

**Against their best plan:** Their best answer is to join in: take Talismans and put spare Buys into Coppers, with Jester stealing copies, while still buying Provinces.
- **Mirror:** whoever wins the Talisman split usually wins the Copper split. So take Talismans first and hardest, and don't let them reach 4+.
- **If they ignore the Coppers and play BM** (worked from my line above): around turn 13 I have about 40 Coppers, about 8 Talismans and 3 Estates, roughly 50 VP. They have 3–4 Provinces, their 7 Coppers and 3 Estates, roughly 34 VP. Then I close the third pile.
- **Against the runner-up (BM+X):** the flood still wins on that clock, unless they take 15+ Coppers. That's why Counting House and Bank Provinces are part of the plan.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Talisman / Silver.
- **5/2:** Talisman / Travelling Fair (2 Coppers). Raze is the alternative at $2.

**Buy priorities:**
1. **Turns 3–6:** Talisman at every $4+ (two with one in play). Silver or Fair + Coppers on weak hands.
2. **From about turn 6–7:** spend $5 hands with Talismans on Fair×n and Coppers.
3. One or two Counting Houses at $5 on hands without a Talisman, once there are about 15 Coppers.
4. Bank at $7.
5. **Big Counting House turns:** Provinces through Fair if the Copper pile is safely on its way to empty, otherwise flood more Coppers.
6. **Greening:** Provinces on any $8+ turn from about turn 9. Duchies or Estates late. Then end the game on the third pile while ahead.

**Ending:** Three piles: Copper, Talisman, then Raze, Enchantress or Curses (Curses cost 0 net VP under Tower). Likely around turn 13–15. The Province pile isn't the clock.

**Watch out for:**
- **Opponent opens Talisman or Jester:** contest Talismans immediately.
- **Opponent quietly buying Coppers:** every one they take is a 2-VP swing. Speed up the floods and push Provinces through Counting House and Bank.
- **More players:** the Copper pile splits more ways. Talisman count matters even more, and the piles empty sooner.

**Challenged:**
- **The Province plan, re-tested at its best:** BM+Haunted Woods buying Coppers with spare Fair Buys while taking Provinces. It ties the flood only if it takes about 15 Coppers and 4 Provinces by turn 13. The flood player's Talisman multiplier makes that hard, but not impossible.
- **How the pick loses:** the opponent wins the Talisman split (mirror going second), or Jester draws away enough Coppers and Talismans.

**Least sure of:** How fast the Copper pile empties. Talisman density falls as the Coppers pile up, so k+1 drops toward about 1.7 late. If emptying slips past turn 15, a contesting opponent's Provinces come back into it. Counting House turns are the fix. The test is to track Coppers left against turn number from turn 7.

**Assumed:**
- Tower counts every Copper you own, starting Coppers included, once the Copper pile is empty. Copper is a Supply pile.
- Talisman triggers on Copper and Curse, since both cost $0 and aren't Victory cards.
- Travelling Fair's topdecking is optional, so floods go to the discard pile for Counting House.
- **First-edition only:** Counting House, Talisman, Farming Village and Horse Traders. Jester remains in the 2nd edition.
