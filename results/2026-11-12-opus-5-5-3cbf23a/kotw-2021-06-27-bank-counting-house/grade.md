## Beyond the key
- **Talisman gains a copy of every Copper (and Curse) bought:** plausible. Both cost $0, are not Victory cards, and cost $4 or less.
- **Tower scores every Copper you own, starting ones included, and a cleared Talisman pile scores too:** plausible. Tower counts each non-Victory card you have from an empty Supply pile.
- **Curses cost 0 net VP under Tower if the Curse pile empties, so it can be the third pile:** plausible. Curse is a non-Victory Supply card, so Tower's +1 cancels its −1.
- **Jester can take a copy of a Copper or Talisman it reveals, which drains the pile for the Jester player:** plausible. With a non-Victory card, Jester gives the copy to either player, the Jester player's choice.
- **Enchantress mostly misses a Treasure deck, apart from blanking a Counting House played first, and playing Settlers or Raze first absorbs it:** plausible. It only replaces the first Action played. But it understates the threat the key names.
- **The Haunted Woods attack is almost useless against the flood:** plausible. By the time you buy, your hand is usually empty of Treasures.
- **Horse Traders' discards go to the discard pile where Counting House can take the Coppers back that turn:** doubtful. Both are terminal Actions, so this needs a village or Madman-style Actions the line doesn't plan for.
- **The Copper pile empties around turns 11–13, worth about 35–40 VP:** doubtful. It needs 6+ Talismans by turn 6, and the analysis itself says Talisman density falls as the Coppers pile up.
- **Raze, at $2 and doubled by Talisman, is the easy third pile:** plausible. Raze is a non-Victory card costing $4 or less.

## Summary
The analysis found the right cards (Counting House, Travelling Fair, Bank, Tower, buying Coppers) but built them around a Talisman/Tower Copper-pile race. It missed the topdeck-every-gain, never-reshuffle loop the whole thread converged on. So it plans a turn 13–15 pile ending instead of a turn 8–9 Bank/Province finish, and it downplays Enchantress as a threat.
