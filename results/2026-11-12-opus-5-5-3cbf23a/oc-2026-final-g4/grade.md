## Beyond the key
- Aristocrat sequencing: four copies run themselves (+3 Actions, +3 Cards, +$3, +3 Buys), and a 5th needs one spare Action: plausible. Starting with 1 Action, the 1st gives 3 and the 2nd–4th use them up, so the 5th needs one more.
- First Mate plays every Jester or Corsair in hand for one Action: plausible. "Play any number of Action cards with the same name from your hand."
- Corsair's attack misses Loots and Action money: plausible. It trashes only Silver or Gold.
- Ritual values (Loot +6, Lab/Jester/First Mate +4, Flag Bearer +3 net of the Curse): plausible. Ritual gives +1 VP per $1 of the trashed card, minus 1 VP for the Curse.
- Flag Bearer/Silver opening hits $5 on turn 2 one time in six: plausible. The Flag's 6th card comes from a reshuffle of 4 Copper, Estate and Flag Bearer.
- Ghost Town first on 4/3 gives $5 on turn 2 three times in five: plausible. Ghost Town is played that night, and its +1 Card at the start of turn 2 reshuffles 3 Copper and 2 Estates.
- Tactician is weak here, at most one: doubtful. In a deck with no thinning and a slog ahead, a 5-card turn with +Buy is a standard tool, and both finalists bought Tacticians (1 and 2).
- Three piles possible (Curses + Aristocrats + Labs or Eggs): plausible but speculative. Curses do drain from both Jester and Ritual.

## Summary
The analysis finds every interaction the key lists: Ritual on Flag Bearer, Jester junking, Egg into a Loot, the no-thinning Lab/Aristocrat build. But it ranks them as an engine plan with Ritual as late payload. It doesn't see that Ritual on Flag Bearer would become the game, a Flag war that slows both decks. It also tells the player to hold the Flag throughout, with no point at which to stop contesting and build.
