## Beyond the key
- Durations played last turn (Grotto, Amulet, Enchantress) are still in play and count toward Peddler's discount: plausible. A Duration with an effect still to come stays in play until the Clean-up of the turn that effect happens, and Peddler counts Action cards in play.
- Hammer gains a Peddler at the Buy-phase discount: plausible. Hammer is a Treasure played in the Buy phase, and Peddler's reduction applies "during a player's Buy phase".
- Remodel can't gain Fortune: plausible. A cost of $8 plus 8 Debt isn't "up to" any cost in coins alone.
- Monastery is worth more than its rating because the board has many gains: doubtful. Most turns have one gain with no +Buy, and the commentators said Monastery trashed almost nothing when Minionpawn opened it.
- Fortune as a midgame add-on doubling $12 into three Provinces: plausible but slow. The five Gladiators have to leave the pile first, and Fortune costs a turn of Debt; the analysis rightly makes it optional.
- Transmute is dominated by Remodel (Peddler to Duchy vs Peddler to Province): plausible from card text.
- Enchantress's attack lands on the opener because no opener is a village: plausible. The first Action played becomes +1 Card +1 Action.
- Way of the Squirrel's cards come after the Clean-up draw, giving 7-card hands: plausible. "At the end of this turn" happens after Clean-up.

## Summary
The analysis has the expert plan: Grotto and Way of the Squirrel for next-turn draw, Farmhands as the village, Peddler as payload, Foray Loots for +Buy, and Remodel turning $8 Peddlers into Provinces. Its 4/3 line is almost exactly VRSmith's. It misses that Grotto makes early greening cheap and only half-states the multiple-Remodel ending. It also leans on Enchantress's attack and on Monastery more than the experts did, though the winner did buy Enchantress.
