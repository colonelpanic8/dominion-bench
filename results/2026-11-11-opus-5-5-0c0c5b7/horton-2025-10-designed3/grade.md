## Beyond the key
- Hill Fort's Action-phase gain doesn't stop Exploration from firing: plausible. Exploration checks only gains "during" the Buy phase.
- Hill Fort + Reckless Guide is "a Laboratory with a spare Action": wrong, though minor. Hill Fort uses an Action and Guide spends one and gives two, so the pair nets +2 cards for the Hill Fort and no extra Actions: exactly a Laboratory.
- A called Reckless Guide returns to its pile, so the Guide pile never empties: plausible. Calling puts it into play, and Reckless returns it when discarded from play.
- Hill Fort gaining Ducat to hand trashes a Copper: plausible. Ducat's on-gain trash works with the gained-to-hand timing.
- Cabin Boy can trash itself for a Stronghold when Stronghold is the top Fort: plausible. Stronghold is a Duration and Cabin Boy has no cost cap; split-pile rules allow only the top card.
- Tent topdecks itself and two plays rotate Tent → Garrison → Hill Fort: plausible. Rotation moves all copies of the top card to the bottom.
- "Ducat plus Destrier costs $7 for both": doubtful. The cost math holds (Destrier $6 − 1 gain), but buying both needs a second Buy, which only a Ducat played that turn provides; a newly bought Ducat gives none.
- Rotating Hill Fort to the bottom after buying yours denies the opponent: plausible. It's a real tactic, though Tent plays can bring it back.
- Buying the Exploration Project still triggers Exploration that turn: plausible. A Project isn't a card gain.

## Summary
The analysis matches the designer's main line: Tent/Exploration opening, the Forts pile as the engine, a contested Hill Fort race, Reckless Guides for draw, and Destrier made cheap by gains. Its main miss is Garrison: Horton's path takes two Garrisons early, and this line rotates past them to rush Hill Fort. It also has a small arithmetic slip on Hill Fort + Guide.
