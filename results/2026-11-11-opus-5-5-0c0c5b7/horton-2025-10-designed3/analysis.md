# Kingdom analysis: Forts, Destrier, Ducat, Steward, Cabin Boy, Guide (Reckless), Wishing Well, Ratcatcher, Harbinger, Mystic, plus the Exploration project

**Board:** The board turns on gaining cards in the Action phase. Hill Fort can gain a card costing up to $4 straight into your hand. That gain doesn't count for Exploration, which only checks the Buy phase. So a deck that gains with Hill Fort and then buys nothing still grows every turn, and also banks +1 Coffers and +1 Villager. Guide has the Reckless trait, so it gives +2 Cards +2 Actions when played. Ducat provides +Buy and Coffers, and Steward and Ratcatcher do the trashing. There are no attacks and nothing else of note at $6+. Two things are open:

- **The money is thin.** The Treasures here are Copper, Silver and Ducat, and the other sources are Tent, Garrison, Steward, Cabin Boy, Mystic and Stronghold at $2–3 each.
- **Hill Forts are hard to reach.** There are only 4, and they sit third in the Forts pile.

**Key interactions:**
- **Hill Fort + Reckless Guide:** Hill Fort puts a Guide into your hand, and Guide gives +2 Cards +2 Actions. Net, the pair is a Laboratory with a spare Action, every turn, from about turn 5. Guide goes to the Tavern mat. Call it later to redraw a bad hand, and then Reckless sends it back to its pile, so the Guide pile never runs out.
- **Hill Fort + Exploration:** gain in the Action phase, skip the buy, and get +1 Coffers +1 Villager. The Villagers let you play Hill Fort, Steward, Tent and Garrison, which are all terminal. The Coffers pay for one big buying turn later.
- **Hill Fort + Cabin Boy or Silver to hand:**
  - Cabin Boy is a cantrip that gives +$2 next turn, so it's payload that doesn't use up an Action.
  - Silver to hand is +$2 right now.
  - Late in the game, a Cabin Boy can trash itself to gain a Stronghold at any cost (2 VP, and +3 Cards next turn), if Stronghold is the top Fort.
- **Hill Fort + Ducat to hand:** the gain trashes a Copper from your hand. Ducat then gives +1 Buy and +1 Coffers when played, and playing it doesn't count as a gain, so Exploration still fires.
- **Tent rotation:** Tent puts itself back on your deck, so you play it every turn. Each play can rotate the Forts once. Two plays take the top from Tent to Garrison to Hill Fort, so an opening Tent can get you a Hill Fort on turn 4.
- **Gains make Destrier cheaper:** each card gained this turn knocks $1 off. A Hill Fort gain, a Cabin Boy gain at the start of the turn, or a Ducat bought alongside it all count. Ducat plus Destrier costs $7 for both. After 2 gains, Destrier costs $4, so a third Hill Fort can gain it into your hand.

**Other cards:**
- **Steward:** Villagers make its +2 Cards usable. Its trash-2 turns are also turns with no buy, which feed Exploration.
- **Ratcatcher:** cheap thinning that doesn't use an Action. Pick it up with spare Ducat Buys.
- **Garrison:** take it to hand with Hill Fort on a big turn. Each later gain (Ducats, Provinces) adds a card to next turn's hand.
- **Mystic:** +$2 that doesn't use an Action. It's acceptable late payload, but at $5 it competes with Hill Fort.
- **Harbinger:** none worth it. It can put a Destrier or Cabin Boy back on your deck, which is marginal.
- **Wishing Well:** none. Its only partner is the Tent you put back on your deck, and you'd draw that anyway.

**Ratings check:** Steward (3.0) and Destrier (2.6) match my view. Exploration (−1.4), Guide (−1.8), Reckless (−1.1), Cabin Boy (−0.2) and Ducat (−1.3) are all rated well below what they're worth here, and Hill Fort is the reason for every one. Mystic (−4.0) and Harbinger (−3.9) agree with my view that they're marginal.

### Build lines (paper, average draws, going first)

**Engine (Exploration, Tent, Hill Fort):**
- **T1–2:** Exploration ($4) and Tent ($3).
- **T3:** Tent and 3 Coppers make $5. Rotate the Forts so Garrison is on top. Buy Steward.
- **T4:** Tent is back on top of the deck. Rotate again so Hill Fort is on top, and buy Hill Fort with $5.
- **T5:**
  - Hill Fort gains a Guide into hand, which draws into Steward.
  - Steward trashes an Estate and a Copper.
  - Tent gives $2.
  - Buy nothing, for +1 Coffers +1 Villager.
- **T6:** Buy Hill Fort #2, using a Coffer if needed. The first Hill Fort gains a Ducat into hand, trashing a Copper.
- **T7–8:**
  - Each Hill Fort gains a Guide (for draw) or a Cabin Boy (for payload) into hand.
  - Buy Ducat plus Destrier for $7, or skip buying for Exploration.
  - Add a Ratcatcher on a spare Buy.
- **About T9 deck:**
  - Engine pieces: Tent, Steward, 3 Hill Forts, 1–2 Destriers.
  - Payload and Buys: 3 Cabin Boys, 2–3 Ducats.
  - Junk left: 3–4 Coppers and an Estate.
  - Bank: about 6 Coffers and spare Villagers.
- **Built turn (about T10–11):** draw the whole deck. The money adds up to about $14:
  - Tent: $2
  - Cabin Boys: $6
  - Coppers: $4
  - The last Hill Fort gains a Silver into hand: $2

  Add Coffers to reach $16–20. With 3 or more Buys, that's 2 Provinces, or 2 Provinces and a Duchy. Repeat each turn, banking on the weaker turns.
- **Late:** Cabin Boys turn into Strongholds once Stronghold is the top Fort.

**Money (Steward or Tent plus money, with a Ducat):**
- **T1–2:** Silver and Steward.
- **About T5–6:** Gold.
- **About T7–8:** first Province.
- **About T15–17:** 4 Provinces. Nothing on this board lifts money past one Province per turn.

| Plan | Starts scoring | Can end by | Peak VP/turn | VP ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hill Fort engine | ~T10 | ~T13–14 | 2 Provinces (+ Duchy) | Provinces + Duchies + 8 VP from Strongholds |
| Steward/Tent money | ~T7 | ~T20+ (with no help) | 1 Province | about half the Provinces before the engine takes over |

**Plans considered:**
- **Hill Fort engine with Exploration:** the line above. It needs 2–3 of the 4 Hill Forts. Its weakness is that the Hill Fort pile is contested, and the opponent's Tent can rotate Hill Fort off the top.
- **Engine without Hill Forts (Destrier, Steward, Villagers, bought Guides):** this is the backup if you get shut out of Hill Forts. It grows more slowly, because every piece has to be bought, and buying cancels Exploration.
- **Steward or Tent money with Ducat:** the floor. 4 Provinces by about turn 16.

**Against their best plan:** no attacks are on the board, so the engine just has to win the race. Against money it scores 2 Provinces a turn from about turn 11 and is ahead by turn 12–13. Money cannot end the game before turn 18 or so, and no pile empties early: the Guides keep returning to their pile and the Forts pile holds 16 cards.

In an engine mirror, the Forts pile decides the game:
- Count the Hill Forts. Getting 2 of the 4 is the minimum.
- If you go second, think about rotating Hill Fort to the bottom after you buy yours, to deny the opponent.
- Green earlier than you would against money.

**Advocate's case (for money):** open Silver and Steward, Gold by turn 5, Provinces from turn 7. Money peaks at about one Province every other turn. It has about 3 Provinces when the engine's first double turn arrives (about turn 10–11). The engine pulls level around turn 12 and ends the game by turn 14, with money at about 4 Provinces. Money falls behind at turn 12, by a Province or more, and keeps falling behind. Money only wins if the engine is shut out of Hill Forts completely.

**Recommendation:** build the Hill Fort, Guide and Exploration engine:
- Use Steward, Ducats and Ratcatcher to thin.
- Use Cabin Boys and Silvers gained into hand, plus banked Coffers, as payload.
- Use Ducats for +Buy.

The board has real draw (Hill Fort with Guide, Destrier), real Actions (Villagers, Guide), and payload beyond one Province a turn (Coffers with Ducat Buys, and Strongholds). Money doesn't threaten to end the game early.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Exploration and Tent. Tent sets up a Hill Fort on turn 4.
- **5/2:** Exploration and Ratcatcher (or Ducat). Nothing at $5 is worth it this early, so arrange your starting deck for 4/3.

**Buy priorities:**
- **Shuffles 1–2:** Hill Fort as soon as it is the top Fort, then Steward and a second Hill Fort.
- **Shuffle 3:**
  - Ducat plus Destrier for $7, and Ratcatcher on spare Buys.
  - Hill Fort gains go to Guide when you need draw, Cabin Boy or Silver when you need money.
  - Skip buying whenever the turn's buy would add little.
- **Greening:** from about turn 10–11, once the deck reliably makes $16 or more with 2 or more Buys. Spend your Coffers on that turn. Later, turn Cabin Boys into Strongholds.

**Ending:** most likely the Provinces run out around turn 13–15. A three-pile ending is unlikely: Guides return to their pile and the Forts pile has 16 cards. Ducat (10 cards) is the only pile that could empty.

**Watch out for:**
- **Rotation wars:** the opponent's Tent can move Hill Fort to the bottom. If that happens, switch to the Destrier and Villager version, and buy out Tents and Garrisons with Ducat Buys to bring Hill Fort back up.
- **Trashing too hard:** removing too many Coppers before Cabin Boys and Silvers replace them leaves you short of money.
- **Missed reshuffles:** Durations (Cabin Boy, Stronghold) bought at the end of a shuffle can miss the next one.

**Least sure of:** how fast the payload comes in. If the built turn makes only about $12, the first double turn slips to turn 12–13, which is still ahead of money but closer. To test it, play the line out with Hill Forts split 2–2 between the players.

**Assumed:**
- Calling a Reckless Guide is not "playing" it, so the call happens once. After the call, Guide is discarded from play and returns to its pile.
- Buying a Project isn't gaining a card, so Exploration fires on the turn you buy it.
- Rotating the Forts moves every remaining copy of the top card to the bottom of the pile.
- Destrier's discount counts gains made at the start of the turn, such as Cabin Boy's.
- Cabin Boy's "gain a Duration card" has no cost limit, but it can only gain the top card of the Forts pile.
- With 3 or more players, the Hill Fort split gets worse and rotation becomes harder to control.
