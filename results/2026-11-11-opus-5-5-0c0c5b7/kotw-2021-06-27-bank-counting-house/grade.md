## Beyond the key
- Talisman multiplies Copper buys, since Copper is a $0 non-Victory card: plausible. Talisman's text covers any non-Victory card costing $4 or less.
- Talisman bought with k Talismans in play gains k+1: plausible. Talisman costs $4 and isn't a Victory card. Several Talismans in play each trigger.
- 46 Coppers in the Supply for 2 players: plausible. 60 minus 7 per player.
- Three-pile Tower ending on Copper, Talisman and Raze: plausible by card text, but slow next to the key's Province finish.
- Raze hurts this plan (trashing a Copper costs a Tower VP and looks at 0 cards): plausible. Raze looks at one card per $1 of the trashed card's cost.
- Counting House is useless on T3–4 because the discard is empty right after the shuffle: plausible. The turn-2 cleanup shuffles the whole deck.
- Haunted Woods is weak against a Copper deck that holds few cards after buying: plausible. It only topdecks the cards left in hand when you gain something you bought.
- Jester against a Copper deck usually flips a Copper, which you take: plausible by text. It misses that milling the deck threatens a forced reshuffle, which is the key's concern.
- Duchies are never better than Copper buys with 2+ Talismans in play: doubtful. Coppers are only worth VP if the Copper pile empties, and in a split pile they are worth less than a sure 3 VP.
- Talisman doesn't trigger on Events: plausible. Buying an Event isn't buying a card.

## Summary
The analysis finds the right ingredients (Counting House, Travelling Fair, Bank, Coppers, Tower, Horse Traders) and even names the combo. It misreads the engine as a slow Talisman-driven Copper flood that scores mostly through Tower. It never sets up the every-turn, no-reshuffle Travelling Fair/Counting House loop, so its clock runs at T15–17 instead of T8–9 and it never plans the multi-Bank Province finish.
