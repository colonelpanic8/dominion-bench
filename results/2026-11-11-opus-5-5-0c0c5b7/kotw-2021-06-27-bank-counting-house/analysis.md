# Kingdom analysis: Bank, Counting House, Enchantress, Farming Village, Haunted Woods, Horse Traders, Jester, Raze, Settlers/Bustling Village, Talisman · Travelling Fair, Tower

**Short answer:** fill your deck with Coppers. Talisman doubles each Copper you buy, Travelling Fair and Horse Traders give the Buys, and Counting House plus Bank turn the Coppers into Provinces. When the Copper pile runs out, Tower makes every Copper you own worth 1 VP.

## Inventory
- **$2:** Raze, Settlers (split pile with Bustling Village at $5), Travelling Fair (Event).
- **$3:** Enchantress.
- **$4:** Farming Village, Horse Traders, Talisman.
- **$5:** Counting House, Haunted Woods, Jester, Bustling Village.
- **$7:** Bank.
- **Landmark:** Tower.
- **Setup:** no Colonies and no Shelters. The starting deck is 7 Copper and 3 Estate, so the opening splits are 4/3 or 5/2.
- **First-edition-only cards:** Counting House, Talisman, Horse Traders and Farming Village.
- **Durations:** Enchantress and Haunted Woods.
- **Copper pile:** 60 minus the 14 in starting decks, so **46 Coppers** are in the Supply.

---

**Board:** Tower changes what Copper is worth. Copper is a $0 Supply pile with 46 cards. If it empties, every Copper you own, the starting ones included, scores 1 VP. The board also supplies everything a Copper deck needs:
- Talisman gains copies of cheap cards you buy, and Copper costs $0.
- Travelling Fair and Horse Traders give +Buy.
- Counting House puts every Copper in your discard pile into your hand.
- Bank pays $1 for each Treasure in play.
- Settlers pulls a Copper from the discard, and Bustling Village pulls a Settlers.

What's open: how fast the Copper pile drains in a mirror, and how much Enchantress hurts the one big terminal (Counting House).

**Key interactions:**
- **Talisman + any Buy + Copper (counted by Tower).** Each Buy spent on Copper gains (Talismans in play + 1) Coppers. With 2 Talismans in play and 3 Buys, that's 9 Coppers, worth about 9 VP once the pile is gone, for $0–2. The rate is about 3–6 Coppers per turn from around turn 6, and 6–10 later.
- **Talisman + Talisman.** Buying a Talisman with k Talismans in play gains k+1 of them. The 10-card pile empties fast, and the Talismans count for Tower too. Splitting this pile with the opponent decides how fast each of you gains Coppers.
- **Coppers you just bought + Counting House + Bank.** Coppers bought this turn sit in your discard pile, so the next Counting House pulls them all. A typical hand: Counting House pulls 10 Coppers, plus Bank, Talisman and one Copper in hand. That's 13 Treasures in play: $12, plus $13 from Bank, for **$25**. Buy Travelling Fair and you can take two Provinces with spare money. This is the Counting House + Travelling Fair combo listed in `combos.md`, with Tower on top.
- **Travelling Fair's top-decking.** Leave Coppers in the discard pile for Counting House. Put a newly gained Counting House or Bank on top of your deck so it's in your next hand.
- **Tower's third pile.** The game ends when three piles are empty: Copper, Talisman, and one cheap pile that Talisman multiplies. Raze ($2) is the easiest third pile, and Enchantress is next. Every card you own from those piles is worth 1 VP.

**Other cards:**
- **Settlers:** Talisman gives two per buy. In this deck it's a cantrip that also pulls a Copper, so effectively +$1. Lead with it to absorb an Enchantress. Good.
- **Bustling Village:** returns a Settlers. It's a village if your deck needs one, but low priority.
- **Farming Village:** in a Copper-heavy deck it's +2 Actions and draws a Treasure. It lets you play Counting House, Horse Traders and Haunted Woods in the same turn. One or two copies.
- **Horse Traders:** your main +Buy, and Talisman gives two per buy. It also reacts to Enchantress, Jester and Haunted Woods. Discarding 2 cards costs little when your hand is full of Coppers.
- **Enchantress:** the best attack here. The opponent's first Action is often their Counting House, which turns into a cantrip. Talisman gives two per buy, and it draws +2 at the start of your next turn. Opponents counter by playing Settlers or Farming Village first.
- **Jester:** +$2. Against a Copper deck it usually flips a Copper, which you take for yourself, so it moves a Tower point to you. If it flips a Talisman you take that too, and a Victory card gives them a Curse. Decent, but it's terminal.
- **Haunted Woods:** +3 cards at the start of your next turn is useful. The attack is weak against Copper decks, which rarely hold cards after buying. Against money decks it's real, because they put their Provinces back on top. One copy at most.
- **Raze:** works against this plan. Trashing a Copper looks at 0 cards and costs Tower VP, and trashing an Estate loses 1 VP. Its only use is as the cheap third pile at the end.

**Ratings check:** Tower 0.69, Raze 2.0, Haunted Woods 1.17, Enchantress 0.75, Travelling Fair 0.45. The cards in my plan are all rated low: Bank −1.6, Talisman −1.5, Settlers −2.4, Horse Traders −2.25 and Counting House −3.1. Talisman, Horse Traders and Counting House are first-edition ratings with high uncertainty (phi 1.0). The gap is expected: these cards are weak on an average board, but Tower makes Copper a VP pile, which changes what each of them does. Raze's high rating comes from trashing, which hurts you here.

### Plans considered
1. **Copper flood (Talisman / Travelling Fair / Horse Traders, then Counting House / Bank, scoring with Tower).**
   - Line: Talismans multiply by turn 5, Coppers start flowing by turn 6, the first $20+ Counting House turn comes around turn 11–12, and after that each Counting House turn takes two Provinces.
   - Needs: 5+ Talismans, 2–3 Horse Traders, 1–2 Counting Houses, 1–2 Banks, Travelling Fair whenever you have $2 spare.
   - Weakness: Enchantress hitting Counting House; losing the Talisman split.
2. **Village / draw engine (Farming Village or Bustling Village with Haunted Woods and Enchantress draw, Horse Traders as payload).** All the draw is terminal Durations, at most +3, and there's no real trashing. It never draws its deck reliably. Rejected. Counting House drawing Coppers is this board's real draw.
3. **Big Money + Haunted Woods (or Jester), with Bank as Gold-plus.**
   - Line: $6 by turn 3–4, Provinces from about turn 7–8, 4 Provinces by about turn 15.
   - Weakness: its only VP is the Province pile, while the flood player drains the Coppers alone and takes 30+ Tower VP.

### Build lines
- **Copper flood** (solo, average draws, opening 4/3 with Talisman and Silver):
  - T3: Talisman + 3 Copper = $4. Buy Talisman, gain a copy (3 Talismans).
  - T4: Silver + 3 Copper = $5. Buy Horse Traders.
  - Deck now 15 cards. T5: 2 Talismans + 2 Copper = $4. Buy Talisman, gain 3 in all (6 Talismans).
  - T6: Horse Traders (discard 2 Coppers), Talisman, Silver = $6 and 2 Buys. Buy Travelling Fair (3 Buys left, $4), then Horse Traders (gain 2), then 2 Coppers (gain 4).
  - T7: weak hand. Buy Silver or Enchantress.
  - T8: Horse Traders, 2 Talismans = $5 and 2 Buys. Buy Counting House, then 1 Copper (gain 3).
  - T9: Travelling Fair, then Settlers (gain 3), then Copper (gain 3).
  - T10–11: more Talismans and Horse Traders; buy Bank once you hit $7.
  - **T12:** Counting House pulls about 15–18 Coppers. With Talisman and Bank that's about $30. Travelling Fair, then **2 Provinces**, plus Coppers with the leftover Buy.
  - Built turn, turn 13 on: a Counting House turn makes $25–35 and 3–4 Buys. That's 2 Provinces plus 3–6 Coppers, about 15–18 VP counting Tower. A turn without Counting House buys 4–9 Coppers.
  - Solo, about 35 of the 46 Coppers are yours by turn 15–16, and Talisman, then Raze, empty after them.
- **Big Money + Haunted Woods:** standard money line, with 4–5 Provinces by turn 15–16.

**Against their best plan:**
- **Flood vs Big Money + Haunted Woods:** suppose money takes 5 Provinces by turn 16, worth about 30 VP plus Estates (and 7 Tower VP if the pile empties). The flood has about 35 Coppers, about 7 Talismans and a few Razes, which is 45+ VP from Tower, plus 2–3 Provinces. The flood wins by a lot. Haunted Woods barely touches it.
- **Flood mirror (the real matchup):**
  - Contest the Talisman pile early. Each Talisman bought with Talismans in play gains extras.
  - Jester moves Coppers to your side.
  - Enchantress taxes their Counting House, so keep Settlers or Farming Village to lead with.
  - The game moves faster, probably ending on three piles around turn 13–14.
- **Engine:** it loses to the flood the same way money does, only more slowly.

**Advocate's case** (a Province race with Big Money + Haunted Woods or Jester):
- It wants to end the game on Provinces before the Copper pile empties, and it never helps drain Coppers.
- But the flood player drains the pile alone. By my line it's about 35 Coppers by turn 15–16, and in a mirror both players drain it.
- By turn 15, money has about 4–5 Provinces, roughly 30 VP. The flood has about 20 VP showing (Provinces and Estates) plus about 35 Coppers in hand that become 35 VP when the pile empties. With the Copper pile at about 11 left, the flood can finish it in 1–2 turns.
- So money falls behind for good around turn 12. That's the flood's first double-Province turn, which matches money's Province count while the flood holds about 25 Coppers in reserve.
- Money's only escape is buying all 8 Provinces before Coppers + Talisman + Raze run out. Plain money needs about 21–23 turns for that, and the flood is taking Provinces too.

**Recommendation:** **Copper flood.**
- Talisman with Travelling Fair and Horse Traders for gains.
- Counting House and Bank for Provinces.
- Tower for scoring, ending on three piles: Copper, Talisman, then Raze or Enchantress.

The best reply is the same plan, which makes the game a split of the Talisman and Copper piles. Enchantress and Jester are the tools for winning that split.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Talisman / Silver. Enchantress instead of Silver if you go first.
- **5/2:** Talisman / Settlers. Haunted Woods / Settlers is an acceptable alternative. Counting House is useless on turns 3–4, because your discard pile is empty right after the shuffle.

**Buy priorities:**
- **Shuffles 2–3:**
  - Talisman whenever you have $4 and a Talisman in play.
  - Horse Traders up to 2–3.
  - Spare $2 → Travelling Fair.
  - Spend spare Buys on Copper.
  - One Counting House by about turn 8, and 1–2 Enchantress (Talisman doubles them).
- **Then:** Bank at $7, a second Counting House, and Farming Village if your terminals are colliding.
- **Greening:**
  - Coppers count as VP from the start, as long as the pile empties.
  - Provinces on Counting House turns from about turn 11–12.
  - Duchies are never better than a Copper buy while you have 2+ Talismans in play.

**Ending:**
- The usual end is three piles: Copper (46), Talisman (10), then Raze, Enchantress or Settlers/Bustling Village (the split pile is slower because Bustling Village isn't doubled).
- Whoever leads on Tower count drains the piles.
- Track the Copper split every turn. If you're behind on Coppers, stop draining the pile and take Provinces instead.

**Watch out for:**
- Enchantress on your Counting House. Lead with Settlers or Farming Village, and Horse Traders can react.
- Losing the Talisman split.
- An opponent who ends the game on three piles while you're behind on Tower count.

**Least sure of:** how fast the flood converts to Provinces before about turn 12. It depends on a Counting House turn finding 15+ Coppers. The test is a sharper paper line with variance. Even if Provinces come late, the Tower VP from Coppers probably still carries the plan.

**How the pick loses:**
- In a mirror: losing the Talisman and Copper split, or having your Counting House turns hit by Enchantress every time.
- Against money: only if the Copper pile somehow doesn't empty. That can't happen if you keep buying Coppers.

The opponent sees your Talisman opening right away and can mirror it, so expect a contest over the Talisman pile.
