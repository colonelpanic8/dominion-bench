## Beyond the key
- Ferry on Old Witch makes Witch $3 on your turns, so a 4/3 opens Ferry then a turn-2 Old Witch: plausible. Ferry's -$2 applies to an Action Supply pile on your turns; Old Witch $5 → $3. Horton doesn't discuss Old Witch at all, so this centering is a real divergence rather than a key-confirmed idea.
- Hermit can gain a Ferry'd Old Witch: plausible. Hermit gains "costing up to $3" on your turn, when the token makes Witch cost $3.
- Trashing a Curse from hand on the opponent's Old Witch triggers Sewers: plausible. Sewers fires "when you trash a card", on any turn.
- Madman played from a 5-card hand draws to 8: plausible. 4 cards left in hand, +1 Card per card in hand.
- Late re-Ferry onto Bridge lets Hermit gain Bridges at $2: plausible. Bridge $4 → $2 on your turns, within Hermit's $3 gain.
- Rogue is "weak" because the trash will rarely be empty: plausible. Rogue only attacks if no $3–$6 card is in the trash, and trashed Hermits ($3) fill it; still, that gain is the R5 use, not a weakness.
- Doubts that Sewers can trash from hand when Hermit is trashed in Clean-up: doubtful. Clean-up discards may be ordered, so Hermit can leave play while the hand is still held; the designer's opening uses exactly this.
- A three-pile ending of Curses + Old Witches + Duchies is realistic: doubtful. It also advises at most 3–4 Witches each, which leaves the 10-card Witch pile short of empty.

## Summary
The analysis spots most of the board's pieces (Hermit/Sewers thinning, Diplomat's reaction and Outpost village trick, Rogue re-gaining Hermits, the Madman/Bridge burst) but builds around a Ferry/Old Witch Curse war the designer never discusses. Bridge is demoted to a cash-out, Diplomat is a minor buy, and the Hermit self-trash Sewers trigger is set aside. It avoids the pure megaturn trap, but a player following it would play a different deck from Horton's Diplomat/Bridge engine.
