## Beyond the key
- A Curse revealed by Jester isn't a Victory card, so Jester hands out a copy of it: plausible. Jester gives a Curse only for a Victory card, and otherwise a copy of the discarded card.
- A Tunnel discarded by Jester gives its owner a Gold and a Curse: plausible. It is discarded outside Clean-up, so Tunnel can trigger, and it is a Victory card, so Jester gives a Curse.
- Litter played as Squirrel takes no Debt: plausible. A Way replaces the card's instructions, and +1 Debt is one of them.
- Market Squares in hand when Cage trashes itself each gain a Gold: plausible. Cage is trashed when you gain a Victory card, which triggers Market Square's reaction from hand. It's niche, though.
- Seaway is worth $5 only late: doubtful. Both finalists bought it for Market Square, putting a second +Buy on their densest cheap-Action pile in a game decided by gaining Actions and Vineyards.
- The cheap-Action Vineyard plan can't afford to three-pile before ~T17: doubtful. The actual game three-piled Vineyard, Border Guard and Market Square on turn 14, with both decks carrying Litter and Jester for money.
- Astrolabe adds nothing to Vineyard counts: plausible. It is a Treasure, not an Action.
- Seaway's +Buy token makes Border Guard a cheap Market: plausible from card text.

## Summary
The analysis gets the key's cards right: Litter as the core, Jester as payload, Patrol over Council Room, and Vineyards fed by non-terminal Actions. But it builds a slower Province + Vineyard deck that expects to end on Provinces around T16–18. It treats the Vineyard/cheap-Action three-pile race that decided the game as a secondary threat, and it undervalues Cage on Estates and Seaway. The key is C-quality and partly read from the result, and the commentators themselves expected a longer game, so the gap is partly defensible.
