**Note:** Mandarin and Noble Brigand are first-edition-only Hinterlands cards. Card text is quoted from the kingdom file.

### Inventory

- **Piles:** Beggar $2, Coin of the Realm $2, Encampment/Plunder $2/$5 (split pile: 5 Encampments on top of 5 Plunders), Scrying Pool $2+Potion, Noble Brigand $4, Trader $4, Mandarin $5, Scholar $5, Hunting Grounds $6, Forge $7.
- **Setup:** no landscapes, no Colonies, no Shelters. The Potion pile is in play.
- **Special mechanics:** Reserve (Coin of the Realm on the Tavern mat), VP tokens (Plunder only).
- **+Buy:** none anywhere on the board.
- **Price points:** crowded at $2, thin at $3–5 (Mandarin and Scholar are weak), and the key cards sit at $6 and $7.
- **Starting deck:** 7 Copper and 3 Estate, so turns 1–2 split 4/3 or 5/2.

**Board:** There is no +Buy, so the only way to score more than one Province per turn is to gain cards. The board's gainer is **Forge**, and its best fuel is **Hunting Grounds**:
- Hunting Grounds costs $6. Trash it with any $2 card and Forge gains a card costing exactly $8, which is a Province. Hunting Grounds' own trash trigger adds a Duchy.
- Draw: Hunting Grounds and Scholar.
- Actions: Coin of the Realm, plus Encampment when you reveal a Gold or Plunder.
- Trashing: Trader, and Forge itself.
- No junker, so the attacks are mild.

What's still open: how many Forges and spare Hunting Grounds you can afford with one gain per turn, and whether Scrying Pool earns its Potion.

**Key interactions:**
- **Forge + Hunting Grounds + any $2 card.** Forge trashes Hunting Grounds ($6) plus an Estate, Encampment, Coin of the Realm or Beggar ($2), for $8 in total. You gain a Province, and Hunting Grounds' trigger gains a Duchy: **9 VP from one Action**, and you still get your buy. From about turn 9–11.
- **Forge + a lone Hunting Grounds.** Trashing it alone ($6) gains a new Hunting Grounds plus a Duchy. That's +3 VP and your deck loses nothing, so you can repeat it every turn. Hunting Grounds + 2 Coppers also costs $6, so it gains a Hunting Grounds and a Duchy and thins two Coppers.
- **Forge on Treasure.** Gold + Estate, or Silver + Silver + Estate, adds up to $8 and gains a Province. Three Estates or two Silvers add up to $6 and gain a Hunting Grounds or Gold. Early on, Estate + 2–4 Coppers ($2) thins your deck and gains a Coin of the Realm or Encampment, so Forge is also a trasher that brings in a village.
- **Trader + Hunting Grounds.** Trashing Hunting Grounds gains 6 Silvers plus a Duchy, a late-game cash-out. Trader on an Estate gains 2 Silvers, which is the opener's job. If Trader is in your hand, its Reaction can swap each Estate from a Hunting Grounds trigger, or a Copper from Beggar or Noble Brigand, for a Silver.
- **Encampment as fodder.** An Encampment that fails to stay is returned at the start of Clean-up anyway, so trashing it to Forge first costs nothing.

**Other cards:**
- **Coin of the Realm:** played as a Treasure it goes on the Tavern mat. Call it to play Hunting Grounds and then Forge in the same turn. It's also $2 fodder while it's in your hand.
- **Encampment:** best with Gold, which keeps it in your deck. Otherwise it's a one-shot +2 Cards, +2 Actions that you can then Forge.
- **Plunder:** only available once the Encampments are gone, and they keep returning to the pile. Rarely matters.
- **Mandarin:** +$3, and it can put Hunting Grounds or Forge on top of your deck to pair them next turn. A fine 5/2 opener, otherwise minor.
- **Scholar:** only with Coin of the Realm. Inferior to Hunting Grounds.
- **Scrying Pool:** wants a deck of mostly Actions. Its +1 Action doesn't make it a village, and every Treasure or Victory card stops its reveal. Weak with Coppers and gained green cards.
- **Noble Brigand:** steals the opponent's Silvers and Golds. It's a mild junker against a deck heavy in Actions. Weak.
- **Beggar:** its Reaction gains 2 Silvers against an opponent who plays Scrying Pool or Noble Brigand every turn. Also $2 fodder.

**Ratings check:** Forge (−0.8), Hunting Grounds (+0.6) and Trader (−4.0) are rated below average on an average board. Here they rate far higher because they combine: each makes the others' payload. Scrying Pool (+3.05) and Encampment (+2.8) are rated far higher than their value here, because this board has no +Buy and no Action-dense trashing.

### Plans considered
1. **Forge/Hunting Grounds deck (the pick).**
   - How it works: open Trader for economy. Get 2–3 Hunting Grounds, 1–2 Forges, a Gold or two and 1–2 Coin of the Realm.
   - Every hand with both Forge and a spare Hunting Grounds turns into Province + Duchy, and you still buy.
   - Weakness: one Buy per turn caps the build rate.
2. **Hunting Grounds Big Money (the floor):** 4 Provinces around turn 14–15, about 6 VP per turn, and no way to score faster.
3. **Scrying Pool / Coin of the Realm engine (highest stated ceiling):** 3 Forges plus 3 spare Hunting Grounds per turn would be about 33 VP per turn. It needs Potion, several Scrying Pools, lots of trashing and about 12 pieces, all with one Buy per turn.

**Against their best plan:**
- **Pick vs. Hunting Grounds BM:** from about turn 11 the pick's Forge turns score 9–15 VP where BM scores 6. The pick wins the Duchies and the Province split.
- **Mirror:** the Duchy split and the second Forge decide it. Green Duchies with Forge early.
- **Noble Brigand:** it steals your Golds. Keep a Beggar in hand only if the opponent plays it every turn.
- **Runner-up (Scrying Pool) vs. the pick:** it's two turns slower to the same payload, and it loses unless it reaches 3 Forges.

**Advocate's case:**
- *The case:* Scrying Pool is the board's top-rated card, and one draws your whole Action chain. With Coin of the Realm on the mat (so it doesn't stop reveals) and Forge thinning, the deck could play 3 Forges per turn. That's 3 Provinces + 3 Duchies + a bought Province, about 33 VP per turn, and two such turns end the game.
- *Where it falls behind:* the limit on output is Forge copies and the single Buy, not draw. On turns 3–7 the advocate's gains go to Potion and Scrying Pools instead of Silver and Hunting Grounds. It has Hunting Grounds and Forge around turn 9–10, against turn 6–8 for the pick.
- *Turn 12:* the pick has about 1 Province + 1–2 Duchies (roughly 12 VP more). The advocate has only just had its first Forge turn.
- *Endgame:* every Forge turn adds a Province and a Duchy to the deck, and each one stops a Scrying Pool reveal. The 3-Forge turn needs a thin, all-Action deck that keeps losing its thinness, so it rarely arrives before the Provinces run out around turn 15–16.
- *Verdict:* the pick stands.

**How the pick loses:** the opponent gets 2 Forges first and wins the Duchy split. Or Noble Brigand repeatedly takes your Golds, which also strands your Encampments. You'll see both coming: a $7 opener-follow-up, or a Noble Brigand buy.

**Recommendation:** build the Forge/Hunting Grounds deck.
- Hunting Grounds is both your draw and your fuel: play it when Forge isn't in hand, and trash it when Forge is.
- It beats Hunting Grounds Big Money on the same opening once Forge arrives, because each Forge turn is worth about 1.5 Provinces.
- It beats the Scrying Pool engine because that engine adds draw this board doesn't need and nothing toward the cap that matters here (one Buy, Forge copies).

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Trader / Silver.
- **5/2:** Trader / Coin of the Realm. Alternative: Mandarin / Coin of the Realm if you'd rather have money than trashing. There's nothing good at $5.

**Buy priorities:**
- **$7:** Forge (first copy as soon as you have a Hunting Grounds, a second one later). Otherwise Gold.
- **$6:** Hunting Grounds (2–3 copies), then Gold.
- **$8+:** Province once you have a Forge. Your first $8+ before that should be Forge.
- **$3–5:** Silver.
- **$2:** Coin of the Realm (up to 2). Late on, Encampment as Forge fodder.
- **Greening:** Forge greens from its first use. Buy Provinces at $8 from about turn 9–10. Take Duchies at $5–7, or from Forge, once 4 or fewer Provinces are left. Trader on a Hunting Grounds (Duchy + 6 Silvers) is the late cash-out.

**Ending:**
- The Provinces usually run out around turn 15–17.
- The Duchies drain fast (Forge, Trader and Hunting Grounds all gain them). They can empty, so count the split.
- A three-pile ending on Duchies, Estates and Hunting Grounds is possible but unlikely. Watch it if both players take "3 Estates" from Hunting Grounds.

**Watch out for:**
- An opponent racing for Forges: contest the second Forge.
- Noble Brigand taking your Golds: favour Silver plus Forge on Treasure, and keep a Beggar.
- With 3+ players: 12 Provinces, so Duchies matter more, and the Scrying Pool and Noble Brigand attacks gain value.

**Least sure of:** that Scrying Pool should be skipped. If the opponent's Scrying Pool deck reaches 2–3 Forges with full draw by turn 12, it outscores the pick. Test: play both lines out to turn 14 with real shuffles.
