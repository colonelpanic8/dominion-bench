# Kingdom analysis: horton-2026-04-finals

**Recommendation:** open 5/2 Governor / nothing and build a lean Governor engine. Gold gets turned into Provinces, and the game should end around turn 12–13. Governor-Big Money is the fallback and is unusually strong here, but my paper lines have the engine about two turns ahead.

**Board:** Governor is the centre of this board. It already gives +1 Action and three modes: +3 Cards, gain a Gold, or "trash a card and gain one costing exactly $2 more". The Friendly trait lets it gain copies of itself: each turn you end holding an unplayed Governor, you can swap it for a new one. That turns the 10-card Governor pile into a race that decides the game. Throne Room and Flagship double Governor. The remodel mode turns Gold into Province (and Silver into Governor or Duchy), so the payload goes past one Province per turn without needing $8. There are no attacks: Leprechaun's Hexes only hit the person who plays it. The only interference is what Governor gives the opponent (+1 card, a Silver, or their own remodel at $1 more), plus the fight over the pile. The open questions were +Buy and where the Golds come from. Forager, Tactician and Governor's own Gold mode answer them.

**Key interactions:**
- **Governor + Friendly:** you can play one Governor and hold another to gain a fresh copy, so a 2-Governor hand ends as 3 Governors. Holding a lone Governor early (turns 3–5) adds a $5 card on top of your normal buy and gives the opponent nothing. Expect the pile to be gone by about turn 8–10, split roughly 5/5. Whoever gets ahead first tends to stay ahead.
- **Throne Room or Flagship + Governor:** Throne Room on Governor is +6 Cards and +1 Action net, so it is draw and village in one card. In Gold mode it gains 2 Golds (the opponent gets 2 Silvers). In remodel mode it turns 2 Golds into 2 Provinces. Flagship on Governor is the same plus $2, and nets zero Actions.
- **Gold → Province:** Golds come from Governor's Gold mode, Leprechaun and Wish. A built turn can take about 2–3 Provinces through remodels and buy another with $8 or more.
- **Forager + the trash:** remodeling Golds puts Gold in the trash. Coppers get there from Forager itself and from the opponent's remodels. Silvers get there from Silver → Governor or Duchy remodels. That makes each Forager worth about +$3 and +Buy, without using an Action.
- **Merchant Camp + Way of the Owl:** Merchant Camp puts itself back on your deck after you play it, so you always start with a village. Late in an engine turn, playing any spare Action as Owl with 0–2 cards in hand is roughly +4–6 Cards. That gives the deck spare draw.

**Other cards:**
- **Experiment:** best with Anvil, which gains a pair for a discarded Copper, or with Forager's +Buy. Each pair is a temporary boost, worth about +4 cards over its two plays. Played as Owl it doesn't return to its pile.
- **Anvil:** Experiment pairs and Throne Room. It gains a $4 card in the Buy phase without using an Action, but it is a $1 stop card.
- **Tactician:** play it last with one dead card left, for 10 cards and +Buy next turn. Throne Room on it does nothing the second time. One copy at most.
- **Leprechaun:** in a built engine, play it as exactly your 7th card in play to get a Gold plus a Wish. The Wish can take a Governor to hand while the pile lasts. Otherwise play it as Owl. Low priority.
- **Advisor:** none worth having. The opponent picks the discard and will take your Governor.
- **Way of the Owl:** best with Merchant Camp, as above.

Ratings check: my view and the ratings agree on Governor (5.50), Forager (2.68) and Throne Room (2.73). I rate two things above their ratings:
- **Friendly (−0.11):** on this pile it is a free Governor every turn you can spare one.
- **Merchant Camp (−2.39):** putting itself back on the deck and working with Owl make it a dependable village here.

**Plans considered:**
- **A. Governor engine (pick).** Governors, then 1–2 Throne Rooms, 1 Flagship, 1–2 Merchant Camps and 2 Foragers, with Owl for spare draw. The payload is Gold → Province remodels plus buys.
  - Line: about 4 Governors by turn 7. Throne Room Gold-mode turns on turns 8–9. First turn with 3 or more Provinces about turn 10. Ends about turn 12–13.
  - Needs at least 4–5 Governors and at least 1 Throne Room or Flagship.
  - Weakness: losing the Governor split, and handing the opponent Silvers through Gold mode.
- **B. Governor-Big Money (floor).** Governors, Gold and Province, with remodels late.
  - Line: Provinces from about turn 8–9 at roughly 1–1.5 per turn. About 4 Provinces by turn 12. Alone it would empty the Provinces around turn 15–16.
  - Weakness: it caps out where plan A keeps going.
- **C. Leprechaun Gold flood.** Leprechauns plus Governor remodels.
  - Weakness: every play without exactly 7 cards in play costs a Hex (Misery is −2/−4 VP, Plague and Locusts give Curses). I folded the useful part into plan A as an optional single copy.

**Against their best plan:**
- **A vs B:** B leads by about 2 Provinces around turn 10. A then takes 3–4 per turn on turns 10–12, which wins the race to the last Province by about turn 12, against B needing until about turn 15.
- **Governor gifts:** each side's Governors feed the other roughly equally. Prefer draw or remodel mode over Gold mode while the opponent's deck is still weak, because their free Silver matters most then.
- **A vs A:** the Governor split decides it. Use Friendly aggressively early, and green one turn earlier than you would against money.
- **Three-pile risk:** none realistic. Only Governor is sure to empty, and nothing on this board drains Curses fast.

**Advocate's case** (a bigger version of A, since no higher-ceiling plan was rejected): add a Tactician, Anvil and a 7th-card Leprechaun for Wish → Governor, and aim for one 5–6 Province turn on turn 11. Line: T1–7 as above, plus Anvil on turn 6. That delays the first big turn to turn 11, but that turn can end the game. **Verdict:** each extra piece costs a buy from turns 6–8 that would otherwise have been a Governor or Throne Room. In a mirror, losing a Governor or two in that window is worse than the extra ceiling. So keep A lean, and add these only once the Governor pile is gone.

**How the pick loses:** opening 4/3 against a 5/2 Governor opener and losing the split, ending around 3 to 7 Governors. The opponent sees that coming at turn 1, so in this tournament format both players should pick 5/2.

**Open:**
- **5/2 (recommended):** Governor / nothing. Nothing on the board costs $2, and Copper hurts the deck.
- **4/3:** Forager + Silver for the best chance of hitting $5 on turns 3–4. Flagship + Forager is a close alternative, since Flagship counts as $2 and later doubles Governor.

**Buy priorities:**
- **Shuffles 1–2:** Governor at $5 or more, and Friendly-swap whenever the extra draw wouldn't change your buy. At $4: Throne Room or Flagship. At $3: Forager, then Merchant Camp. At $6–7: Governor while the pile is contested, otherwise Gold.
- **Shuffle 3:** a second Throne Room or Flagship, a second Forager, Gold mode for Golds. Add Tactician, Anvil or Leprechaun only once the Governor pile is empty.
- **Greening:** start once a turn produces 2 or more Golds to remodel plus $8 or more (about turn 9–10). After that, use remodels every turn and turn Silvers into Duchies.

**Ending:** the Provinces run out. Governor empties around turn 8–10, Experiment rarely does, and Curses only come from your own Hexes. Watch the Penultimate Province Rule: a Throne Room on Governor in remodel mode can take the last two Provinces at once.

**Watch out for:**
1. Falling behind in Governors. If you are behind, contest harder and hold Governors at clean-up.
2. Gold mode feeding the opponent's economy early.
3. Leprechaun played without exactly 7 cards in play. Misery is permanent −VP.

With 3 or more players, every Governor play gives each opponent a card, so it is weaker relative to the others and the pile empties sooner. Buy Governors faster.

**Least sure of:** whether 5/2 Governor really beats 4/3 Forager/Silver. Forager/Silver gets thinning and +Buy and usually hits $5 on turn 3 or 4. The test is whether the 4/3 deck has 4 Governors by turn 7. If it does, the openings are close and the 4/3 thinning wins.

**Assumed:**
- **Friendly:** you discard an unplayed Governor from hand at the start of Clean-up.
- **Governor's remodel:** "exactly $2 more" lets Gold become Province and Silver become Governor or Duchy. The opponent's version can trash a Copper and gain nothing, which is free thinning for them.
- **Throne Room on Experiment:** Experiment still gives its effect twice. Only the second "return this" fails.
- **Flagship's replay:** it can replay Throne Room, and if no Action follows it that turn it carries over to the next turn.
- **Owl:** a card played as Owl uses none of its own text, so it is terminal, and an Owl'd Experiment stays out.
- **Tournament rule:** each player picks their own split.

The repo context has nothing specific on Friendly or Governor mirrors. The turn counts come from my own paper lines with average draws, not from tested games.
