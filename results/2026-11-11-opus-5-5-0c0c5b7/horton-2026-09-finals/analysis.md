# Kingdom analysis: horton-2026-09-finals

**Board:** The board revolves around **Triumph** combined with **Footpad's below-the-line rule**. Footpad's rule applies whether or not anyone buys Footpad: every card gained in the Action phase draws a card. So every gainer here (Weaver, Stonemason, Change, Artificer, and Hostelry's Horses) is also draw. Triumph pays 1 VP for every card gained that turn, and there are only **8 Estates** to fuel it. Whoever has the biggest gain turns, with enough Buys to take several Triumphs, wins. Two things are still open. First, the only +Buy is Flourishing Trade, which turns on only after 5 River Shrine plays. Second, the 8-Estate cap makes Triumph a contested pile.

**Setup:** 2 players, no Colonies, no Shelters. You choose your split, so 4/3 or 5/2. The only Omen is River Shrine, so the Prophecy starts with 5 Sun and every River Shrine play, by either player, removes one. Special rules in play: Debt (Change, Triumph), Potion (Alchemist, Vineyard), Coffers, Villagers, and Horses (a non-Supply pile). Prices: there's a cluster of good cards at $4 (River Shrine, Hostelry, Weaver, Change, Exploration). The $5s are Witch's Hut, Artificer and Footpad. Stonemason is the only $2, and nothing costs more than $5 except Gold and Province.

**Key interactions:**
- **Weaver + Footpad's rule:** Weaver becomes "gain 2 Silvers, +2 Cards". A Weaver discarded by Witch's Hut or Artificer is played for free, and so is one the opponent's Footpad makes you discard.
- **Hostelry gained in the Action phase:** gaining a Hostelry with Weaver, Artificer or Change, holding N Treasures, gives N Horses plus the Hostelry. That's N+1 gains, each drawing a card. With 4 Treasures held: 5 gains and 5 cards. This is the board's biggest source of gains.
- **Artificer + Footpad's rule:** the gained card lands on your deck and is drawn at once. Discarding a Weaver to Artificer plays it as well. Discarding 4 cards to gain a Hostelry onto your deck draws the Hostelry, and its Horses draw more.
- **Stonemason overpay:** on a buy, $6 gets Stonemason plus 2 Hostelries or 2 Weavers, and $7 gets two $5 cards. That's 3 gains for one Buy, and the board's best way to build. Played from hand, Stonemason trashes a Copper outright.
- **Triumph stacking:** with G gains this turn, k Triumphs score about kG + k(k+1)/2 VP (each later Triumph also counts the earlier Triumphs' Estates). With G=10, two Triumphs score 11+12 = 23 VP.
- **Flourishing Trade (FT) + Hostelry / Exploration:** once FT is active, every spare Action is a Buy. Hostelry nets +1 Action, and Exploration banks a Villager on every turn with no Buy-phase gain. Those banked Villagers become the extra Triumph Buys on a big turn. FT also makes everything $1 cheaper: Weaver and River Shrine then reach the $5 cards, Stonemason costs $1, and Province costs $7.
- **Change + Triumph debt:** carrying Triumph's 5 Debt into the next turn turns each Change into +$3.
- **River Shrine:** trashes 2 cards, then gains a $4 card at Clean-up as long as you gained nothing in the Buy phase. Buying a Project isn't a gain, so you can buy Exploration and still get the free gain. River Shrine is also the only Sun source.

**Other cards:**
- **Witch's Hut:** best partner is Weaver. Discarding a Weaver plus another Action curses the opponent and plays the Weaver for free. Hut is also the only big draw card.
- **Footpad (the card):** its Coffers let you pay for a Hostelry without playing your Treasures, so you can discard them for Horses. The attack is weak against decks that draw, and it triggers the opponent's Weavers. Take one at most.
- **Exploration:** best partner is FT (Villagers become Buys). River Shrine and Weaver gain outside the Buy phase, so Exploration triggers on most building turns.
- **Change:** best partner is Triumph (pays its debt). It can also turn a Copper into a Hostelry, which is an Action-phase gain.
- **Alchemist:** needs a Potion, and the Footpad rule plus Horses already supply non-terminal draw. Skip it.
- **Vineyard:** Potion plus a Buy each, scoring 1 VP per 3 Actions. Horses go back to their pile, so they don't count. That's 4–5 VP per Vineyard late, which only matters once the Estates are gone.

**Ratings check** (Glicko): Triumph (2.74), Hostelry (2.37) and River Shrine (2.13) are rated high, and so are my picks. I rate two cards well above their ratings:
- **Weaver (0.59):** much stronger here because of Footpad's rule and the discard outlets.
- **Exploration (−1.42):** much stronger here because of FT and the Triumph Buys.

Footpad (−0.42) is about right as a card to buy. Its value on this board comes from the global rule, not from buying it.

### Plans considered
1. **Gain engine scoring with Triumph** (Hostelry, Weaver, River Shrine, Artificer, Hut, Stonemason overpay, Exploration).
   - First big turn around T10–11, about 20–60 VP.
   - Needs 2 River Shrines (for FT), 3–4 Hostelries and 2–3 Weavers.
   - Weakness: the opponent can take the Estates first.
2. **Witch's Hut or Footpad Big Money:** about 4 Provinces by T13–14. FT, activated by the opponent, makes Provinces $7. Hut almost never curses from a money deck.
3. **Vineyard action deck:** a Potion plus Vineyards on top of Stonemason-overpay Actions. Peaks at about 5 VP per Potion per turn, from around T11.
4. **Estate denial:** take small Triumphs early (3–4 VP each) to starve the opponent's engine. Each one costs $5 and a Buy while you're building.

### Build lines
- **Engine (opening 4/3: River Shrine / Silver).** Average draws:
  - T3: River Shrine with 3 Coppers and an Estate. Trash 2 Estates, buy nothing, and gain a Weaver at Clean-up.
  - T4: Silver and 3 Coppers make $5: Witch's Hut.
  - T5: Weaver gains 2 Silvers and draws 2. That hand makes $5: Artificer.
  - T6: River Shrine trashes 2 Coppers and gains a Hostelry (Sun 3). After this shuffle the deck is 14 cards: 5C, 1E, 3S, River Shrine, Weaver, Hut, Artificer, Hostelry.
  - T7: Hostelry → Hut (draws 4, discards the Estate and a Copper) → Artificer → Weaver (2 Silvers, +2 Cards). That's $11 with one Buy: Stonemason overpaying $4 for 2 Hostelries.
  - T8–9: second River Shrine, plus Exploration bought on a River Shrine turn. Stonemason overpay on $6–7 hands. Mirror River Shrine plays finish FT around T8–9 (around T10 if you play it alone).
  - Built turn, about T11, with a ~24-card deck (4 Hostelry, 3 Weaver, 2 Artificer, Hut, Stonemason, 2 River Shrine, about 7 Silvers):
    - Gains: two Weavers taking Silvers (4 gains), one Weaver gaining a Hostelry with 4 Treasures held (5 gains), Artificer (1), Stonemason (2). That's about 12 gains.
    - Money: about $14 plus Coffers. Buys: 1 spare Action plus about 3 Villagers.
    - Result: 3–4 Triumphs ≈ 13+14+15(+16) VP, so 40–60 VP. With only one Buy (FT not yet active): one Triumph ≈ 13 VP.
- **Hut-BM:** T1–2 Hut and Silver. Gold around T5–6, first Province around T8–9, 4 Provinces around T14, all 8 alone around T21.

**Against their best plan:**
- **Engine mirror:** decided by the first big Triumph turn and the split of the 8 Estates. Hut curses and a faster FT tip it. Player 2 should push harder for an early Hut.
- **Money:** against the engine, money has about 24 VP by T14, and the engine's first big turn at T11 already passes that. Money's only interference is Footpad, which triggers your Weavers.
- **Denial:** it spends T5–9 on 3–4 VP Triumphs with a stalled deck. The engine takes the remaining Estates at 12+ VP each, then buys Provinces with spare Buys.

**Advocate's case** (bigger version of the pick: save everything for one huge turn): build without Buy-phase gains through T11, so Exploration banks about 6 Villagers and 6 Coffers. Then on T12 make about 14 gains and take 5 Triumphs ≈ 15+16+17+18+19 = 85 VP. That's the higher ceiling.
- **Where it falls behind:** it doesn't win the mirror. If the opponent takes even 2–3 Estates on T10–11, the saved turn is capped at the 5–6 left, and saving gave up roughly a T10–11 Triumph turn of 13–25 VP.
- **Verdict:** bank Villagers, but take Triumphs as soon as one turn's gains reach about 7 (worth more than a Province). Save up only if the opponent has no gain engine.

**How the pick loses:**
- The opponent reaches 8+ gains a turn first and takes 4+ Estates.
- Repeated Witch's Hut curses stall your build.
- You stay stuck on one Buy because FT is late. Fix that by getting the second River Shrine early.

All three are visible from the opponent's opening and their Hut purchases.

### Recommendation
Build the **gain engine and score with Triumph**. It is the only plan whose payload grows with the deck. A worked line puts its first big turn around T11, beating money's T14 for 24 VP. In the mirror, what matters is speed and the Estate split.

**Open:**
- **4/3** (the recommended split): River Shrine / Silver.
- **5/2** (worse, since it delays your first trasher and your Sun plays): Artificer / Stonemason. Artificer is non-terminal, so the two never collide, and Stonemason trashes Coppers.

**Buy priorities:**
1. On River Shrine turns, buy nothing (Projects are fine) and take the free gain: Weaver first, then Hostelry.
2. At $5: Witch's Hut first, then Artificer.
3. At $6–7: Stonemason overpay. Get 2 Hostelries until Hostelries are at least equal to your terminals, then Weavers or Artificers.
4. At $4: a second River Shrine, Exploration once, and Hostelry. Gold only when nothing else fits.
5. Target deck: about 4 Hostelry, 3 Weaver, 2 Artificer, 1–2 Hut, 2 River Shrine, Stonemason, and optionally 1 Change.

**Greening:** start Triumph once a turn's gains reach about 7, and once FT is active take 2+ per turn. Spend leftover Buys and $ on Provinces ($7 under FT).

**Ending:**
- Estates will run out (Triumph, plus Stonemason turning a Silver into 2 Estates as a denial tool when you're ahead).
- Curses will run out if Huts fire often.
- The third pile is probably Hostelry or Weaver, which Stonemason overpay drains 2 at a time.
- The leader closes the game on piles. Otherwise it ends on Provinces.

**Watch out for:**
- **Small early Triumphs:** an opponent taking them is worth answering with your own once your gains are about 7.
- **Curses:** an opponent with heavy curses calls for more trashing with River Shrine and Change (Curse → Silver).
- **Slow FT:** if FT looks slow, add the second River Shrine sooner.
- **More players:** with 3+ players the Prophecy needs more Sun, the Estates and Province pile grow to 12, Triumph turns get contested by more players, and Hut's curses get more valuable.

**Least sure of:** whether to save Triumphs for the biggest turn or take them every turn once gains reach about 7. A paper mirror in which each player commits to one approach would settle it.

**Assumed:**
- **FT's "use Action plays as Buys"** means unused Actions, including those from Villagers, can be spent as Buys. FT's cost cut doesn't change Triumph's Debt cost.
- **Triumph** counts every card gained that turn, in any phase, including Horses and its own Estate. You need no Debt to buy it, and you must pay off its Debt before buying another.
- **Footpad's +1 Card** triggers on gains caused by other gains (Hostelry's Horses), and draws an Artificer gain that was put on your deck.
- **Buying a Project** isn't a gain, so it doesn't break River Shrine's Clean-up gain or Exploration.
- **Not given in the file:** the Horse pile size (assumed 30).
