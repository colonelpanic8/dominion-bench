**Board:** This is a Gold-flood board, and Courtier makes it one. Courtier gives one choice per type of the card you reveal, and the revealed card stays in your hand. Most cards here have two or three types: Tunnel is Victory–Reaction, Village Green is Action–Duration–Reaction, Barge is Action–Duration and Cardinal is Action–Attack. So a Courtier almost never gives just one choice. With two choices it is +$3 plus a Gold (terminal), or +Action plus +$3 (non-terminal). The real draw is thin: Barge nets +2 cards and Village Green nets 0. That makes a full draw-everything engine hard. The questions this leaves are how far past one Province per turn the deck can get, and how much the alternative VP (Tunnel and Silk Road) matters.

**Inventory**
- **Piles:** Lurker $2, Steward $3, Tunnel $3 (a Victory pile, 8 cards), Cardinal $4, Silk Road $4 (a Victory pile, 8 cards, **first edition only**), Village Green $4, Barge $5, Courtier $5, Displace $5, Mandarin $5 (**first edition only**).
- **Landscapes:** Gamble (a $2 Event), Way of the Goat.
- **Special rules:** Exile (Cardinal, Displace) and Durations (Barge, Village Green).
- **Unknown setup:** I assumed no Colonies and no Shelters.
- **Prices:** $5 is crowded (Barge, Courtier, Displace, Mandarin). $2 only has Lurker and Gamble. Nothing costs more than $5 except Gold.
- **Starting deck:** 7 Copper and 3 Estates, so the opening is 4/3 or 5/2.

**Key interactions:**
- **Courtier + any two-type card:** reveal a Tunnel for +$3 and a Gold. Or take +Action and +$3, which keeps Courtier non-terminal. One Tunnel in hand serves every Courtier you play that turn.
  - Revealing a Village Green gives three choices: +Action, +$3 and a Gold (or +Buy late).
  - From about turn 5, each Courtier play is worth roughly $3 plus a Gold, and the Golds compound.
- **Gamble + Tunnel / Village Green / Courtier:**
  - A Tunnel discarded from the top of the deck gains a Gold.
  - A discarded Village Green plays itself.
  - A Courtier flipped in the Buy phase is played without using an Action, and Tunnels still in hand give it two choices.
  - Gamble is about break-even on $ and gives +Buy. It gets better as the deck fills with Gold.
- **Displace + the Golds:** Displace exiles a card from hand and gains a different card costing up to $2 more. Some useful trades:
  - Gold → Province.
  - Tunnel → Duchy. The exiled Tunnel still scores its 2 VP, so this is +3 VP and one less green card in the deck.
  - Estate → Silk Road or Village Green. The exiled Estate still scores and still counts for Silk Road.
  - Province → Gold late, which keeps the 6 VP and turns a dead card into money.
  - One Displace in a Gold-flood deck works as a repeatable Province gainer.
- **Tunnel + Silk Road:** Silk Road counts Victory cards, and Tunnels and exiled cards count. Something like 3 Estates + 4 Tunnels + 4 Provinces + 2 Duchies + 3 Silk Roads is 16 Victory cards, so each Silk Road is worth 4 VP for $4. With spare Buys late, that is better value than Duchy.

**Other cards:**
- **Mandarin (1st ed.):** a weaker Courtier. It has two tricks:
  - Put a Tunnel from your hand on your deck, then Gamble discards it for a Gold.
  - Buy it on a Gold-heavy turn (with +Buy), and its on-gain effect puts your played Treasures back on your deck. Next turn's hand is then the same money.
- **Barge:** draw and +Buy, and the main way to turn the Gold into two Provinces in a turn. Its "next turn" mode gives a bigger starting hand. Its two types also make it a Courtier key.
- **Village Green:** gives the Actions to play several Courtiers, Barge and Displace together. It is also the best card to reveal to Courtier.
- **Steward:** a flexible opener, but heavy trashing isn't wanted. Estates help Silk Road and Coppers help a money-shaped deck.
- **Cardinal:** bad here.
  - Against Tunnel decks it discards their Tunnels, which gains them Golds.
  - A discarded Village Green plays itself for them.
  - An exiled Gold comes back to their discard pile the next time they gain a Gold, and Courtier gains them a lot of Golds.
  - Exiled Tunnels and Silk Roads still score.
- **Lurker:** two plays (trash a $5 Action from the Supply, then gain it from the trash) make a slow non-terminal gainer. Its only partner here is Way of the Goat. Skip it.
- **Way of the Goat:** a terminal "trash 1" for any spare Action, such as a Cardinal or Steward you don't need. Minor.

**Ratings as a check:**
- **Courtier (−0.28):** it is far better here than its rating, because almost every card on the board gives it two or more choices.
- **Tunnel (−1.71):** it is much better here than its rating, for three reasons:
  - It is a Courtier key.
  - Gamble discards it for a Gold.
  - It counts for Silk Road.
- **Cardinal (−2.26):** I agree with the low rating, and this board makes it worse.
- **Silk Road and Mandarin:** both are first edition only and rated with a large uncertainty, so I gave those ratings little weight.
- **Steward (3.04):** on this board it is good mostly as an opener.

### Build lines (average draws, 4/3 opening)

**Courtier Gold-flood (the pick)**
- **Turns 1–2:** Silver, Tunnel.
- **Turns 3–4:** Courtier at $5, Tunnel at $3 (Village Green at $4). 14 cards.
- **Turns 5–7:** Courtier is played once, with a Tunnel in hand about 55% of the time. That turn makes about $5 plus a Gold and buys a second Courtier. The other turns buy Village Green or Tunnel. About 18 cards.
- **Turns 8–10:** two Courtier plays, often non-terminal off a Village Green or Tunnel, and each gains a Gold. Buys: Barge, Gold, a third Courtier. About 23 cards, with $21 of Treasure among them.
- **Turn 11 on:** a Province most turns.
  - About one turn in three, Barge or Courtier +Buy plus Gamble makes $16 or more with 2 Buys, which is two Provinces.
  - Add one Displace around turn 10 (Gold → Province).
- Four Provinces by about turn 14, and scoring speeds up instead of flattening, because Golds keep arriving.

**Thin engine (Steward / Village Green / Barge / Courtier)**
- **Turns 1–2:** Steward, Silver.
- **Turns 3–7:** trash Estates and Coppers. Buy Barge, Courtier and Village Green, about one per turn.
- Drawing a 14-card deck needs about 4–5 Barges and as many Village Greens, which is 9–10 pieces at $4–5.
- By turn 12 it has about 7–8 of them, so it still stalls about half the time. First reliable double-Province turn is around turns 13–14.

**Barge Big Money (the floor)**
- Silver/Silver, then Barge at the first $5. Golds and Provinces after that.
- Four Provinces around turns 14–16.

| Plan | Starts scoring | Can end by | Peak VP/turn | VP ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Courtier Gold-flood | ~T10–11 | ~T16–17 | Province + Silk Road or Tunnel (two Provinces on Barge turns) | Provinces, Duchies, Tunnels, Silk Roads |
| Thin engine | ~T12–13 | ~T16 | 2 Provinces | Same |
| Barge Big Money | ~T11 | ~T20 | 1 Province | Provinces and Duchies |
| Tunnel/Silk Road slog | ~T6 | Three piles ~T15 | ~4 VP | Half the Tunnel and Silk Road VP (~25) |

**Plans considered:**
- **Courtier Gold-flood, supported by Village Green and Barge:**
  - **How it works:** money-shaped early. Courtier gains a Gold almost every play, then Barge, Gamble and Courtier's +Buy turn the Gold into Provinces.
  - **Needs:** 3–4 Courtiers and 2–4 Tunnels as keys, both contested.
  - **Weakness:** it is still mostly one Province per turn until about turn 12.
- **Thin engine:** described above. It is short on pieces at $4–5 and its draw is thin.
- **Tunnel/Silk Road slog:** scores early, but the opponent buys Tunnels too. Splitting the alternative-VP piles caps it at about 25 VP, which a Province deck passes.
- **Barge Big Money:** the floor, about two turns slower.

**Against their best plan:** In the mirror, the Courtier pile (10) and the Tunnel pile (8) decide the game. Take a third Courtier before a third Gold, and take Tunnels at $3 even while building. Against a Barge Big Money player, the pick is ahead by turn 13 and its Gold keeps growing, while their deck flattens out. Cardinal against you is close to harmless: discarded Tunnels gain you Golds, a discarded Village Green plays itself, and exiled Golds come back when you gain a Gold.

**Advocate's case (thin engine):** Its best line is Steward/Silver, then trashing on turns 3–4, then Barge and Courtier at every $5 and Village Green at $4. Courtiers are non-terminal off Barge or Village Green. Its best case is a Barge-started turn of about 8 cards making $15 with 3 Buys on turn 12.

It falls behind on turns 9–12. At turn 10 it has about $8 of Treasure against the Gold-flood's $21 plus Courtiers. On turns 11–12 it buys pieces while the pick takes 2–3 Provinces. Its double-Province turns arrive about turn 13, with 6–8 Provinces left to split. That is about 2 Provinces behind, with no faster way to catch up. The verdict holds, but the gap is small. The pick is really this engine with Golds in place of the thinning.

**Recommendation:** Build a Courtier-centric Gold flood. Courtier is the main payload and Tunnels are its keys. Add Village Green for Actions, Barge for draw and +Buy, and Gamble as spare +Buy. Add one Displace mid-game. Finish on Provinces, then Silk Roads, Tunnels and Duchies.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Silver / Tunnel. (Steward / Tunnel is acceptable.)
- **5/2:** Courtier / nothing. Gamble or Lurker on turn 2 just cycles a card. Courtier on turns 3–4 still gains a Gold off an Estate or Copper.

**Buy priorities:**
- **Shuffles 2–3:** Courtier up to 3 copies. Village Green at $4, Tunnel at $3 (2–4 total). Gold at $6–7.
- **Shuffle 4:** one Barge and one Displace. Gamble with spare $2, especially when you need a Buy.
- **Greening:** Provinces at $8 from about turn 11. Silk Road with spare $4 once you have about 12 or more Victory cards. Tunnel with spare $3. Duchy once 4 Provinces are left. Use Displace to turn Gold into Province.

**Ending:** Most likely on Provinces. A three-pile ending is possible because Gamble +Buy drains the cheap Victory piles. The likely piles are Tunnel, Silk Road and Duchy, or Courtier in a mirror. Track the Silk Road values and the Tunnel count when the game is close.

**Watch out for:**
- Losing the Courtier split.
- Over-trashing. Coppers and Estates help this deck.
- An opponent emptying the Tunnel and Silk Road piles while ahead on alternative VP.

**What would flip the plan:**
- **Shelters:** Hovel, Necropolis and Overgrown Estate each have two types, so the starting deck is full of Courtier keys, and Necropolis is a village. That favours an earlier Courtier.
- **Colonies:** they favour the Gold flood even more.
- **3 or more players:** Courtier and Tunnel are more contested, and a three-pile ending comes sooner.

**Least sure of:** Whether the thin engine overtakes the pick by turn 13. That rests on my paper line, and engines usually run faster than a paper line shows. The test is whether a Barge-and-Village-Green deck can play three Courtiers and two Barges on turn 11 often enough.

**Assumed:**
- No Colonies or Shelters.
- First-edition Silk Road and Mandarin are in use.
- A card played by Gamble needs no Action.
- A revealed card stays in hand, so one Tunnel serves several Courtiers.
- Exiled cards score and count for Silk Road.
- Gaining a Gold lets you discard your exiled Golds.

**How the pick loses:** An opponent who sees your Silver/Tunnel opening goes heavy on Courtiers and Tunnels to win both splits, then drains the Tunnel and Silk Road piles while ahead.
