## Beyond the key
- A revealed card stays in hand, so one Tunnel or Village Green keys every Courtier that turn: plausible. Courtier only reveals; nothing moves the card.
- Tunnel (Victory–Reaction) gives Courtier two choices: plausible. Two types by card text.
- Gamble discarding a Tunnel gains a Gold; discarding a Village Green plays it: plausible. Both trigger on a discard outside Clean-up, which Gamble's discard is.
- Gamble flipping Courtier in the Buy phase plays it without spending an Action: plausible. Gamble says "you may play it", no Action needed.
- Mandarin topdecks a Tunnel so Gamble discards it for a Gold: plausible. Mandarin's play puts any card from hand on the deck.
- Displace Province → Gold keeps the 6 VP on the Exile mat: plausible. Gold costs within $2 more and is differently named; exiled cards are still yours at scoring.
- Exiled cards count toward Silk Road: plausible. Silk Road counts Victory cards you have, Exile included.
- Cardinal against a Tunnel/Village Green deck helps the victim (Tunnels gain Golds, VG plays itself, exiled Golds return when gaining Gold): plausible. Cardinal discards the non-exiled card, triggering Tunnel/VG, and Exile copies are discardable on gaining a copy.
- Shelters would make the starting deck full of two-type Courtier keys: plausible. Hovel, Necropolis and Overgrown Estate each have two types.
- Mandarin is "a weaker Courtier" here: doubtful. Its on-gain replays every Gold in play, which is what the key's plan is built on; the analysis undervalued that.

## Summary
The analysis correctly makes Courtier the payload, uses Village Green as its multi-type key and builds in +Buy, and it even spots Mandarin's on-gain topdecking played Treasures. But it treats Mandarin as a minor trick instead of the engine that replays the Golds every turn, never topdecks the Courtier key with Mandarin's play, and so projects a much slower Province-a-turn deck than the key's combo.
