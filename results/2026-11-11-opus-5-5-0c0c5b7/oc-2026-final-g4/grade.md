## Beyond the key
- First Mate + Jester (plays every Jester in hand without spending Actions, then draws to 6) as the central engine piece: plausible. That's what First Mate's text does. Still, the commentators thought spending a gain on First Mate was a real cost here, and neither player bought one.
- Crop Rotation finds an Estate in about 91% of 6-card hands from 12 cards with 3 Estates: plausible. 1 − C(9,6)/C(12,6) = 1 − 84/924 ≈ 0.91.
- Crop Rotation can't discard Curses: plausible. A Curse isn't a Victory card.
- Ritual nets cost − 1 (Lab +4, Loot +6) and stops working once Curses run out: plausible. Ritual says "If you do" (gain a Curse), and the Curse is −1 VP.
- An early Jester gives a Curse about 30% of the time: plausible. 3 of the 10 starting cards are Estates.
- Several Corsairs still trash only one Silver/Gold per turn: plausible. The text says "the first Silver or Gold they play each turn".
- Skip Tactician because it clashes with First Mate and Crop Rotation: doubtful. Crop Rotation still fires at the start of the Tactician turn, and +5 Cards +1 Action +1 Buy is one of the board's few big draw-and-Buy sources. Both players bought Tacticians.
- Aristocrat played by First Mate counts toward the in-play tally: plausible. Aristocrat counts the Aristocrats in play, however they were played.

## Summary
The analysis gets the junk-heavy, trashing-free character of the board right. It also gets Jester, Crop Rotation and Egg right, along with a Lab/Aristocrat/Ghost Town engine much like the commentators'. It misses the game's defining line, Ritual on Flag Bearer for VP plus the Flag, and the Flag war that followed. It demotes Ritual to an endgame extra and builds around First Mate, a card the commentators thought too costly.
