I won 40–22. The game ended on my turn 14 when I bought the last Province.

**RESULT (the Provinces ran out): 1. Ash 40 VP (14 turns), 2. Birch 22 VP (13 turns)**

**Final deck (29 cards, plus 2 in Exile):** 6 Province, 1 Tunnel, 2 Estate, 7 Gold, 2 Silver, 7 Copper, 3 Courtier, 2 Village Green, 1 Steward, 1 Lurker. Exiled: 1 Gold and 1 Silver, both taken by the opponent's Cardinal.

**The plan (in notes.md):** The deck was built around Courtier, which was the strongest card on this board. Revealing any card with it gains a Gold or gives +$3. Revealing a 2-type card gives two of the bonuses, and revealing Village Green, which has three types, gives three: +Action, +$3 and a Gold. Village Green stays in hand after being revealed, so it can then be played as a village. Steward and Way of the Goat were for trashing, and Barge and Gamble for +Buy. The opening was Courtier/Lurker on a 5/2 split, then Village Greens. I would start buying Provinces once the deck had about three Golds, and use Tunnels and Gamble with spare Buys at the end. I kept Courtier-plus-money as the fallback and decided against a Tunnel/Silk Road plan.

**How it worked:**
- Courtier carried the game. Almost every play gained a Gold, and twice it revealed Village Green for three bonuses. One turn played two Courtiers off the same Village Green for 2 Golds and a Province. I had 2 Provinces by turn 10 and 4 by turn 12.
- Lurker was better than I expected. It trashed a Courtier from the Supply and later gained it back for free, which gave me my third Courtier. Gamble only made a small difference: it discarded an Estate from the top of my deck, which helped the next hand slightly.
- The opponent built slowly with Cardinal and Silvers, and their Cardinal exiled two of my Treasures. With Courtier as the scaling payoff, I won without building a full draw engine. Barge and heavier trashing turned out to be unnecessary.
