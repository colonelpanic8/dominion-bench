# Kingdom analysis: Barge, Cardinal, Courtier, Displace, Lurker, Mandarin, Silk Road, Steward, Tunnel, Village Green · Gamble, Way of the Goat

**Setup:** 2 players. The kingdom file doesn't say whether Colonies or Shelters are used. I assume neither, so the starting deck is 7 Copper and 3 Estate, with 4/3 or 5/2 openings. Mandarin and Silk Road are first-edition-only cards (Hinterlands 1st ed.). Way of the Goat applies to every Action. Gamble is a $2 Event. Exile is in play through Cardinal and Displace.

**Board:** The main payload is Gold gains. Courtier gives the most when it reveals a card with several types: Village Green gives 3 picks, and Tunnel, Barge or Cardinal give 2. Tunnel and Gamble add more Gold gains. The deck control is moderate: Village Green is a village whose +2 Actions can wait until next turn, Barge is terminal +3 Cards that can also wait, and Steward and Way of the Goat thin. Steward is the only fast trasher. Two things are still open. Draw only adds about 2 cards per Village Green and Barge pair, so this is a thin "good-stuff" engine, not a full draw-your-deck engine. Points beyond Provinces come from Tunnel (2 VP at $3), Silk Road and Duchy.

**Key interactions:**
- **Courtier + Village Green:** reveal Village Green and pick 3 of +Action, +$3, gain a Gold and +Buy. Revealing doesn't use the card, so you can still play Village Green afterwards. The usual pick is +Action, +$3 and a Gold, which makes Courtier non-terminal and worth about $3 plus a Gold. Turn the +Action into +Buy when you're greening. This works from the first shuffle that has both cards, around turns 5–7.
- **Courtier + Tunnel:** reveal Tunnel and get +$3 and a Gold (or +$3 and +Buy). A Tunnel in hand stops being a dead card. Revealing an Estate or a Treasure gives one pick, either a Gold or +$3.
- **Gamble + Tunnel, or Mandarin → Tunnel → Gamble:** Gamble discards the top card of your deck. If it's a Tunnel, Tunnel gains you a Gold, and Gamble's +1 Buy refunds the Buy it used. Mandarin can put a Tunnel from your hand on top first, which makes it certain. For Mandarin plus $2, you net $1 and a Gold.
- **Gamble as a Buy-phase play:** it plays the top Action or Treasure, even after you've bought something. A Gold nets +$1 and a Courtier nets about +$1. A Barge or Village Green can be set for next turn: Barge for +3 Cards and +1 Buy, Village Green for +1 Card and +2 Actions. So with $2 left over, Gamble is roughly free, except when the top card is a Copper or a green card.
- **Mandarin's on-gain effect:** buying Mandarin puts every Treasure you have in play back on top of your deck. Next turn you redraw the same Treasures. This is a late "repeat a good turn" trick (Province plus Mandarin, then Province again). It needs +Buy, and a terminal $3 card in the deck is the cost. Gambling the replaced Golds again nets only +$1 each, so it pays only with 5 or more Golds in play.
- **Displace on Gold or green cards:** Displace exiles a card from your hand and gains one costing up to $2 more. Gold becomes a Province. Tunnel or Silk Road becomes a Duchy, or Silk Road becomes a Gold. Exiled cards still score and still count for Silk Road. So late in the game it turns the Golds Courtier made into Provinces, and turns green cards into bigger green cards without the deck getting worse.

**Other cards:**
- **Cardinal:** its attack mostly backfires on this board. If it discards the opponent's Tunnel, they gain a Gold. If it discards their Village Green, they play it for next turn. Any Exiled Gold comes back to them the next time Courtier gains them a Gold. Treat it as a weak terminal +$2 at most.
- **Lurker:** a slow gainer. Use it once to trash a $5 Action from the Supply and again to gain it from the trash (the opponent can take it too). It can also drain piles. Low value.
- **Silk Road:** best partner is Tunnel, which counts as a Victory card. In a normal game you end with about 12–16 Victory cards, so each Silk Road is worth 3–4 VP for $4. It's a good extra purchase in the endgame.
- **Way of the Goat:** makes a dead extra Action (a colliding Mandarin, Cardinal or Lurker) trash a Copper. It's light thinning, not a plan.
- **Barge:** the deck's draw, and +Buy. Playing it for next turn (start-of-turn draw) is usually better.
- **Steward:** the opening card. Trash 2 early, then use it for +2 Cards or +$2.

**Ratings check:** Steward (3.0), Barge (2.6) and Village Green (2.0) are rated high, and I agree with all three. Courtier is rated −0.3, and I rate it well above that here: Village Green and Tunnel are strong cards to reveal, and gaining a Gold each play builds the deck. Cardinal is rated −2.3, and I agree it's weak, more so here. Mandarin (−3.2) and Silk Road (−2.75) are deprecated first-edition cards with high uncertainty. Mandarin is mediocre here. Silk Road is better than its rating late in the game, because Tunnels raise its count.

### Build lines

- **Barge Big Money (baseline):** turns 1–2 Silver/Silver, or Barge/Silver on 5/2. Turns 3–4 Barge. Gold at $6–7 and a second Barge by about turn 7. First $6 around turn 5 and first $8 around turn 7–8. 4 Provinces by about turn 14. Gamble with a spare $2 turns Province hands into Province plus Silver or Tunnel.
- **Courtier Gold-flood with Village Green, Barge and Steward (pick):**
  - T1 Silver, T2 Steward. Deck: 7C 3E S St.
  - T3: C C C S E = $5. Buy Courtier.
  - T4: Steward trashes 2 Estates. Buy nothing.
  - T5: Courtier reveals the last Estate to gain a Gold, then S + C C = $4. Buy Village Green.
  - T6: Steward +$2 with C×4 = $6. Buy Gold.
  - T7: Courtier reveals Village Green (+Action, +$3, Gold), then play Village Green. $9: buy Courtier #2.
  - T8: about $8. Buy Barge.
  - By T9 the deck is about 17 cards: 7C 1E S St 2Co VG 3G Barge, with Golds still arriving from every Courtier play.
  - From T10, a typical turn: Barge from last turn gives +3 Cards, Village Green and 2 Courtiers give $6, 2 Golds and +Buy, and the Treasures add about $8. That's about $14–17 with 2 Buys, usually Province plus Gold or Duchy, sometimes 2 Provinces. Late in the game add Displace (Gold → Province) and Silk Roads and Tunnels with the extra Buys.
- **Tunnel/Silk Road slog:** buy Tunnels at $3 and Silk Roads at $4, using Gamble and Courtier for Golds. In 2-player those piles hold 8 cards each and both players take from them, so you get roughly 4–5 Silk Roads worth about 3 VP each. It scores slowly and has no answer to a deck taking 2 Provinces a turn.

| Plan | Starts scoring | Can end by | Peak VP/turn | VP ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Barge Big Money | ~T8 | ~T20 alone | 6 (1 Province) | ~half the Provinces |
| Courtier Gold-flood | ~T10 | ~T16–17 | 9–12 (Province + Duchy/Silk Road, sometimes 2 Provinces) | Provinces + Tunnels, Silk Roads, Duchies |
| Tunnel/Silk Road slog | ~T6 | T25+ | ~3–4 | Silk Road and Tunnel piles, split with the opponent |

**Against their best plan:** Against Barge Big Money, the Gold-flood falls behind by about one Province around turn 10. From there it gains Golds every turn and often a second buy, while Big Money levels off at one Province every other turn. The Gold-flood also takes the 2-VP cards (Tunnels, Silk Roads, Duchies), so a 4–4 Province split goes its way. Against a mirror, contest Courtier and Village Green, and green a turn or two earlier. Cardinal against you is mostly harmless and often helps you, so a Cardinal plan is no threat. The slog loses to both: it can't end the game, and it can't keep up with 2 Provinces a turn.

**Challenged:** I gave a full draw-everything engine (several Village Greens and Barges, heavy Steward and Goat thinning) one more try. With a net +2 cards per pair and no lab-type draw, it needs about 4 of each before it draws the whole deck, around turn 12–13. That's no faster than the Gold-flood and has less money, so I keep the hybrid. The pick loses in three ways: if it misses its Village Green and Courtier pairing over the first two shuffles (so get a second Village Green early), if it over-trashes and hits only $4–5, or if it greens too late against a Big Money player who is already at 4 Provinces.

**Recommendation:** Courtier Gold-flood. Courtier is the payload and Village Green is both its best reveal target and the village. Barge plays for next turn as draw plus +Buy. Steward thins early, and Gamble uses any spare $2. It holds up against Big Money because it buys Gold every turn and then Province plus a second buy, and Cardinal can't really hurt it.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Silver/Steward, or Village Green/Steward if you want to pair Village Green with Courtier early.
- **5/2:** Courtier/nothing, or Courtier/Lurker. Barge/nothing is also fine. Don't Gamble on turn 2.

**Buy priorities:**
1. First shuffle: 1–2 Courtiers at $5 and a Village Green at $4.
2. Next: Barge, a second Village Green, Gold at $6–7. Tunnel at $3 over Silver once you have a Courtier. Gamble with a spare $2.
3. Steward trashes Estates and some Coppers, but keep about 5 Coppers.
4. Green from about turn 10, or once you hit $13 or more with 2 Buys: Province first, then Duchy, Silk Road or Tunnel with extra Buys.
5. Endgame: Displace for Gold → Province, Mandarin to repeat a big turn.

**Ending:** Most likely the Provinces run out, around turns 17–19. Tunnel and Silk Road (8 each) plus Duchy is a real three-pile risk once both players buy the cheap green cards. Lurker can push an unwanted Action pile toward empty. Count piles from turn 14.

**Watch out for:**
- An opponent buying Tunnels lets them turn your Gamble-style plays and their own Gambles into Golds. Take your share of Tunnels.
- An opponent rushing the Tunnel/Silk Road/Duchy piles.
- Mandarin's on-gain trick only pays with several Golds in play and +Buy. Don't buy it early.

**Least sure of:** Whether the Gold-flood really beats Barge Big Money, which rests on Courtier finding a Village Green or Tunnel to reveal often enough. If two-type reveals happen less than about half the time, Courtier is a $5 Silver-plus, and Barge Big Money with Gamble is roughly as good. Test: play out turns 5–12 of both lines with real shuffles.

**Assumed:**
- No Colonies and no Shelters. Shelters would add 2-type reveals for Courtier and Necropolis as a village. Colonies would favour the Gold-flood more.
- Gamble can play a card after you've bought something (the rules reference says so).
- Gamble's discard counts as "other than during Clean-up" for Tunnel and Village Green.
- A Way replaces all of a card's text, so Lurker played as Goat gives no +Action.
- Exiled cards score and count for Silk Road.
- Mandarin and Silk Road text is first edition, as given in the kingdom file.

With 3 or more players, the Tunnel and Silk Road piles go to 12 each and empty sooner, and Courtier and Village Green are contested more. Lean further toward Golds and an earlier greening.
