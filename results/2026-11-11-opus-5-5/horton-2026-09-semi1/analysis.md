I worked from the card text in the kingdom file and the repo context, and kept it to a 2-player tournament game where each player picks a 4/3 or 5/2 opening. The build lines are average-draw paper games, not simulations.

## Kingdom: Transmute (Reckless), Silk Merchant, Lurker, Council Room, Figurine, Shop, Poet, Highway, Tools, Kitsune · Panic (Prophecy), Prepare (Event)

**Inventory:**
- **$0+Potion:** Transmute. Reckless means it trashes 2 cards per play and goes back to its pile after use. It brings the Potion pile.
- **$2:** Lurker.
- **$3:** Shop and Prepare.
- **$4:** Silk Merchant, Poet and Tools.
- **$5:** Council Room, Figurine, Highway and Kitsune.
- **Prophecy:** Panic needs 5 Suns removed in a 2-player game. Only Poet and Kitsune remove Suns.
- **Tokens:** Coffers and Villagers come from Silk Merchant.
- **Setup:** standard starting deck, no Colonies, no Shelters. The split is your choice.

**Board:** Kitsune is the board's junker, and it doubles as a village (+2 Actions) and +$2. Every Kitsune play also removes a Sun, so in a Kitsune game **Panic will probably turn on around turns 6–9**. After that every Treasure you play gives +2 Buys and then goes back to its pile. Coppers thin themselves out, but Silver and Gold become one-shots. So the economy has to come from things that aren't Treasures: Coffers (Silk Merchant, Lurker), Kitsune's +$2, Shop's +$1 and Highway's cost cuts. The open question was where the draw and Actions come from. The answer is Kitsune and Villagers for Actions, and Silk Merchant and Council Room for draw.

**Key interactions:**
- **Lurker + Silk Merchant:** Lurker trashes a Silk Merchant from the Supply for +1 Coffers and +1 Villager. Your next Lurker takes it back from the trash for another +1 Coffers and +1 Villager, and you get the card.
  - Every Lurker play is worth about +$1 and +1 Action, banked as tokens, from turn 3 or 4.
  - Every second play also adds a free Silk Merchant (draw and +Buy).
  - The opponent's Lurker can take that Silk Merchant out of the trash too.
- **Kitsune + Panic:** each Kitsune play removes a Sun.
  - If both players play about one Kitsune per turn from turn 3 or 4, Panic is on by about turn 6–8.
  - After that, each Copper you play is $1 plus 2 Buys and then leaves your deck. That is free thinning for an Action deck, and it ruins any deck built on Gold.
- **Highway + Panic Buys + Silk Merchant:** cost cuts and plenty of Buys together.
  - With 2 Highways in play, Province costs $6, Duchy $3 and Estate $0.
  - With 3 Highways in play, Province costs $5 and Silk Merchant costs $1. Its +1 Coffers refunds that $1, so it's free.
  - This is both the endgame payload and a way to empty piles: Estates cost nothing, and every Treasure you play adds 2 Buys.
- **Prepare + terminal collision:** at the end of a turn, set aside the Council Rooms and Silk Merchants you couldn't play. They all play at the start of your next turn without using Actions. It's a $3 village, and Panic gives you the spare Buy to use it.
- **Tools + cards in play:** Tools is a non-terminal gainer of whatever $5 card you have in play (Kitsune, Highway, Silk Merchant for +1 Coffers and +1 Villager). Once Panic is on it goes back to its pile after one play, so it's a $4 one-shot that also gives +2 Buys.

**Other cards:**
- **Council Room:** the main big draw, run off Kitsune and Villagers. The card it gives the opponent is an acceptable cost.
- **Poet:** a weak Laboratory. It draws Coppers, Estates, Curses, Lurkers and Shops, which are all $3 or less, plus more cards under Highway. It also removes a Sun.
- **Shop:** a Peddler that plays one different Action for free. It works with Council Room or Silk Merchant. It's filler.
- **Figurine:** none. It's draw in the Buy phase that gets thrown away once Panic is on.
- **Transmute (Reckless):** see "Challenged". Two Estates become two Golds and two Curses get trashed, but it's too slow and too dependent on Potion.

**Ratings check:**
- My view matches the ratings for Silk Merchant (6.84), Kitsune (4.48) and Highway (3.33).
- Council Room (−0.94) and Prepare (−1.39) are worth more here. Kitsune and Villagers make Council Room playable, and Prepare is a cheap village.
- Figurine (−2.95) is worth less than its rating, because Panic eats it.
- Transmute is rated −6.84. Reckless doubles it, but my view still matches the rating.

### Plans considered
1. **Big Money + Council Room, or + Kitsune (the baseline).**
   - Without Panic, it would take about 4 Provinces by turn 14–15.
   - In practice the opponent's Kitsune plays turn Panic on by about turn 9 even if you play no Omens, and Kitsune adds a Curse almost every turn.
   - After Panic, every Gold is a one-shot. The deck falls apart with about 2 Provinces.
   - **Rejected.**
2. **Kitsune / Silk Merchant / Lurker engine with Council Room draw and Highway payload.**
   - It takes Panic as both its trasher and its source of Buys.
   - It reaches $6 by turns 3–5 with Coffers, its first Province on about turns 10–11, and double Provinces by about turns 13–14.
   - It plays Kitsune almost every turn from turn 5.
   - It needs 3–4 Kitsunes, 3 or more Silk Merchants, 2 Lurkers and 2–3 Highways.
   - Its weakness is the Curses you can't trash, and a three-pile ending that comes early.
3. **Potion → Reckless Transmute (Estates become Golds; Duchies late).** Your first Transmute play lands on about turns 5–7, and the Golds become one-shots once Panic is on. **Rejected** (see "Challenged").

### Build lines
- **Engine (plan 2), opening 5/2:**
  - **Turns 1–2:** Kitsune / Lurker.
  - **Turn 3:** Kitsune with 3 Coppers and an Estate. Choose Curse + $2, for $5 total, and buy a second Kitsune.
  - **Turn 4:** Lurker trashes a Silk Merchant from the Supply (+1 Coffers, +1 Villager). $3 plus 1 Coffers buys a Silk Merchant (another +1 Coffers, +1 Villager).
  - **Turn 5:** Kitsune (+2 Actions, Curse), then Kitsune (Curse, +$2), then Silk Merchant. That makes $5–6 with 2 Buys: Silk Merchant + Lurker.
  - **Turn 6:** Lurker takes the Silk Merchant back from the trash. Buy Kitsune #3 or Council Room with Coffers. Panic is about to turn on (about 5 Suns between both players).
  - **Turns 7–9:** play Coppers when you need them, so they leave your deck. Add Highway, Council Room and a second Lurker. Curses run out at about turn 9 or 10.
  - **Built turn (about turn 13):**
    - Play 3 Kitsunes (each +2 Actions, +$2) for $6.
    - Draw through Silk Merchants and Council Room, and play 2 Highways.
    - Add a couple of Coppers ($2, plus 4 Buys) and 4 Coffers, for about $12.
    - That buys 2 Provinces at $6 each, plus Estates at $0. About 13 VP.
- **Big Money + Council Room:** Silver/Silver, then Council Room. $6 on turns 3–4 and $8 on about turn 6. Once Panic turns on at about turn 9 (from the opponent's Kitsunes), it is down to Silver hands by about turn 11.

**Against their best plan:**
- The best reply is the mirror.
  - The Curse split decides it, so getting Kitsune first and playing it every turn matters most.
  - Contest Kitsune and Silk Merchant: both piles can run out. You can also take the Silk Merchants their Lurker trashed.
- Against money, Panic and the Curses beat them. Make sure you keep playing Kitsune so Panic turns on.

**Challenged:**
- **Transmute, re-tested:**
  - Potion costs your $4 opening slot, and the turn you buy Transmute is a turn with only 1 Buy.
  - Two Golds arrive on about turn 7, while the opponent has been cursing since turn 3.
  - Late in the game it can turn 2 Actions into 2 Duchies, but Highway already prices Duchy at $3.
  - Its one real use is trashing Curses. Only consider it in a deep Curse slog, and before Panic is on.
- **How the pick loses:** you lose the Curse split, or the opponent ends the game on three piles while ahead (Curses + Kitsune or Silk Merchant + Estates). Track the pile counts every turn.

**Recommendation:** the Kitsune / Silk Merchant / Lurker engine, with Council Room as draw, Highway as payload, and Panic as both trashing and +Buy. It holds up against the mirror and crushes money.

**Open:**
- **5/2 (choose this):** Kitsune / Lurker.
- **4/3, if you can't choose:** Silk Merchant / Silk Merchant. Turn 2 is $3 plus 1 Coffers, and you finish with 2 Villagers. Get Kitsune as soon as you reach $5.

**Buy priorities:**
- **Shuffles 1–2:** at $5 buy Kitsune (until you have 3–4), at $4 Silk Merchant, at $2–3 a second Lurker (use spare Buys for these).
- **Then:** Council Room as draw, 2–3 Highways, Prepare whenever terminals are stuck in hand, and Tools with any spare $4.
- **Never:** Gold or Silver, because Panic makes them one-shots.
- **Greening:** start once you can reach 2 Provinces a turn, around turns 12–14. Pick up free Estates under Highway whenever you have Buys left over.

**Ending:**
- Curses are certain to run out.
- Kitsune and Silk Merchant are at risk too. Lurker trashing from the Supply speeds both up.
- Estates empty quickly under 2 Highways plus Panic Buys.
- A three-pile ending is more likely than the Provinces running out, and the player ahead controls it.

**Watch out for:**
- Losing the Kitsune or Curse race. If you're behind on Curses, pivot to Duchies and Estates under Highway, and end it on piles if you're ahead.
- A money opponent who skips Omens: Panic is then up to your own Kitsune plays.
- The opponent's Lurker trashing Kitsunes or Highways from the Supply to deny you them.
- With more players, Panic needs 8 Suns or more, Curses get split, and Council Room helps every opponent.

**Least sure of:**
- When Panic turns on, and how much losing Treasures hurts the engine's own early Coppers.
- **Test:** a paper game of the mirror that counts the turn of the 5th Sun.
- If Panic turns on later than turn 10, money hybrids and Tools get better.

**Assumed:**
- Lurker trashing Silk Merchant from the Supply gives the player using Lurker +1 Coffers and +1 Villager. This is the usual on-trash ruling, but I couldn't confirm it from the repo.
- "Costs $3 or less" (Poet) excludes Transmute's Potion cost.
- Reckless Transmute resolves its trash-and-gain twice, one card at a time.
- Panic triggers on every Treasure you play, including Tools and Figurine.
- Cards that Prepare plays don't use up Actions.
