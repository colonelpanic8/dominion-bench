# Kingdom analysis: Forts, Destrier, Ducat, Steward, Cabin Boy, Guide (Reckless), Wishing Well, Ratcatcher, Harbinger, Mystic, plus the Exploration project

**Board:** There is no Curse-giver and no attack of any kind, so this is a straight race to Provinces. Scoring is Provinces and Duchies, plus 4 Strongholds (2 VP each) inside the Forts pile. Only one card adds hand size without using up Actions: Destrier, at $6. There is no true village. Villagers from Exploration come at most once per turn. Reckless Guide is a village only once (see Assumed). So you play about one terminal per turn. The question that matters is which terminal to give that one Action to. The best answer is Tent, because it puts itself back on your deck every turn.

**Key interactions:**
- **Tent puts itself back on your deck.** Tent goes onto your deck when it's discarded from play. Clean-up discards before you draw, so Tent is in your next hand every turn. That gives a guaranteed +$2 using the one Action this board has spare. Your hand becomes $2 plus 4 cards, against 5 cards for a Silver deck. Tent wins while your average card is under $2, which is all game:
  - 15-card deck with $15 in it: Silver deck averages 5×17/16 = $5.3. Tent deck averages 2+4×1 = $6.0.
  - Late deck (18 cards, $30): $8.4 against $8.7.
  
  Tent also never misses a shuffle.
- **Tent can turn the Forts pile.** Each Tent play may move the top card to the bottom: Tent → Garrison → Hill Fort → Stronghold → Tent. Your permanent Tent can turn it once per turn, so you control what's on top during your turn. Stronghold is 3 turns away from the start.
- **Cabin Boy can become a Stronghold.** At the start of your next turn, Cabin Boy can trash itself to gain a Duration. If Stronghold is on top of Forts, that's 2 VP in exchange for the +$2. This is a cheap greening tool once the game is near its end. The catch: the trigger happens before you play Tent, so the opponent's Tent turns are what decide which Fort is on top at that moment.
- **Destrier gets cheaper with gains.** Each card gained this turn makes it $1 cheaper, and Ducat or Hill Fort gains supply those. Ducat bought first makes Destrier $5, so the two together cost $7. This is real, but it only feeds a lab plan that's too slow here (see Challenged).

**Other cards:**
- **Cabin Boy:** a Silver that doesn't use up a hand slot. It draws a card, keeps your Action for Tent, and gives +$2 next turn. Pairs with Stronghold.
- **Hill Fort:** in the +1 Card +1 Action mode it's a non-terminal gainer of a card up to $4 (e.g. Cabin Boy). A good $5 buy whenever it's on top; Tent can turn the pile to it.
- **Stronghold:** 2 VP plus a terminal +$3 (playing it instead of Tent breaks Tent's loop for a turn). Use it as greening at $6–7.
- **Ratcatcher:** a non-terminal trasher. It removes Estates at the start of a turn without using your Action. Pairs with Tent.
- **Ducat:** late +Buy (e.g. Province + Estate). Also feeds Destrier.
- **Mystic:** +1 Action lets you play a second Tent or a Stronghold alongside Tent. Not worth $5 in practice.
- **Steward:** the best-rated card on the board, but it competes with Tent for the Action. You'd only use it for early trashing (see plan B).
- **Garrison:** a worse Tent, because it doesn't come back to your hand. Its next-turn draw only matters with several gains a turn.
- **Wishing Well and Harbinger:** none. Harbinger putting Gold back on top is minor. Wishing Well can't name Tent profitably, because Tent is already in your hand when you play it.
- **Guide (Reckless):** a one-time +2 Cards +2 Actions, then it sits on the Tavern mat. None worth building around.
- **Exploration:** only pays on turns with no gain in the Buy phase. Hill Fort and Cabin Boy gains happen outside the Buy phase, so they don't stop it. It still almost never fires in a deck that buys every turn.

**Ratings check:**
- Tent is rated −2.17, against my view that it's the key card. On an average board Tent competes for the Action and its put-back is a minor bonus. Here no terminal is better than it, and the put-back makes it a permanent +$2.
- Steward (3.04) and Destrier (2.61) are the top-rated cards. They get less use here because Steward competes with Tent and Destrier needs a lab plan that's too slow.

**Plans considered:**
- **A. Tent + Cabin Boy money** (recommended).
  - Line: $6 by T4, Gold T4–T7, first Province about T8, 4 Provinces about T13–14.
  - Needs: 1 Tent, played by T3–4.
  - Weakness: it caps at one Province per turn.
- **B. Steward money** (the standard money baseline: Steward/Silver open, Steward used as +2 Cards).
  - Line: $6 about T5, first Province about T9, 4 Provinces about T15–16.
  - Weakness: Steward is weaker draw than Smithy.
- **C. Thin Destrier lab deck** (Ratcatcher/Steward trashing, about 4–5 Destriers, Cabin Boy/Mystic payload, Ducat Buys, Strongholds).
  - Line: about 5 buys of $5–6 before it draws its deck. First double-Province turn around T14 at best.
  - Weakness: its payload is one terminal plus $2 non-terminals, and it gets there too late.
- **D. Hill Fort + Exploration** (Hill Fort gains in the Action phase, skip buying, bank Coffers and Villagers).
  - Weakness: only 4 Hill Forts, at $5, behind 2 turns of the Forts pile. Skipping buys costs more than 1 Coffers + 1 Villager returns.

### Build lines
- **A (Tent + Cabin Boy money):**
  - T1–2: Cabin Boy / Tent.
  - T3: Cabin Boy + 3 Copper, draws a card, $4 → Cabin Boy.
  - T4: Tent + 2 Copper + Estate, plus $2 from Cabin Boy = $6 → Gold. Turn Forts (Garrison on top).
  - T5–7: Tent + 4 cards ≈ $5.5–7 → Gold/Gold/Cabin Boy. Keep turning Forts toward Hill Fort, and buy Hill Fort at $5 if it's on top.
  - T8+: ≈ $7–8 → Province on most turns.
  - Built turn: Tent $2 + Gold + Gold + Cabin Boy carry-over $2 ≈ $10, 1 Buy, so 6 VP per turn.
- **B (Steward money):**
  - T1–2: Steward / Silver.
  - The Steward hits its turn about half the time. Golds on T5–8, first Province about T9.
  - Built turn: ≈ $8, roughly every other turn.
- **C (Destrier lab, re-tested below):**
  - T1–2: Steward / Ratcatcher.
  - T3–6: trash, then Silver/Cabin Boy to reach $6.
  - T7–12: Destrier, Destrier (Ducat-assisted to $5), Gold, Destrier…
  - Built turn about T13–14: Tent $2 + 2 Gold + 2 Mystic/Cabin Boy ≈ $12–14 with 2 Buys. That's Province + Duchy, not 2 Provinces.

**Against their best plan:**
- **A against A (the mirror):** an even race. The edges are:
  - **Who controls the Forts pile:** you can only turn it forward, so whoever wants the next Fort in the cycle wins that fight.
  - **Strongholds:** both players' late Cabin Boys turn into 2 VP, and the 4 Strongholds can swing the Duchy dance.
  - **The penultimate Province.**
  
  Pick up 1–2 Ducats late for Province + Estate turns.
- **A against C:** C is still building when A has 3–4 Provinces. With no attacks, C can't slow A down, so A wins.
- **A against B:** A is about 1–2 turns faster to 4 Provinces.

**Challenged:**
- **Re-testing C:** I re-ran the lab plan with cheap Destriers via Ducat. Each discount costs a Buy and adds a weak card, and the payload stays capped by one terminal. C still doesn't reach $16 with 2 Buys before A has about 4 Provinces.
- **How A loses:** to an opponent who's luckier on $8 turns, or who wins the Forts-pile or Stronghold fight in the endgame. That's visible from a Tent opening, so mirror it.

**Recommendation:** Plan A, Tent + Cabin Boy money. With no attacks there is nothing to punish a money deck. Tent is the strongest use of the one Action, and no rival plan scores earlier or at a higher rate.

**Open:**
- **4/3** (take this under the tournament rule): Cabin Boy / Tent.
- **5/2:** Cabin Boy / Ratcatcher, then Tent on the next $3+. This is worse, because Tent arrives a shuffle late.

**Buy priorities:**
- **Shuffles 1–3:**
  - $8: Province from about T7–8.
  - $6–7: Gold.
  - $5: Hill Fort if it's on top, otherwise Cabin Boy.
  - $4: Cabin Boy (up to about 3).
  - $3: Silver.
  - $2: Ratcatcher (one).
  - A second Tent isn't needed. Its only use would come with Mystic.
- **Greening:**
  - With about 5 Provinces left: Stronghold at $6–7 (turn the pile to it), Duchy at $5.
  - From about 3 Provinces left: Cabin Boys trash themselves into Strongholds, Duchy at $6–7, Estate at $2–4.
  - Late $10+: Ducat-assisted Province + Estate.

**Ending:** The Province pile runs out, around T17–19 in a mirror. A three-pile ending is very unlikely, since the Forts pile is 16 cards and counts as a single pile.

**Watch out for:**
- **Your opponent turning the Forts pile.** It can take a Hill Fort or Stronghold off the top before your turn, and it decides whether your Cabin Boys can become Strongholds.
- **Playing Steward or Stronghold instead of Tent.** Tent then goes to your discard pile, and its loop is broken until you draw it again.
- **An opponent going for the lab plan.** Stay on money; don't follow them.

**Least sure of:** That Tent beats Steward and Gold as the X. The ratings disagree strongly with me here. The test is to simulate Tent + Cabin Boy money against Steward money with both aiming at Provinces, a fair comparison for simulators.

**Assumed:**
- **Reckless Guide:** when played, it goes to the Tavern mat, so it's never "discarded from play" and stays out of your deck. If you later call it, it's discarded from play at Clean-up and goes back to its pile. Doubling "put this on your Tavern mat" does nothing extra.
- **Destrier cost reduction:** cards gained at the start of the turn (Cabin Boy) and in the Action phase (Hill Fort) count toward it.
- **Forts pile:** turning the pile moves all copies of the top card to the bottom. It counts as one pile for the three-pile ending.
- **Players:** 2. With more players there are still no attacks, but the Forts pile turns unpredictably and the 4 Strongholds are spread thinner. Plan A still holds.
