## Beyond the key
- Tent puts itself on top of your deck at Clean-up, so it is in every hand as a permanent +$2: plausible. Tent goes onto the deck when discarded from play, and Clean-up discards before the redraw.
- Tent beats a Silver deck all game (5×17/16 = $5.3 against $6.0): doubtful. The comparison decks aren't defined consistently, and the "late deck" numbers ($8.4 against $8.7) show Tent losing ground as the deck improves.
- Cabin Boy can trash itself to gain a Stronghold when Stronghold is on top: plausible. Stronghold is a Duration, and only the top Fort can be gained. The same applies to Garrison, which the analysis doesn't note.
- Hill Fort and Cabin Boy gains happen outside the Buy phase, so they don't stop Exploration: plausible from Exploration's text. It undercuts the analysis's own dismissal of a Hill Fort/Exploration deck.
- Reckless Guide goes to the Tavern mat, so it's never discarded from play until it's called, then returns to its pile: plausible. A called Reserve is moved into play and is discarded at Clean-up, where Reckless returns it.
- Rotating moves all copies of the top Fort to the bottom, and Forts counts as one pile: plausible, from the split-pile rules.
- Mystic's +1 Action lets you play a second terminal alongside Tent, but it isn't worth $5: doubtful. Exploration Villagers also supply extra Actions, and the card text doesn't settle Mystic's value either way.

## Summary
The analysis builds Tent + Cabin Boy money. It dismisses Garrison, Exploration and Reckless Guide, and treats Hill Fort as an opportunistic $5 buy, while the designer's plan is Tent/Exploration into two Garrisons and then emptying the Hill Forts. It identifies the Ducat/Hill Fort → cheap Destrier mechanism but argues it is too slow and advises not contesting it, which is the designer's warned-against trap.
