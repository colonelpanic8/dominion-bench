# Analysis: Beggar, Coin of the Realm, Encampment, Forge, Hunting Grounds, Mandarin, Noble Brigand, Scholar, Scrying Pool, Trader

**Setup:** 2 players. No Colonies or Shelters, so the starting deck is the usual 7 Copper and 3 Estate. Openings split 4/3 or 5/2.

**Price points:**
- Potion: Scrying Pool costs $2 plus a Potion.
- $2: Beggar, Coin of the Realm, Encampment.
- $4: Trader, Noble Brigand.
- $5: Mandarin, Scholar, and Plunder (only once all 5 Encampments are gone).
- $6: Hunting Grounds.
- $7: Forge.

There is nothing worth buying at $3, and the $5 slot is weak.

**First-edition cards:** Mandarin and Noble Brigand are first-edition only.

**Board:** No card here gives +Buy, so the single buy can't score more than one Province a turn. The only way past that is Forge's extra gain. Hunting Grounds is the best draw on the board, and it makes Forge good. Trashing Hunting Grounds with Forge gains a $6 card plus a Duchy, or a Province plus a Duchy if you also trash an Estate. The open questions are whether the conditional villages (Encampment needs a Gold in hand, Coin of the Realm works once per shuffle) and Scrying Pool can support a real engine, or whether money with Hunting Grounds and Forge is faster.

**Key interactions:**
- **Forge + Hunting Grounds, trash Hunting Grounds alone or with Coppers** ($6): gain a Hunting Grounds back, and its trash trigger gains a Duchy. That is +3 VP without using the buy, and it trashes any Coppers you add. Each time it uses up 1 Hunting Grounds and 1 Duchy from the Supply. It works from about turn 10, on any turn where Forge and Hunting Grounds meet.
- **Forge + Hunting Grounds + Estate** ($8): gain a Province, plus a Duchy (or 3 Estates). That is +8 VP net, but you lose the Hunting Grounds. Forge can also trash Gold + Estate, or 4 Estates, for a Province.
- **Forge as a trasher:** 3 Estates gain a Gold or a Hunting Grounds. 4 Coppers + Estate gain a $2 card (Coin of the Realm, Encampment or Estate). The gain is mandatory, so Coppers alone just give you a Copper back.
- **Mandarin's on-gain topdeck on a 5/2:** buy Mandarin on the $5 turn. The 5 Coppers in play go back on top of your deck, so your next turn has $5 again. In effect, 5/2 becomes $5/$5/$2 before the second shuffle. Later in the game, buying Mandarin with Golds in play sets up a Province hand for next turn.
- **Trader + Hunting Grounds** (late): trash Hunting Grounds for 6 Silvers and a Duchy. Once you've started greening, those Silvers raise the money density of a money deck.

**Other cards:**
- **Encampment:** needs a Gold in hand to stay. Forge makes Gold from 3 Estates. Even when it returns to its pile, it was +2 Cards and +2 Actions for $2. Its best use is letting you play Hunting Grounds and then Forge in one turn.
- **Coin of the Realm:** call it after Hunting Grounds for +2 Actions, then play Forge. It's the cheap fix for that collision.
- **Scrying Pool:** wants a deck dense in Actions. The Actions here are Encampment and Hunting Grounds, and there's no good Copper trashing apart from the $7 Forge. Its attack mostly just sifts the opponent's top card.
- **Scholar:** a weaker Hunting Grounds in money decks. No village makes it worth the hand discard.
- **Noble Brigand:** steals Silver and Gold, and its attack also happens when you buy it. It's best against the money mirror, but it is a terminal that collides with Hunting Grounds.
- **Beggar:** its reaction answers Noble Brigand and Scrying Pool with 2 Silvers. The play effect adds Coppers, which is bad here.
- **Plunder:** in practice it's unreachable, because Encampments return to their pile.
- **Ratings:** these broadly agree with this reading. Scrying Pool (3.05) and Encampment/Plunder (3.0) are rated high, but their usual support is missing here: trashing for Scrying Pool, an easy Gold for Encampment. Forge (−0.81) and Trader (−3.99) rate low, but Hunting Grounds lifts both well above their average.

### Build lines

- **Hunting Grounds money + Forge** (4/3: Trader/Silver):
  - T3–4: Trader trashes an Estate for 2 Silvers, plus a Silver. The deck is now 15 cards: 7 Copper, 2 Estate, 5 Silver, Trader ($17 in total, about $5.7 per hand).
  - T5: Hunting Grounds. T6: Gold. T7: Gold, or a Province on $8. T8: second Hunting Grounds. T9: Province. T10: Forge.
  - From T11, buy a Province on most Hunting Grounds hands. A Forge turn with Hunting Grounds in hand (roughly 40–50% of Forge turns) adds a Duchy, or Province plus Duchy with an Estate, on top of the buy.
  - About 4 Provinces plus 1–2 free Duchies by T14–15.
- **Scrying Pool / Encampment / Hunting Grounds / Forge engine** (Potion/Silver):
  - T3: Scrying Pool. T4: Silver. T5: second Scrying Pool. T6: Gold. T7: Forge. T8: third Scrying Pool.
  - T9: Forge trashes Estate + 3 Coppers for an Encampment. T10: Hunting Grounds.
  - At T12 the deck has about 7 Actions in 18 cards, so Scrying Pool only draws about 1.6 cards per play.
  - The deck draws itself around T14–15.
  - Built turn: Forge on Hunting Grounds (Hunting Grounds + Duchy, 3 VP), second Forge on Hunting Grounds + Estate (Province + Duchy, 9 VP), and a Province buy. That's 12–18 VP.
- **Forge pile-out slog:** play Forge on Hunting Grounds every turn and drain the Duchy (8), Estate (8) and Hunting Grounds (10) piles.
  - Choosing 3 Estates on each Hunting Grounds trash empties the Estate pile after about 3 trashes.
  - Realistically it ends around T18 or later, and it only works if the opponent is also greening.

**Plans considered:**
- **Hunting Grounds money + Forge:** hits $6 on T5 and $8 from about T7. It needs 2 Hunting Grounds and 1 Forge. Its weakness is that Forge and Hunting Grounds are both terminals.
- **Scrying Pool engine:** reaches $8 and full draw only around T14. It needs about 4 Scrying Pools, Gold and Encampments. Its weaknesses are low Action density, no Copper trashing until Forge, and a single buy.
- **Forge pile-out slog:** has a low scoring rate and needs the opponent to cooperate. It works better as a finishing tool than as a plan.

**Against their best plan:**
- **Hunting Grounds money against the engine:** the engine needs to be online by about T14. By then, money has 3–4 Provinces, and Forge Duchies keep coming. The engine needs about 3 turns of 15 VP or more while staying ahead on Provinces, which my line doesn't show.
- **Money mirror:** it comes down to who gets Forge working first, and to Noble Brigand. A Noble Brigand bought late on a $4–5 hand hits a deck with 2–3 Golds about 60% of the time.

**Challenged:**
- I gave the engine Forge-made Gold for Encampment and a T7 Forge, and it still only draws its deck around T14. Without +Buy, Scrying Pool's draw has nothing to cash it in.
- The money pick loses if Hunting Grounds and Forge keep colliding. Coin of the Realm or one Encampment fixes that cheaply. It also loses if the opponent's Noble Brigand strips your Golds.

**Recommendation:** Play Hunting Grounds money with one Forge, and use Forge to turn Hunting Grounds into VP. It reaches Provinces as fast as any plan here, and from about T11 Forge adds 3–9 VP per Forge play without using the buy. It holds up against the engine on speed, and in the mirror it has the same tools.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Trader/Silver. On T3–4 Trader turns an Estate into 2 Silvers, and you can trash Trader itself later for 4 Silvers.
- **5/2:** Mandarin, then Trader or Silver on the repeated $5 turn, and Coin of the Realm or nothing at $2. This uses the Mandarin topdeck trick to get two $5 turns.
- **If going for the engine:** Potion/Silver on 4/3.

**Buy priorities:**
- First two shuffles: Hunting Grounds at $6 (×2), Gold at $6–7 after that, Silver at $3–5.
- From about T9: Province at $8, and one Forge at $7 once you own 2 Hunting Grounds.
- Add a Coin of the Realm or Encampment at $2–4 only to fix Hunting Grounds/Forge collisions.
- Greening:
  - Buy a Duchy instead of a Gold with 4 or fewer Provinces left.
  - Forge on Hunting Grounds whenever the two meet. Take Province + Duchy if you have an Estate to add. Choose 3 Estates once the Duchies are gone.
  - Late, Trader on Hunting Grounds gives 6 Silvers and a Duchy.

**Ending:** Most likely on Provinces. The Duchy pile can also empty, because Forge plus greening drains it. Hunting Grounds and Estates make a three-pile ending possible but slow, and it only becomes likely if both players run Forge.

**Watch out for:**
- **Opponent's Noble Brigand:** consider buying one at $4–5 yourself. A Trader in hand can also swap the Copper it gives you for a Silver.
- **Penultimate Province:** without +Buy you can't take two Provinces in one turn by buying, but Forge's extra Province plus your buy can take the last two in one turn. Count the opponent's Forge turns too.
- **What would change the plan:** if the opponent goes Scrying Pool, race them. Don't contest the Potion.

**Least sure of:** That the Scrying Pool engine is too slow. If someone thins their Coppers early (an early Forge, or Trader on Estates) and still draws their deck by about T12, the engine's 12–18 VP turns beat money. The test is a careful paper game of that line.

**Assumed:**
- Forge's gain is mandatory, so trashing only Coppers gains a Copper.
- Encampment goes back to its own pile, so Plunder is effectively unavailable.
- Scrying Pool's attack sifts both players' top cards.
- Mandarin and Noble Brigand are first-edition cards, read from the text given.
- Turn estimates come from rough paper games, not a simulation.
