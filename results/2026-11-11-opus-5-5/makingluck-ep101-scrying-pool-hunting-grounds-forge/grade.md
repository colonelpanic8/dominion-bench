## Beyond the key
- Forge on Hunting Grounds alone gains Hunting Grounds back plus a Duchy, and Hunting Grounds + Estate gains a Province plus a Duchy: plausible. Hunting Grounds costs $6, $6 + $2 = $8, and its on-trash gains a Duchy.
- Forge on Coppers alone must gain a $0 card: plausible. The gain is "exactly equal" and mandatory if a card at that cost exists (Copper, or a Curse).
- Mandarin on a 5/2 gives $5/$5/$2 before the second shuffle: plausible. The 5 Coppers in play go onto the deck and are redrawn at Clean-up, leaving 2 Coppers and 3 Estates for turn 3.
- Trader on Hunting Grounds late gives 6 Silvers plus a Duchy: plausible. Its cost is $6, and the Duchy comes from its on-trash.
- Coin of the Realm called after Hunting Grounds lets you play Forge in the same turn: plausible. It gives +2 Actions after an Action is played.
- Plunder is effectively unreachable because Encampments return to their pile: plausible. Encampments go back unless you reveal a Gold, so the pile rarely empties.
- Forge's extra Province plus the buy can take the last two Provinces in one turn: plausible. Forge gains independently of the Buy.
- Scrying Pool's attack "mostly just sifts the opponent's top card": doubtful. Paired with Noble Brigand, Scrying Pool can leave a revealed Gold or Silver on top for Noble Brigand to steal. The hosts found that to be its main strength here.

## Summary
The analysis finds the board's core: no +Buy, Hunting Grounds draw, and Forge as both the thinner and the only route past one Province a turn. Its Forge-on-Hunting-Grounds VP lines are sound. It falls for the Trader/Silver opening over Silver/Silver, uses Coin of the Realm only as a collision fix, and writes off the Scrying Pool deck by missing its Noble Brigand synergy, which testing showed was about equal.
