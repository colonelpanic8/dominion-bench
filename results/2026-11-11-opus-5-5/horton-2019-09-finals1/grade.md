## Beyond the key
- Ferry on Old Witch makes it a $3 card that Hermit can gain, so Old Witch is the core of the board: plausible. Ferry's -$2 applies to the whole Action pile and Hermit gains up to $3, so a Hermit-gained Witch works. The key's designer never discusses Old Witch, though, so whether a Curse war dominates his intended Bridge decks is untested.
- Sewers triggers when you trash a Curse from Old Witch's attack, as a free defence: plausible. Sewers fires on any trashing "other than with this", including on the opponent's turn.
- The Sewers trigger on the Hermit→Madman exchange is useless because your hand is already being discarded: wrong. The brief's text exchanges Hermit "at the end of your Buy phase", while the hand is still held, so Sewers can trash a card from hand. This is exactly the trick the designer's opening relies on.
- Madman played from a 5-card hand gives 8 cards and 2 Actions: plausible. 4 cards left in hand → +4 Cards.
- Diplomat's +2 Actions needs a hand of 4 or fewer, counting Diplomat: plausible. 3 cards left + 2 drawn = 5.
- Rogue can never trash Hireling because Hireling stays in play: plausible. Rogue only reveals the top 2 cards of the deck.
- "$9 plus Bridge's +$1 buys Province + Duchy + Estate" (and "$10 → Province + Duchy"): wrong. With one Bridge those cost $7 + $4 + $1 = $12 (Province + Duchy alone is $11), more than $10.
- Ferry's discount applies only on the owner's turn: plausible, from Ferry's text ("on your turns").

## Summary
The analysis turns the board into an Old Witch Curse war with Ferry and treats Hermit/Sewers as cleanup. It explicitly rejects the Bridge/Diplomat engine the designer rated strongest, even after working out the Outpost enabler, and it misreads the Hermit-exchange Sewers trick at the heart of the opening. What it builds is close to the designer's third option (Trove/Hireling money) and avoids the Madman megaturn trap, but a player following it would not play the way the designer intended.
