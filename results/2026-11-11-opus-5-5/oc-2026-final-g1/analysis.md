# Analysis: oc-2026-final-g1 (2 players, no Colonies, no Shelters)

**Board:** This board is about money. There's no trashing, and all three draw cards (Council Room, Patrol, Litter) cost $5, along with Jester. Litter is the only village. It is non-terminal (+2 Cards +2 Actions), but each play costs $1 of Debt, and it draws only one card net. Scoring is Provinces, plus 2VP Tunnels and Vineyards (which need Potion). The open questions were whether Tunnel or Market Square have anything to trigger them, and whether Jester is worth a slot.

**Key interactions:**
- **Border Guard + Tunnel:** Border Guard discards one of the two cards it reveals. That counts as discarding outside Clean-up, so a revealed Tunnel gains a Gold. With 2 Tunnels in 15 cards, a Border Guard play hits about 27% of the time. With 4 Tunnels it's about 48%, and higher with the Lantern (reveal 3).
- **Cage + Market Square:** Cage trashes itself when you next gain a Victory card, and that can happen the same turn. Each Market Square still in hand can then be discarded for a Gold. Cage is the only thing on the board that trashes, so this is a one-shot per $2 Cage.
- **Cage as an overflow bank:** on a turn when Council Room draws more than $8, play Cage, set aside up to 4 spare Treasures, then buy the Province. The set-aside cards come back on top of the next 5-card hand. Council Room's +Buy can pay for the $2 Cage.
- **Way of the Squirrel:** if you already hold $8 with one Buy, play Council Room as Squirrel. You get +2 Cards at end of turn instead of +4 now, and the opponent doesn't draw a card. Litter played as Squirrel adds no Debt.
- **Jester against greening or Tunnel-heavy decks:** a revealed Victory card gives them a Curse. A revealed Gold or Silver lets you take a copy, and a revealed Copper can go to them as junk. Watch the other direction: Jester discarding their Tunnel gives them a Gold along with the Curse, and their Jester does the same to you.

**Other cards:**
- **Patrol:** it pulls Victory cards out of the next 4 into hand and puts the rest back in any order, which cleans up the next hand. It's a reasonable second draw card. The downside is that it pulls Tunnels into hand, away from Border Guard.
- **Astrolabe:** pairs with Council Room for Province+Tunnel turns. It's an acceptable $3–4 filler in place of Silver.
- **Vineyard:** pairs only with a deck full of Actions, which needs Litter, Market Square and Border Guard. A money deck has 4–6 Actions, so each Vineyard is worth 1–2VP, and it costs a $4 Potion to unlock. Rejected.
- **Seaway:** gains a Market Square or Border Guard and puts +Buy on that pile. That's $5 for a $2–3 card. Weak here.
- **Litter:** best as the one village that lets Council Room and Jester both be played in the same turn.

**Plans considered:**
1. **Council Room big money (baseline):** Silver/Silver, Council Room on turn 3–4, Gold at $6–7, Provinces from about turn 7–8, 4 Provinces by about turn 14–15. Weakness: each Council Room play gives the opponent a card.
2. **Two Jesters with money:** Jester plays as a terminal Silver that attacks every time. It's played about 55% of turns, and over turns 3–16 that gives the opponent about 2 Curses plus Coppers, or Golds and Silvers for you. Weakness: no draw, so its own economy is slower than plan 1's.
3. **Litter / Council Room / Jester engine with Market Square for +Buy:** it has to buy about 7 cards costing $5, there's no trashing, and Litter only draws one card net. The worked line below has its first turn with two Provinces around turn 15 or later. Too slow.
4. **Tunnel / Border Guard / Cage / Market Square Gold flood:** a deck built mainly to gain Golds. The Gold gains are conditional and one-shot, so it's a side effect, not a plan.

### Build lines
- **Plan 1 (Council Room money):**
  - Turns 1–2: Silver, Silver. Turns 3–4: Council Room, Silver.
  - Turns 5–6: Council Room hands draw 9 cards for about $8, so Gold or Province. Second Council Room or Gold around turn 7.
  - Built turn: 9 cards, about $10, 2 Buys, so Province plus a Silver, Cage or Tunnel.
- **Recommended hybrid:**
  - Turns 1–2: Silver, Silver. Turns 3–4: Jester and Silver (or Council Room).
  - Turns 5–6: Gold and Council Room.
  - Turns 7–8: Council Room turn of about $9–10 gives Province plus a Cage. The other turn gets Gold.
  - Turns 9–10: one Litter, once you hold Jester and Council Room (3 terminals by the second Jester), then a second Jester.
  - Turns 10–16: a Province most turns. The opponent has taken about 2–3 Curses.
- **Engine:** turns 1–10 go to buying 3 Litters, 2 Council Rooms and 2 Jesters while 10 junk cards stay in the deck. Turns of about $14, minus $3–4 of Debt, arrive around turn 13–15.

**Against their best plan:** Their best plan is the same Council Room/Jester money deck. Against it, the hybrid keeps Council Room's draw and takes about −2 to −3 VP off them in Curses. Their deck ends up with Coppers while yours gets copies of their Golds.

Against plain Council Room money (plan 1), the hybrid wins on the attack, but plan 1 draws better. The swing is a Curse or two either way.

If they build a Tunnel-heavy deck, your Jester feeds them Golds. In that case lean toward Council Room, Patrol and Gold, and don't add a second Jester.

**Challenged:** I re-tested the Litter engine with Squirrel to absorb collisions and Market Square for +Buy. With no trashing, all-$5 pieces and $1 of Debt per Litter, it is still about two turns too slow.

The hybrid loses when Jester keeps hitting Coppers (giving junk the opponent barely notices), or when the opponent's lucky Council Room shuffles outpace a Jester that isn't drawing anything.

**Recommendation:** Play Council Room money with 1–2 Jesters as the second terminal and one Litter once you have 3 terminals. Use Squirrel to bank surplus draw, and pick up Cage, Tunnel and Border Guard with spare Buys or $2–4 hands. This holds up against a mirror and against plain Council Room money, and only a Tunnel-heavy opponent blunts it.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Silver/Silver. Astrolabe is acceptable on the $3.
- **5/2:** Jester/Border Guard. A Jester on turn 3–4 hits a deck that is 30% Estates. If you're second player, Council Room/Border Guard is the more solid choice.

**Buy priorities:**
- **First and second shuffles:** Jester, then Council Room, at $5. Gold at $6–7. Silver at $3–4.
- **Third shuffle:** Province at $8. Second Jester or a Litter at $5 once you have Council Room plus Jester. Cage with a spare Buy.
- **Greening:** Duchy at $5–7 once 4 or fewer Provinces are left. Late, at $3–4, buy Tunnel rather than Estate (2VP, and Border Guard can turn it into a Gold).

**Ending:** The Province pile runs out around turns 15–17. A three-pile ending is unlikely: Curses only come from Jester at roughly 0.3 per play, and the cheap piles aren't being drained.

**Watch out for:**
- Buying Tunnels early when the opponent has Jester. Jester discarding your Tunnel gives you a Gold, but also a Curse.
- Playing Council Room on a hand that already has $8 (use Squirrel).
- Litter's Debt in a deck that doesn't need it.
- With 3 or more players, Jester gets better and Council Room gets worse, so Patrol becomes the better draw card.

**Least sure of:** Whether Jester beats a second Council Room or Patrol as the second terminal. The test is to track Jester's results over the first 5 plays (Curses and Golds against Coppers). If most are Coppers, switch to Patrol or Gold.

**Assumed:**
- Cage triggers on the same turn if you gain a Victory card, and the cards come back to hand after the Clean-up draw.
- Squirrel's +2 Cards arrive after Clean-up, so the next hand is 7 cards.
- Jester's forced discard of a Tunnel triggers Tunnel's Gold.
- You must pay off Litter's Debt before you can buy anything, so in practice it costs $1 per play.
