## Beyond the key
- Talisman doubles bought Coppers (and Settlers, Raze, Talisman), and Travelling Fair can topdeck the copies: plausible. Talisman covers any non-Victory card costing $4 or less, and Travelling Fair applies to every card gained that turn.
- The lock needs a new Counting House every turn, so the Counting House pile may empty for ~8 Tower VP: plausible. The played Counting House sits in the discard pile until a reshuffle, and Tower counts any empty non-Victory Supply pile.
- Enchantress is beaten by playing a Settlers or Raze first: plausible. Enchantress only cancels the first Action played each turn.
- Haunted Woods barely hurts the lock because your hand is empty when you buy: plausible. Treasures are in play by then, so it only topdecks leftover cards.
- Farming Village fits badly because it keeps the first Copper it finds instead of discarding it: plausible. It reveals until a Treasure and puts that card in hand, so the Copper never reaches the discard pile for Counting House.
- In the mirror, whoever starts the lock first takes most of the Counting House pile: plausible. The pile has 10 cards and each lock turn uses one.
- 5/2 opening Counting House/Raze: doubtful. On turn 3–4 the discard pile holds few Coppers, so an early Counting House is weak. The analysis itself says the card struggles early.
- "With N Coppers you make about $N+3" and "Bank pays about N+3": doubtful. Bank pays $1 per Treasure in play, about N+1 with N Coppers, and nothing in the build accounts for the +3.

## Summary
It finds the right combo, the right Bank finisher and the right counters to the attacks. But it builds slowly through Talisman, Silver and a single Bank instead of just buying Coppers, and stacking Banks never comes up. So it expects a T15–17 finish against the key's 8–9 and treats money as a live rival when the combo should win easily.
