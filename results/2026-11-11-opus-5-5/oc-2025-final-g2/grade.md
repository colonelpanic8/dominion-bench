## Beyond the key
- Remodel can't gain Fortune because its cost includes Debt: plausible. Fortune costs $8 plus 8 Debt, and "costing up to $X" can't cover a Debt component.
- Buying Fortune with 2 Gladiators in play also gains 2 Golds: plausible. That is Fortune's on-gain text.
- Each unmatched Gladiator trashes one from the pile, so Fortune appears around turns 8–10: plausible. The mechanism matches the card text, though the timing is a guess.
- Farmhands' on-gain can set aside a terminal to play next turn, which fixes collisions: plausible. Its text allows any Action or Treasure from hand.
- Durations still in play from last turn (Amulet, Grotto, Enchantress) count toward Peddler's discount: plausible. Peddler counts Action cards in play, and Durations stay in play.
- Enchantress turns the first Action into a cantrip, blocking a Remodel-to-Province turn: plausible. That follows directly from its text.
- Over-thinning hurts Foray, since Copper and Estate supply the distinct names: plausible. Foray needs 3 different names.
- Squirrel's +2 Cards make a 7-card next hand: plausible. "At the end of this turn" comes after the Clean-up draw.
- Transmute isn't worth a Potion when Remodel handles Estates: plausible. It matches the grader note that the winner ignored Transmute.
- Enchantress overrides a Way choice: doubtful. Both are replacement effects on the played card, and the analysis itself flags this as unconfirmed. The affected player probably chooses.
- Gladiator money with Fortune as the core economy: doubtful as a plan. The card text works, but the key says the winner essentially ignored Gladiator/Fortune.

## Summary
The analysis gets Remodel (including Peddler's $8 printed cost), Foray Loots and Squirrel right, and avoids Monastery. But it builds a Gladiator/Fortune money deck, sees Grotto as a one-copy smoother rather than the core draw, and explicitly rejects the Farmhands/Peddler engine the experts and the winner used. A player following it would land near the winner's Remodel-into-Province endgame by a different, weaker route.
