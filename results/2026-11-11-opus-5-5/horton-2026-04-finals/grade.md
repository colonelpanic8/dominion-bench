## Beyond the key
- Governor's remodel turns a Gold into a Province: plausible. You get the +$2 version, so $6 → exactly $8.
- The opponent's remodel can't upgrade a Gold because no Supply card costs exactly $7: plausible. True of this kingdom's piles.
- The opponent's remodel can turn an Estate into an Experiment pair, and an unplayed Experiment can be remodeled into a Governor: plausible. $2 → $3 (Experiment's on-gain gives a second one), and $3 + $2 = $5.
- Way of the Owl rescues a Throne Room that has no Governor to double: plausible. Any Action can be played as "draw until 6".
- Owl-playing Leprechaun dodges the Hex but loses the Gold: plausible. A Way replaces the card's instructions entirely.
- Locusts on a Copper gives a Curse: plausible. That is Locusts' text.
- Tactician discards your held Governors before Clean-up, so they can't be used for Friendly: plausible as a rule reading. Friendly triggers at the start of Clean-up from hand, but the conclusion that this makes Tactician unplayable is what the designer rejects.
- Advisor is not worth buying because the opponent takes your best card: plausible. Governor's draw mode is non-terminal +3 Cards, though Advisor still works as filler draw.
- A built T11 turn makes about 4 Provinces: doubtful. Optimistic count. It assumes 7 Governor plays from 5 Governors, 1 Throne Room and 1 Flagship that all collide in hand, and it leaves out Flagship's +$2.

## Summary
The analysis builds a sound Governor/Friendly/Throne Room engine with Forager payload, which matches Horton's second idea. But it explicitly dismisses Tactician, the card he designed the board around, and so fell into the same trap as both finalists. It also misses the Throne Room/Flagship-on-Leprechaun multi-Wish trick and Owl refilling after Tactician.
