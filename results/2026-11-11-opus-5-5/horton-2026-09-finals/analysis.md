**Kingdom analysis: River Shrine, Hostelry, Change, Alchemist, Stonemason, Vineyard, Witch's Hut, Weaver, Footpad, Artificer · Exploration, Triumph, Flourishing Trade**

### 1. Inventory

- **Prices before the Prophecy:**
  - $2: Stonemason
  - $3+Potion: Alchemist; $0+Potion: Vineyard
  - $4: River Shrine, Hostelry, Change, Weaver, Exploration (Project), Potion
  - $5: Witch's Hut, Footpad, Artificer
  - Triumph is an Event costing $0 + 5 Debt.
- **Price gaps:** nothing worth buying at $3 except Silver, and nothing above $5.
- **Non-Supply pile:** Horses, from Hostelry's on-gain.
- **Special rules:** Debt (Change, Triumph), Potion, Coffers (Footpad, Exploration), Villagers (Exploration), and Footpad's global rule.
- **Footpad's global rule:** it applies whether or not anyone buys Footpad. Every card you gain during an Action phase gives +1 Card.
- **Prophecy:** Flourishing Trade starts with 5 Sun. River Shrine is the only Omen, so it turns on after both players' River Shrine plays add up to 5. Once on, cards cost $1 less, and you may use Action plays as Buys. Debt costs are not reduced.
- **Starting deck:** 7 Copper and 3 Estate. Under the tournament rule you choose your split, 4/3 or 5/2.

---

**Board:** This is a gaining engine that scores with Triumph. Thanks to Footpad's rule, every gain in the Action phase also draws a card. So Weaver, Hostelry's Horses, Artificer, Change and Stonemason all draw while they build, and Witch's Hut and Artificer discarding a Weaver play it for free. Triumph turns a turn with many gains into a large amount of VP. The open point is that Triumph's VP is capped by the 8 Estates both players share. There is no +Buy until Flourishing Trade turns on, so the race is really for those Estates.

**Key interactions:**
- **Footpad's rule + Weaver.** Weaver gains 2 Silvers and draws 2 (a terminal Moat that also gives two Silvers), or gains a $4 card and draws 1. After Flourishing Trade, "up to $4" reaches Gold, Duchy, Witch's Hut, Footpad and Artificer.
- **Weaver + discard outlets.** Witch's Hut (+4 Cards, discard 2) and Artificer (discard N) can discard Weavers. Each discarded Weaver is played for free, using no Action, and gains 1–2 cards that each draw. If Witch's Hut discards Weaver plus any other Action, the opponent also gains a Curse. That makes Witch's Hut a real junker here; on most boards it rarely curses.
- **Hostelry gained in the Action phase.** Gaining it draws 1. Each Treasure you discard to it becomes a Horse, and each Horse gained draws 1. Your hand size stays level, Coppers turn into Horses (+2 Cards, +1 Action each), and you gain a village. Weaver, Artificer, Change (Copper → Hostelry, +4 Debt) and Stonemason's overpay all supply Hostelry gains.
- **Artificer + Footpad's rule.** Artificer's gain goes onto your deck and the +1 Card draws it at once. So "discard 3 (2 after the Prophecy), gain Hostelry" puts a village in hand mid-turn.
- **River Shrine on turns you don't buy.** It trashes 2 cards, then gains a card costing up to $4 at Clean-up ($5 after the Prophecy, which reaches Gold and $5 Kingdom cards). If you don't play your Treasures, you can gain Hostelry at Clean-up and discard those Coppers for Horses. It also advances the Prophecy.
- **Triumph, the payload.** It scores +1 VP per card gained this turn, counting its own Estate. A built turn with 7 gains scores 8 VP for $5. After the Prophecy, spare Actions become Buys, so 2–3 Triumphs at $5 each score about 8+9+10 VP. Each Triumph takes an Estate, and the 8 Estates in the pile are the only fuel both players have.
- **Change + Triumph's Debt.** Unpaid Triumph Debt turns Change into +$3. With no Debt, Change trashes a Triumph Estate to gain a Province (6 Debt, 7 Debt before the Prophecy), or a Copper to gain Hostelry.

**Other cards:**
- **Stonemason:** overpaying is its best partner. $2 + $4 overpay gains 2 Hostelries or Weavers. After the Prophecy it's $1 + $3, or $1 + $4 for 2 Artificers or Witch's Huts. Every one of those gains counts toward Triumph that turn. Played, it trashes a Copper cleanly, since nothing costs less than $0. Don't use it on Estates: you would have to gain 2 Coppers.
- **Footpad (card):** its value is +2 Coffers to pay Triumph Debt. As an attack it is weak against Weaver decks, because a Weaver discarded to it gets played for free.
- **Exploration:** pairs with River Shrine turns and with gaining in the Action phase (no Buy-phase gain gives +1 Coffers and +1 Villager). It conflicts with Triumph, so it is optional filler at most.
- **Alchemist:** with Potion it's only a Laboratory. Hostelry and Horses already do that job, so skip it.
- **Vineyard:** pairs with an Action-dense deck, but each Vineyard needs a Potion in play. Late on, one Vineyard per Potion is about 4–5 VP, which is marginal. Skip it unless you already hold a Potion.
- **Ratings check:** Triumph (2.74), Hostelry (2.37) and River Shrine (2.13) agree with this read. Footpad (−0.42) is low because its global rule is free to everyone, so buying the card adds only Coffers. Witch's Hut (0.42) and Weaver (0.59) rate well below their value here, because this board feeds each of them what it lacks elsewhere. Exploration (−1.42) and Alchemist (−1.13) match my read.

### Build lines

**Weaver / Hostelry / Triumph engine (4/3 opening)**
- **T1–2:** River Shrine, Silver.
- **T3–4:** The River Shrine hand trashes Estate and Estate (or Estate and Copper) and buys nothing. River Shrine gains Weaver at Clean-up. The other hand (Silver + 3 Coppers = $5) buys Artificer.
- **T5–6:** Weaver gains Hostelry in the Action phase, discards 2 Coppers for 2 Horses and draws 3. River Shrine gains Hostelry at Clean-up, discarding the unplayed Coppers for Horses. Buy Witch's Hut at $5, or Stonemason with $4 overpaid at $6.
- **T7–10:**
  - Aim for 3 Weavers, 3–4 Hostelries, Witch's Hut, Artificer and 1 Change, plus Horses from every Hostelry.
  - The Prophecy usually turns on around T8–11. Both players play River Shrine; if only you do, it's around T11.
  - Start using Triumph once a turn gains 5 or more cards, which scores 6+ VP and beats a Province on cost.
- **Built turn (about T12, after the Prophecy):** Hostelry, Hostelry, Artificer, Witch's Hut and Weaver.
  - Artificer discards Weaver and 1 Copper; the free Weaver gains Hostelry plus 1 Horse.
  - Artificer then gains a $2–3 card onto the deck and Footpad's rule draws it.
  - Witch's Hut discards Weaver and a Horse: Weaver plays for free and gains 2 Silvers, and the opponent gains a Curse.
  - The Weaver in hand gains a Duchy.
  - That is about 7 gains, $12–15, and 3 Buys. Buy Triumph, pay 5 Debt, buy Triumph again: about 8 + 9 = 17 VP. With $15, a third Triumph brings it to about 27 VP.

**Big Money + Footpad**
- **T1–2:** Footpad (5/2), or Silver/Silver.
- Then Gold at $6, Province at $8.
- First $6 around T4–5, first $8 around T6–8, and 4 Provinces around T15–16. The attack plays about 1 turn in 3.
- Triumph is worth about 2 VP to this deck (one gain), so its ceiling is Provinces at about 6 VP per turn.

**Weaver money-slog (no villages)**
- Weaver/Silver, then more Weavers, Silvers and Provinces, with Triumph on turns of 2–3 gains for 3–4 VP.
- It is better than BM in the early greening phase, but terminal collision caps it at about one Province-equivalent per turn.

| Plan | Starts scoring | Can end by | Peak VP/turn | VP ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weaver/Hostelry/Triumph engine | ~T10–11 | ~T15–17 (Estates + Curses + a third pile, or Provinces) | 15–25 (2–3 Triumphs) | Its share of 8 Triumph Estates (~10 VP each) + Provinces, Duchies |
| BM + Footpad | ~T7 | ~T20 | 6 | Provinces |
| Weaver slog | ~T8 | ~T20 | 6–8 | Provinces + small Triumphs |

**Plans considered:**
- **Engine** (above). Gaining and drawing builds it about two cards per turn from T3. It needs about 3 Weavers, 3+ Hostelries, 1–2 discard outlets and Triumph timing. Its weakness is that the 8 Estates are shared; if the opponent Triumphs first, your ceiling falls toward Provinces.
- **BM + Footpad.** About 4 Provinces by T15–16, with the attack played about 1 turn in 3. It needs nothing scarce. Its weakness is that it can't score faster than one Province per turn while the engine takes 15+ VP turns from about T12, and Witch's Hut Curses hurt it.
- **Weaver slog.** About 1 Province-equivalent per turn. It needs only Weavers. Its weakness is that it has no villages, so terminals collide and its per-turn VP stays capped.

**Against their best plan:**
- **Against BM:** BM leads by about 12–18 VP around T11. The engine's first Triumph turns (about 17 VP each) overtake it by T13–14. The engine also chooses when the game ends, using Estates, Curses and a third pile.
- **In the mirror:** the 8 Estates and the 10 Curses decide it. Whoever reaches 2 Triumphs per turn first takes most of the Estates.
  - Start Triumphing as soon as your opponent does, even at 6–7 VP, because each Estate they take is gone for you.
  - Get the Prophecy turned on sooner, by playing River Shrine, if your deck has more spare Actions than theirs.
- **Runner-up (BM + Footpad) against the engine:** it loses, unless the engine stalls on Curses or the opponent ignores Triumph.

**Challenged:**
- **Did I underrate money?** Going first, Footpad discarding the opponent down to 3 does deny their early $5s. But Weaver plays for free when discarded, and gains draw cards, so the attack bounces off this engine. BM's 6 VP per turn can't match it.
- **How the pick loses:** you build too long while the opponent Triumphs Estates away at 6–8 VP each and closes the game on piles (Estates + Curses + Hostelry). The opponent can read this from a River Shrine/Weaver opening, so watch the Estate count.

**Recommendation:** Build the Weaver/Hostelry/Triumph engine. It outpaces BM by about T13, and its mirror weakness (the Estate race) can be managed with Triumph timing. The rival plans have no way to score more than about 6 VP per turn.

**Open:**
- **4/3 (preferred):** River Shrine / Silver. You get trashing plus a free $4 gain on each turn you don't buy.
- **5/2:** Artificer / Stonemason. Footpad / Stonemason is a reasonable alternative going first.

**Buy priorities:**
1. **First two shuffles:** River Shrine gains Weaver or Hostelry. Buy Artificer or Witch's Hut at $5, Stonemason with $4 overpaid at $6 (2 Hostelries or Weavers), and 1 Change. Use Weaver to gain Hostelry when your hand has Coppers to turn into Horses, and 2 Silvers when it doesn't. Play River Shrine every time you can.
2. **Third shuffle on:** get a second discard outlet. Stop buying Silver once Weavers flood them in.
3. **Triumph:** start once a turn gains 5+ cards, or as soon as the opponent starts. After the Prophecy, turn spare Actions into extra Triumphs.
4. **Greening:** use Weaver to gain Duchy or Gold. Use Change to turn Triumph Estates into Provinces once the Estates are gone.

**Ending:**
- The Estates (8, drained by Triumph) and the Curses (10, from mirrored Witch's Huts) go first.
- The third pile is likely Hostelry or Weaver (10 each), so expect a three-pile ending around T15–18.
- The Provinces are unlikely to run out first. Count piles every turn.

**Watch out for:**
1. The opponent Triumphing first. Once the Estates are gone, Triumph scores nothing.
2. Footpad attacks against your Weavers help you; your Footpad attacks help a Weaver opponent.
3. Debt. Triumph Debt blocks next turn's buying but not your Action-phase gains, and Change spends it as +$3.

**Least sure of:**
- **4/3 vs 5/2.** I prefer River Shrine's trashing and gaining over a $5 opener. The test is to play both openings to T8 and compare how many Weavers and Hostelries each deck has.
- **When the Prophecy turns on.** If the opponent never plays River Shrine, it may come around T11+, which delays multiple Triumphs per turn.

**Assumed:**
- Footpad's rule fires for any player's gain during any Action phase. So an opponent's Weaver, played after being discarded to my Footpad, draws them a card. I'm reading "an Action phase" literally.
- Overpaying for Stonemason and Flourishing Trade's cost reduction apply as described above.
- Villagers can feed Flourishing Trade's Action-to-Buy conversion. This is a minor point.
- Triumph's Debt isn't reduced by the Prophecy, and Debt must be paid before the next buy.
- Horses count as Action cards for Vineyard while they are in your deck.
