## Beyond the key
- Aristocrat stack (4 in play: +3 Actions, +3 Cards, +$3, +3 Buys; 5th gives +3 Actions again): plausible. Follows from the card's 1/5, 2/6, 3/7, 4/8 table.
- First Mate plays all Aristocrats in hand for one Action, then redraws to 6: plausible. Per card text. The assumption that it can also play Aristocrats drawn mid-resolution is unverified.
- About 60% of 5-card hands from a 20-card deck with 3 Estates hold a Victory card: plausible. 1 − C(17,5)/C(20,5) ≈ 0.60.
- Ritual on a Gold nets +5 VP, on a $5 Action +4, on a Copper −1: plausible. +$cost VP minus the Curse.
- Ritual on an Estate "nets only +1": wrong. +2 VP tokens, minus the Estate's 1 VP, minus the Curse, is net 0.
- Tactician should be skipped: doubtful. It gives +5 Cards, +1 Action and +1 Buy next turn, which is strong in a deck that can't thin (with the Flag, an 11-card hand), and both finalists bought it.
- Corsair is near-blank against an Aristocrat engine: plausible. It only hits Silver and Gold.
- Ghost Town works the turn you gain it: plausible. It's gained to hand during the Buy phase, so it can be played in that turn's Night phase, and its effect lands at the start of the next turn.
- Three-pile ending on Aristocrats, Curses and Eggs/Ghost Towns: plausible. Jester and Ritual both drain Curses.

## Summary
It reads the build the commentators predicted (Aristocrat/Lab/Ghost Town/Alley with Jester, Egg and Crop Rotation) and avoids an endless Flag war. It misses the board's central interaction: trashing Flag Bearer with Ritual takes the Flag and scores 4 VP. Because it writes Ritual off as a late finisher, it doesn't anticipate the Ritual/Flag fight that defined the game.
