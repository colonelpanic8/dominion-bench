# Kingdom analysis: Horton 2026-04 finals

**Board:** The board is built around Governor. It costs $5 and doesn't use up your Action. Each play gives you +3 Cards, a Gold, or "trash a card, gain one costing exactly $2 more", which turns a Gold into a Province. Friendly is on the Governor pile, so a Governor still in your hand at Clean-up can be discarded to gain another Governor for free. Expect both players to fight over the 10 Governors in the first shuffles. Forager gives the only real +Buy and gets stronger as Treasures pile up in the trash. Flagship and Throne Room double a Governor. The one plain village is Merchant Camp, which goes back on top of your deck each turn. Leprechaun gives you a Gold plus a Hex, or a Gold plus a Wish if exactly 7 cards are in play. What's still open is how far beyond plain Governor-money the support cards are worth taking.

**Key interactions:**
- **Governor + Friendly:** early on, don't play a Governor you've drawn. Keep it in hand and gain another Governor at Clean-up. That's a free $5 card, and it still leaves the rest of the hand to buy something. Keeping it beats playing it for draw or Gold until about turn 7–8, because Governors you gain go on to gain more, and every one you take is one the opponent doesn't get. The pile should be empty by about turn 8–9, split roughly 5–5 if both players do this.
- **Governor makes Golds, then turns them into Provinces:** Gold mode now, then trash a Gold for a Province later (exactly $6 to $8). Expect 1–2 Provinces per turn from about turn 10, without needing $8. You can also trash Silver for a Duchy or Governor, Estate for a $4 card, Experiment for a Governor, and Copper for an Estate at the end.
- **Throne Room or Flagship + Governor:** the Governor runs twice, so 2 Golds, or 2 Golds trashed for 2 Provinces. Throne Room on a Governor nets +1 Action, so it doubles as a village.
- **Forager and the shared trash:** after the first Copper is trashed, Forager trashes a card for free. Once Governor has trashed a Gold (and the opponent maybe a Silver or an Anvil), each Forager makes +$2–3 and a Buy. That's the money for a second Province or a Duchy.
- **Leprechaun as the 7th card in play:** you get a Gold and a Wish. The Wish puts a Gold straight into your hand, and a Governor played afterwards turns it into a Province. Throne Room on Leprechaun at exactly 7 gives 2 Golds and 2 Wishes. A drawn Governor deck with a Merchant Camp makes hitting exactly 7 doable.

**Other cards:**
- **Merchant Camp:** the only village. It comes back on top of your deck each turn, so one copy is in nearly every hand, ready for a Flagship or Leprechaun and counting toward the 7.
- **Experiment:** best with Anvil, since any $4 gain brings a pair. It's also Governor fodder (trash it for a Governor or a Duchy). Throne Room on it gives +4 Cards.
- **Anvil:** Experiment pairs and $4 cards. Its distinct name in the trash adds $1 to Forager. It's a minor opener.
- **Way of the Owl:** a fallback. Play Leprechaun as Owl when the count isn't 7, or Advisor or Tactician as draw-to-6.
- **Tactician:** a terminal +Buy that throws away a hand with Governors in it (and it isn't Clean-up, so Friendly doesn't trigger). Nothing here helps it.
- **Advisor:** none. The opponent picks the discard. Skip it.

### Build lines
- **Governor engine (recommended), going first:**
  - **T1–2:** Governor and nothing.
  - **T3:** Governor, 3 Copper and an Estate. Keep the Governor and buy Forager with the $3. At Clean-up, Friendly gains a second Governor.
  - **T4:** $3–4 buys Silver.
  - **T5:** Forager trashes an Estate (+$2 and +Buy). Keep the Governor for a third one.
  - **T6:** Governor, 3 Copper and a Silver make $5. Buy a Governor and gain another, for about 5 total.
  - **T7–8:** Keep gaining until the pile is gone. Add Flagship or Throne Room at $4, plus 1 Merchant Camp.
  - **T8–9:** Chain the Governors: draw mode until your deck is drawn, then Gold mode for 2 Golds and $6–7 for a Flagship or Gold.
  - **T10+:** Trash 1–2 Golds in hand for Provinces with Governors (doubled with Flagship or Throne Room), and use Forager's +Buy to buy a Province or Duchy.
  - **Built turn:** about $10–13 plus 2 Gold trashes, with 2 Buys. That's 2 Provinces, or 2 Provinces and a Duchy.
- **Governor-money:** T1–2 Governor and nothing. Gain Governors (buying and Friendly) and Golds. Play them in Gold mode, then trash Golds for Provinces and buy Provinces at $8. There's no +Buy and no doubling, so it settles at about 1–1.5 Provinces per turn from about T9–10.
- **No Governor (Big Money + Leprechaun or Tactician):** Silver/Silver or Leprechaun/Silver, first $8 about T8–9. Every Governor the opponent plays gives this deck a Silver or a card, but it hands over the whole pile. One Province per turn at best, with Hexes on top.

**Plans considered:**
- **Governor engine:** as above. It needs about 5 Governors (contested), 1 Forager, 1–2 Flagship or Throne Room, and 1 Merchant Camp. Its weakness is losing the Governor split.
- **Governor-money:** simpler and greens a turn earlier, but it tops out lower with no +Buy.
- **No-Governor money:** gives up the best card and the pile. It loses to either Governor plan.

**Against their best plan (the mirror):** the Governor split decides most of it. Going first gives about a 5–5 or 6–4 edge. After the split, the deck with Forager and doublers out-scores the plain version.
- **What your Governors hand the opponent:**
  - Draw mode gives them +1 Card, which can be a Governor they keep for Friendly. Use it only when you need it.
  - Gold mode gives them a Silver, which their own Governors can later trash for a Governor or Duchy.
  - The trash-and-gain mode gives them "exactly $1 more". That's mostly harmless (Estate for an Experiment pair, Silver for a $4 card), but it's free for them.
- **Against Governor-money:** money starts scoring about a turn sooner, but the engine's doubled trashes and +Buy catch up around T11–12, and the engine can then take the last Provinces. You don't need to panic-green.

**Challenged:** I rejected a Leprechaun/Throne Room/Wish plan as a stand-alone plan and re-tested it:
- **Re-test:** at exactly 7 in play it is the strongest payload on the board (2 Golds and 2 Wishes, with the Wish-Golds trashed for Provinces the same turn). But it needs the Governor draw underneath it anyway, so it's an add-on: one Leprechaun around T8–10, with Owl as the fallback when the count is wrong. Played early (Gold plus Hex), Hexes like Plague, Misery and Locusts cost too much.
- **How the pick loses:**
  - Losing the Governor split 4–6, especially going second.
  - Playing Governors early instead of keeping them for Friendly.
  - Leaning on draw mode, which feeds the opponent.

**Recommendation:** go for the Governor engine. Take Governors first and keep them for Friendly while the pile lasts. Add Forager, 1–2 Flagship or Throne Room and a Merchant Camp. Then build Golds in Gold mode and trash them for Provinces. It holds up against the best reply (the same plan) because it takes its share of the pile as early as possible and has the higher ceiling once the pile is gone.

**Open:**
- **5/2 (preferred):** Governor and nothing. There's no useful $2 card, so don't buy Copper. Taking the split lets Friendly start on T3 in the race for the pile.
- **4/3, if you're stuck with it:** Silver/Forager for reliable $5 on T3–4. Flagship/Forager is the greedier alternative, since Flagship doubles the Forager.

**Buy priorities:**
- **Shuffles 1–2:** Governor at $5+, and keep any Governor you draw for Friendly. Get 1 Forager. Silver at $3–4 until you hit $5.
- **Once the pile is gone (~T8):** Flagship or Throne Room at $4, 1 Merchant Camp, then Gold at $6–7 if you can't trash anything for a Province. Optionally 1 Leprechaun.
- **Greening:** start trashing Golds for Provinces about T10. Turn Silvers into Duchies once 4 or fewer Provinces are left. Trash Coppers for Estates and use Forager's Buy for Estates on the last turns.

**Ending:** most likely the Provinces run out around T14–16. Governors empty early and count as one pile. Duchies (bought, plus Silver trashes) could become the second. Experiments only drain if people gain them and don't play them. A three-pile ending is possible but unlikely. Watch the Penultimate Province Rule: count the opponent's Golds in hand and Governor plays, not just their $.

**Watch out for:**
- The Governor count: if you're falling behind in the split, buy Governors at every $5.
- Draw mode feeding the opponent a Governor while the pile lasts.
- Hexes if you play Leprechaun when the count isn't exactly 7. Use Owl instead.
- With 3 or more players, each Governor you play gifts every opponent, and the 10-card pile spreads thinner. That makes the Friendly race and the 5/2 opening even more important.

**Least sure of:** whether Friendly triggers once per Governor in hand or only once per Clean-up. I assumed once per card. If it's once per turn, the pile drains about half as fast, keeping Governors matters a little less, and 4/3 Forager/Silver gets closer to 5/2 Governor. The second doubt is how often a drawn Governor deck really hits exactly 7 for Leprechaun. Playing out a few T9–10 turns would show it.

**Assumed:**
- Friendly discards from hand, not from play, as covered above.
- Throne Room on Experiment still plays it twice even though the first play returns it to the pile.
- Flagship replays the next non-Command Action whether that's this turn or next.
- Leprechaun counts itself and any Durations still in play from last turn among the 7.
- Ratings match my view for Governor (5.5), Throne Room and Forager. I rate Friendly (−0.11), Merchant Camp (−2.39) and Leprechaun (−1.37) higher here than their averages, for the reasons above, and Tactician and Advisor low, as rated.
