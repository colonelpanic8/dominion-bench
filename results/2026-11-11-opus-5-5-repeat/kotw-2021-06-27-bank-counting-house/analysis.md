# Kingdom analysis: Bank, Counting House, Enchantress, Farming Village, Haunted Woods, Horse Traders, Jester, Raze, Settlers/Bustling Village, Talisman · Travelling Fair, Tower

## 1. Inventory

- **Kingdom piles:** Raze $2, Settlers/Bustling Village $2/$5 (a split pile, with Settlers on top), Enchantress $3, Farming Village $4\*, Horse Traders $4\*, Talisman $4\*, Counting House $5\*, Haunted Woods $5, Jester $5, Bank $7.
  - \*These four are first-edition-only cards (Hinterlands, Cornucopia and Prosperity 1st ed.).
- **Landscapes:**
  - **Travelling Fair** (Event, $2): +2 Buys. For the rest of the turn, cards you gain may go on top of your deck.
  - **Tower** (Landmark): at game end, 1 VP for each non-Victory card you own from an empty Supply pile.
- **Setup:** no Colonies, no Shelters, 2 players. The only token-like mechanic is Durations (Enchantress, Haunted Woods). Nothing is a junker except Jester's conditional Curse.
- **Price points:**
  - The $2 slot is good: Raze, Settlers, and Travelling Fair.
  - $4 is crowded. That is Talisman's range, and Talisman doubles anything bought at $4 or less.
  - $5 has three terminals.
  - Above $5 there is only Bank at $7.
- **Starting deck and splits:** 7 Copper and 3 Estate, so turns 1–2 split 4/3 or 5/2. There are **46 Coppers in the Supply** (60 − 14).

## 2–4. Analysis

**Board:**
- Tower turns the Copper pile into a Victory pile. Copper costs $0 and isn't a Victory card, so once the Copper pile is empty, every Copper you own is worth 1 VP. That includes your 7 starting ones.
- Travelling Fair sells +1 net Buy for $2, any number of times per turn.
- Talisman makes every Copper buy gain an extra Copper for each Talisman in play. Talisman also doubles itself, so its own pile drains fast and also scores with Tower.
- Counting House and Bank turn a deck full of Copper into a lot of money.
- So this is a **Copper slog**, not a Province race. What's open:
  - Can the flood happen fast enough?
  - What empties the third pile?
  - Does the opponent contest it?

**Key interactions:**
- **Talisman + Travelling Fair + Copper (Tower VP):**
  - Buy Travelling Fair k times for $2k. That leaves 1 + k Buys.
  - Spend every Buy on Copper. Each Copper buy gains 1 + T Coppers, where T is the number of Talismans in play.
  - Example: $4.5 with 2.4 Talismans in play → 2 Travelling Fairs → 3 Buys × 3.4 ≈ **10 Coppers (10 VP) a turn**, starting around turn 8.
- **Talisman + Talisman + Tower:** buying a Talisman with T in play gains 1 + T. The pile of 10 empties by about turn 7, and every Talisman is then 1 VP. Contesting this pile matters most.
- **Counting House + Travelling Fair + Copper flood** (the first-edition combo listed in `combos.md`):
  - Bought Coppers land in your discard pile.
  - Buy Counting House on a Travelling Fair turn and put it on top of your deck. Next turn it pulls every Copper back into your hand.
  - Example: Counting House pulls 10–20 Coppers → $14–25. With Travelling Fair, that's 1–2 Provinces plus more Coppers.
  - Coppers you discard to Horse Traders also feed Counting House.
- **Bank + Counting House hand:**
  - Example: 17 Coppers + Talisman + Bank = $18 + $19 = **$37**.
  - 3 Travelling Fairs ($6) give 4 Buys, enough for 3 Provinces + a Duchy.
  - So the flooded deck also has a Province endgame.
- **Tower + Curses:** a Curse is not a Victory card. If Jester empties the Curse pile, each Curse scores +1 to offset its −1. That's a minor point.

**Other cards:**
- **Raze:** best partner is Talisman (each $2 buy gains two). Raze is the cheapest third pile for Tower. Its trash is mandatory, so play it only on Estates early. Its trashing works against a Copper plan. The rating (+2.0) is high, but here it's mostly a cheap Tower card and an early Estate remover.
- **Settlers / Bustling Village:** best partner is a Copper-heavy discard pile, since Settlers pulls one Copper back. It's a fine cantrip. It's a poor third pile because Bustling Village costs $5, which Talisman can't double.
- **Farming Village:** in this deck its dig almost always finds a Copper, so it's a non-terminal cantrip. Talisman can double it. It's a backup third pile.
- **Horse Traders:** +Buy +$3, and Talisman doubles it. Its discards feed Counting House. Its reaction gives a card back against Jester, Haunted Woods and Enchantress.
- **Enchantress:** blanks the opponent's first Action, so it blanks a lone Counting House. Play a Raze, Settlers or Farming Village first to absorb it.
- **Haunted Woods:** strongest against a Province money deck. Against the flood it's weak, because the flooder has played every Treasure before buying, so their hand is usually empty when it triggers. Travelling Fair is an Event, not a gain, so it doesn't trigger Haunted Woods at all.
- **Jester:** against a Copper deck you gain the Coppers it flips, each worth +1 VP, and the pile drains faster. It can also gain Talismans while the pile lasts. It's usable in the mirror.
- **Ratings check:** Talisman (−1.5), Bank (−1.6) and Counting House (−3.1, deprecated, high phi) are rated low in general. Here the low ratings are wrong for a board-specific reason: Tower makes $0 Copper buys worth VP, and these are the cards that buy and use Coppers. Tower (+0.69) and Travelling Fair (+0.45) are average in general, and here they decide the board.

## Plans considered

- **A. Copper flood / Tower slog:** Talisman → Travelling Fair Copper buys → Counting House and Bank payoff.
  - Talismans gone by about turn 7, a third pile by about turn 10, Coppers by about turns 13–15.
  - It needs about 6+ of the 10 Talismans.
  - Weaknesses: an opponent who contests Talismans and Coppers, and missing the third pile.
- **B. Haunted Woods Big Money** (the baseline): $6 around turns 4–5, $8 around turns 6–8, Haunted Woods played about 1 turn in 3. About 4 Provinces by turn 14 and all 8 by about turn 19.
  - Weaknesses: there are only 48 VP in Provinces, and its own 7 Coppers are all it gets from Tower.
- **C. Village engine:** Farming Village or Bustling Village + Haunted Woods / Enchantress draw + Bank, Horse Traders or Jester payload.
  - Rejected. The only trashing is Raze, one card at a time. The draw is all terminal Durations. It ignores the 50+ VP sitting in Copper and Talisman.

**Against their best plan:**
- Against B, Plan A wins by about 30+ VP. Haunted Woods barely touches an empty-handed flooder, and B can't reach 75 VP in time.
- Against Plan A, the best answer is **the mirror**. Every Copper or Talisman they take is a 2 VP swing and hurries the end, so the Talisman split (turns 1–7) and the number of Buys decide it.
  - Late in the game, the side with Counting House and Bank also wins the Provinces.
  - In the mirror, whoever is ahead on Tower cards controls the three-pile ending.

**Challenged:**
- I re-tested Plan C with Horse Traders for +Buy and Bustling Village. It still lacks trashing, peaks at about 2 Provinces a turn, and gives up the Copper VP. It stays rejected.
- How Plan A loses:
  - The opponent wins the Talisman race, which cuts the flood rate in half.
  - Or Enchantress blanks a lone Counting House on the key turn.
  - Or I leave the third pile too late, after Talismans are diluted (Raze buys then gain only about 1.8 each).
- An observant opponent who sees a Talisman opening can join in, so plan to win the Talisman pile.

## Recommendation

- **Plan A, the Copper flood.** Win the Talisman pile, buy Travelling Fair Coppers every turn, and drain a cheap third pile (Raze) while Talisman density is high.
- Add 1–2 Counting Houses and a Bank, so the flooded deck also takes Provinces or Duchies and can end the game whichever way suits you.
- It holds up against money (a VP lead money can't catch) and against the mirror (Talismans first, then out-Buy them).

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Talisman / Silver. (Talisman / Enchantress if the opponent's plan relies on one key Action.)
- **5/2:** Talisman / Raze. Raze clears Estates that would clog flood turns, and every Raze bought adds to the third pile.

**Buy priorities:**
1. Talisman at $4–5 until the pile is gone. At $6+, buy Travelling Fair + Talisman and put the Talismans on your deck.
2. Raze with Travelling Fair while 2+ Talismans are in play.
3. Every spare Buy goes to Copper, and each $2 left over buys another Travelling Fair.
4. One Counting House at the first spare $5 after about turn 6 (put it on your deck with Travelling Fair). Bank at $7. Horse Traders at $4 if you need +Buy.
5. Greening: Tower VP needs no greening. On Counting House or Bank turns from about turn 11, buy Provinces (Duchies once 4 or fewer remain) alongside the Coppers.

**Ending:**
- Copper + Talisman + Raze emptying is a three-pile ending you control, at about turns 14–16.
- Duchies or Farming Village can serve as backup third piles.
- If the opponent races Provinces instead, the Copper VP still holds as long as the Copper pile empties, so make sure it does. Count the last Coppers yourself.

**Watch out for:**
- **Talisman contest:** if they take 4+ Talismans, lean harder on Travelling Fair, Horse Traders and Counting House money.
- **Enchantress:** play a cantrip before Counting House.
- **Copper pile not emptying:** Copper VP only counts if the Copper pile is empty, so never let the game end with Coppers left in the Supply.

**Least sure of:** the flood rate of about 10 Coppers a turn, and turns 14–16 for the three-pile ending. Both come from average draws. If Talismans collide less than assumed, or the opponent mirrors, the ending slips toward turns 17–18. Even then, the VP total is still far ahead of Big Money's.

**Assumed:**
- The Copper Supply is 46 (60 − 14).
- Tower counts starting Coppers as cards from the Copper pile.
- Each Talisman in play triggers separately.
- Travelling Fair isn't a gain, so it doesn't trigger Haunted Woods.
- The file lists "Settlers" with Bustling Village text, which I read as the Settlers/Bustling Village split pile.

**More players:** in 3-player games there are 39 Coppers and the Talisman pile gets contested by two others, so the flood pays less per player. Jester and Enchantress get better.
