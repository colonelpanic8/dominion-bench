## Beyond the key
- Forge(Hunting Grounds) as a "Duchy mill" (trash HG for $6, gain a new HG, and the on-trash gains a Duchy): plausible. Forge needs an exact-$6 gain, and HG's on-trash trigger fires.
- Forge(HG + Estate) → Province + Duchy as an endgame finisher: plausible. $6 + $2 = $8.
- The Forge player can force a Duchy + HG + Estate three-pile: doubtful. Milling takes only one HG per play from a 10-card pile, so this needs many Forge plays and is unlikely to be a real lever.
- Trader on an Estate gains 2 Silvers, and on a late HG gains 6 Silvers plus a Duchy: plausible from this Trader text (a Silver per $1).
- Beggar's reaction turns each opposing Pool play into 2 Silvers: plausible, but limited to one per Beggar in hand, so "each Pool play" overstates it against a Pool deck that plays many.
- Encampment opened early is wasted because without a Gold or Plunder it returns to the pile: plausible from card text.
- Plunder is locked until every Encampment is gone: plausible (split-pile rule).
- Forge with nothing trashed must gain a $0 card, so take Copper: plausible (Copper or Curse).
- Mandarin's on-gain repeats this turn's Treasures next turn, so its main use is a 5/2 opening: plausible text reading, and a reasonable judgement on a board with weak $5s.
- The Scrying Pool engine comes online only around T13 for lack of thinning: doubtful. It ignores Forge as a thinner, and playtesting found the Pool deck competitive.

## Summary
The analysis correctly makes Forge the key card on a no-+Buy board and builds around Hunting Grounds with a little Coin of the Realm, which is close to one of the two decks the hosts found competitive. It falls for the Trader/Silver opening the hosts rejected for hitting $6 too slowly, and it dismisses the Scrying Pool deck, missing Pool's role in lining up Noble Brigand against the opponent's Golds.
