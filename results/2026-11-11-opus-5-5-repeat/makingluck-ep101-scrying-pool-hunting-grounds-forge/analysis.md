# Kingdom analysis: Beggar, Coin of the Realm, Encampment/Plunder, Forge, Hunting Grounds, Mandarin, Noble Brigand, Scholar, Scrying Pool, Trader

**Inventory:**
- **Piles by cost:**
  - $2: Beggar, Coin of the Realm (CotR), Encampment (a split pile with Plunder at $5 underneath).
  - $2 plus a Potion: Scrying Pool. Potion costs $4.
  - $4: Noble Brigand (first edition only), Trader.
  - $5: Mandarin (first edition only), Scholar, Plunder.
  - $6: Hunting Grounds (HG).
  - $7: Forge.
- **Setup:** no landscapes, no Colonies and no Shelters. Two players, standard starting deck, so the opening split is 4/3 or 5/2.
- **Special rules:** Potion, and the Tavern mat for CotR. Plunder gives VP tokens. **No card on this board gives +Buy.**
- **Price points:** $3 is empty. $5 is weak, because Plunder is locked until all 5 Encampments are gone.

**Board:** With no +Buy, the most a turn can buy is one card. The second gain comes from **Forge**, and **Hunting Grounds** is the best thing to feed it. The only cheap trasher is Trader, which is slow, so a Scrying Pool engine has little to work with. The real question is whether Forge-plus-HG money beats plain HG money, and who gets the Duchies.

**Key interactions:**
- **Forge + Hunting Grounds in hand (the "Duchy mill"):** Forge trashes HG ($6) and gains a new HG. The HG's trash trigger also gains a Duchy. You end up with a fresh HG plus 3 VP, and your Buy is still free. You get this whenever Forge and HG collide, which is exactly the hand where one of them would otherwise be a dead terminal. Realistic from about turn 9–10.
- **Endgame finisher, Forge(HG + Estate):** $8 of trashed cards gains a Province, and the trigger gains a Duchy. That's about +8 VP net, plus your normal buy. Forge(Duchy + Silver) and Forge(Gold + Estate) also turn into Provinces.
- **Each Forge(HG) play drains both the Duchy pile and the HG pile.** After that, HG's trigger gives 3 Estates. So Duchy + HG + Estate becomes a real three-pile ending, and the Forge player controls when it happens.
- **Trader on an Estate:** gains 2 Silvers, so turns 3–4 build economy while thinning. Trader on a stray HG gives 6 Silvers plus a Duchy (only worth it late).
- **Beggar vs Attacks:** Scrying Pool and Noble Brigand are both Attacks. If the opponent goes for Pool, each of their Pool plays can become 2 Silvers for you, and Beggar turns into a $2 counter.

**Other cards:**
- **Coin of the Realm:** pairs with HG + Forge. Play HG, call CotR, then play Forge. It's the cheapest way to fix terminal collisions.
- **Encampment/Plunder:**
  - Encampment only stays if you reveal a Gold or Plunder. Before that it goes back to its pile, so as an opener it's wasted.
  - In a deck with Golds it's a lab-village: +2 Cards, +2 Actions.
  - Plunder ($2 and +1 VP per play) is good money payload, but it opens up only after all 5 Encampments are bought and kept.
- **Scrying Pool:** wants an Action-dense, thin deck, and this board can't thin fast. In a money deck it isn't worth a Potion plus a buy.
- **Scholar:** needs villages, which here means Encampment. In money, HG is better draw.
- **Mandarin:** a terminal +$3. Its on-gain effect puts the Treasures you played back on your deck, so your next turn repeats this turn's $. Its only real use is the 5/2 opening; otherwise it competes with HG for the terminal slot.
- **Noble Brigand:** steals Silvers from a Trader deck, but the opponent's Beggar blunts it. Filler at $4.
- **Beggar:** see Key interactions. Otherwise it junks your deck.
- **Community ratings (check):** Scrying Pool and Encampment are rated high. Here Pool lacks thinning and fodder, and Encampment is gated behind Gold. Trader is rated low (−4.0), but it's the only cheap trasher and Estate→2 Silvers fits money well. Forge (−0.8) is underrated on this board because HG is perfect Forge fodder.

### Build lines
- **HG money (baseline):**
  - T1–2: Silver/Silver.
  - T3–4: about $5–6, so HG plus Silver.
  - T5–6: Gold/Silver.
  - T7–9: first Province. HG is played about 2 turns in 5.
  - About 4 Provinces by T15. An HG mirror empties the Provinces around T18–19.
- **Trader / HG / Forge money (pick):**
  - T1–2: Trader/Silver.
  - T3–4: Trader trashes an Estate (+2 Silvers). The other hand buys Silver. Deck is about 14 cards, around $1.07 per card.
  - T5–6: HG at $6, plus another Silver or a second Estate trashed.
  - T7–8: HG hands reach $8–9, so Forge ($7), then Gold.
  - T9+: Province at $8.
  - Whenever Forge meets HG (or Estate + Gold, or Duchy + Silver), Forge them for a Duchy or a Province. That's roughly +3 VP every other turn without spending a Buy.
  - Pick up 1–2 CotR at stray $2–4 hands.
  - Built turn: Province bought, plus Forge(HG) for a Duchy. That's 9 VP in one turn.
- **Scrying Pool / Encampment engine:**
  - T1–2: Potion plus Trader or Silver.
  - T3–8: one card per turn: Pools, Gold to keep Encampments, Encampments, HG.
  - Thinning is only Trader, one card per turn, so Pool keeps stopping on Coppers.
  - Forge comes around T10 or later.
  - Built turn (around T13 or later): Province, plus 1–2 Forges on HG or Estates (Province + Duchy each), plus Plunder tokens. On paper that's 15 or more VP, but it isn't reliable.

**Plans considered:**
- **HG money:** simple and reliable. The ceiling is one Province per turn, and it gives up the Duchy pile.
- **Trader/HG/Forge money:** costs about one Gold (the Forge) and, in return, turns terminal collisions into free Duchies and late Provinces. It needs 1 Forge and 2 HGs, which nobody contests except a mirror. Weakness: terminal collisions before CotR arrives, and Forge reaching $7 late on bad draws.
- **Pool/Encampment engine:** highest ceiling, but one buy per turn and slow thinning mean it comes online around T13. HG money already has 2–3 Provinces by then.

**Recommendation:** **Trader-opened HG money with one Forge as a VP mill**, plus 1–2 Coins of the Realm. It's as fast as the baseline, and it adds about 3 VP per Forge play without using its Buy. That beats plain HG money. It also beats the engine on tempo, and it can end the game on the Duchy, HG and Estate piles if the engine looks like catching up.

**Against their best plan:**
- **Mirror:** whoever gets more Forge plays takes the Duchy split, so get Forge before your second Gold. If they mill Duchies first, buy Duchies at $5–7 sooner, because denying them is worth the points.
- **Against Pool:** buy 1–2 Beggars. Each of their Pool plays can become 2 Silvers for you. Then race them: you should have 3–4 Provinces before their engine runs.
- **Against Noble Brigand:** it steals your Silvers. Gold and Forge plays are the answer, and HG fixes the reduced hands.

**Challenged:**
- **Retried the engine:** best version with a Mandarin or Trader opening, Encampment held by an early Gold, and Forge turning the 3 Estates into Gold or HG. It still needs about 10 single-buy gains before it reaches 2 Provinces per turn. Rejected, but re-check if the opponent opens passively.
- **How the pick loses:** Forge arrives late and collides with HG before any CotR is on the mat. Fix: take CotR at $2–4 hands from T5.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** Trader / Silver.
- **5/2:** Trader / Coin of the Realm. Mandarin / CotR is the alternative if you'd rather hit $6 for HG sooner.

**Buy priorities:**
- First shuffles: Trader the Estates. Buy Silver at $3–5 and HG at $6 (2 copies in total).
- Then: Forge at $7, before the first Gold. After that, Gold at $6–7, Province at $8, and CotR at $2–4.
- Forge(HG) for a Duchy whenever possible. Pick 3 Estates instead once the Duchies are gone.
- Green with bought Duchies at about 4 Provinces left. Save Forge(HG + Estate) → Province + Duchy for the last turns.

**Ending:**
- The usual end is Provinces running out.
- A real alternative is Duchy + HG + Estate. Forge-on-HG drains the first two together, and HG's 3-Estate trigger drains the third.
- Encampment/Plunder can also empty if both players buy it.
- Count the piles every turn once the Duchy pile is down to 3.

**Watch out for:**
- **Opponent going Pool + Noble Brigand:** buy Beggars.
- **Forge playing with nothing useful in hand:** trashing nothing still makes you gain a $0 card, so take Copper.
- **More players:** the Duchy split gets thinner and the attacks hit harder, so Beggar improves and the Forge edge shrinks.

**Least sure of:** whether an Encampment/Gold/HG/Plunder engine with Forge (no Pool) can come online by about T11. If so, its Plunder VP tokens plus Forge gains would beat money. The test is to play its first 10 turns on paper with an early Gold.

**Assumed:**
- Trader's exchanged Silver and HG's gained Duchy/Estates go to the discard pile.
- Forge must gain a card if one exists at the exact cost.
- Encampments that go back to the pile keep Plunder locked.
- Mandarin and Noble Brigand are first-edition-only cards (Hinterlands 1e).
- I didn't play the engine out turn by turn, so its build line above is a sketch.
