# Kingdom analysis: horton-2026-09-finals (2 players, no Colonies, no Shelters, 4/3 or 5/2 by choice)

**Board:** Footpad's rule below its line applies all game: any card you gain during an Action phase also draws you a card. That turns every Action-phase gainer into a draw card. Weaver, Stonemason, Change and Artificer all draw, and Artificer's gain lands on your deck, so you draw that very card. Weaver also plays itself when discarded, and Witch's Hut and Artificer both discard. The payoff is **Triumph**, which gives +1 VP per card gained this turn, and Action-phase gains don't use your Buy. The open problem is +Buy. The Kingdom has none. The only source is **Flourishing Trade**, which turns unused Actions into Buys. It switches on after 5 Sun, and only River Shrine removes Sun, so both players' Shrine plays count toward it.

**Inventory:**
- **$2:** Stonemason
- **$4:** River Shrine, Hostelry, Change, Weaver
- **$5:** Witch's Hut, Footpad, Artificer
- **Potion cost:** Alchemist ($3 + Potion), Vineyard ($0 + Potion)
- **Landscapes:** Exploration (Project, $4), Triumph (Event, 5 Debt), Flourishing Trade (Prophecy, starts with 5 Sun)
- **Non-Supply pile:** Horses
- **Special rules:** Debt, Potion, Coffers and Villagers are all in play.
- **Price gaps:** nothing worth buying at $3 except Silver, and nothing in the Kingdom above $5. After the Prophecy every cost drops by $1. River Shrine and Weaver can then gain $5 cards and Duchies, Stonemason costs $1 and Province costs $7.

**Key interactions:**
- **Weaver + Footpad's rule:** "Gain two Silvers" also draws 2 cards. It works like a Smithy that adds $4 of Treasure to your deck, from turn 3.
- **Witch's Hut or Artificer + Weaver:**
  - A Weaver you discard plays for free: 2 Silvers and 2 cards.
  - Witch's Hut: draw 4, then discard 2 Weavers. That plays both of them (4 Silvers, 4 more cards) and, because both discards are Actions, gives the opponent a Curse. Net, your hand grows by about 5.
  - Artificer does the same without using an Action. Its own gain goes onto your deck and is drawn straight away.
- **Action-phase gains + Triumph:** a built turn gains about 8–9 cards before the Buy phase, so a single Triumph is worth about 9–10 VP for $5. That beats a Province at $8. A second Triumph in the same turn counts one more card. It is limited by the Estate pile: only 8 Triumphs for both players combined.
- **River Shrine + skipping the buy:** trash 2 cards, buy nothing, and gain a $4 card free at Clean-up (a $5 card after the Prophecy). Each play also removes a Sun.
- **Stonemason:**
  - Overpay: at $6 it gains 2 Hostelries or 2 Weavers, at $7 two $5 cards. That's how the engine gets built.
  - Played: it trashes a Silver (from all the Weaver Silvers) for 2 Stonemasons, or a Gold for 2 cards costing up to $5 (2 Duchies once the Prophecy makes them $4), and draws 2.
- **Flourishing Trade + Hostelry and Villagers:** spare Actions become Buys, so 2–3 Triumphs per turn ($5 each). Weaver can also gain a Duchy, worth 3 VP, and it still draws a card.

**Other cards:**
- **Hostelry:** the main village. If you gain it during the Action phase (from Weaver or Change), each Horse also draws a card.
- **Change:**
  - It gives +$3 when you carry Debt, for example unpaid Triumph Debt from last turn.
  - Otherwise it removes Curses (Curse → Silver plus 3 Debt) and turns Gold into Province (plus 2 Debt, still an Action-phase gain).
  - It has a useful mid-priority role.
- **Footpad:** worth more for its rule than as a card. The +2 Coffers help reach $6–7 for Stonemason overpays. The attack is weak against Weaver decks, because their discarded Weavers play and refill their hand.
- **Alchemist:** with a Potion in play it can top-deck itself. It's minor here.
- **Vineyard:** the late VP sink. Stonemason overpays push your Action count to about 20, which makes each Vineyard worth about 6–7 VP for a Potion. Weaver can gain a Potion and draw it.
- **Exploration:** it pays on River Shrine turns and other build turns where you buy nothing. Triumph's Estate gain breaks it, so it's low priority.

**Ratings check:**
- **Weaver (0.59) and Footpad (−0.42)** are rated around the median. Here they are top cards, because of Footpad's rule.
- **Triumph (2.74)** matches its value here.
- **Flourishing Trade (−0.06)** is much stronger here, because it supplies the only +Buy.
- **Exploration (−1.42)** fits: it clashes with Triumph.

**Plans considered:**
1. **Gainer engine** (Hostelry, Weaver×3–4, Witch's Hut, Artificer, Stonemason, River Shrine; scores with Triumph). Curses start around turn 9, Triumph around turn 10–11 at 7–9 VP per turn, and after the Prophecy 15–30 VP per turn. It needs about 4 of the 10 Weavers and a Prophecy by about turn 11. Weakness: if the Weaver split goes against you, or the Prophecy comes late, you get only one Triumph per turn.
2. **Weaver money** (Weaver×2–3 plus Hostelry, buying Gold and Provinces). First $8 around turn 6–7, 4 Provinces around turn 13. Weakness: about 3.5 VP per turn, no answer to the Curses, and Triumph is worth only about 3 VP to it.
3. **Big Money + Footpad (×2) or Witch's Hut.** First $6 around turn 5, $8 around turn 8, attacks about every second turn, 4 Provinces around turn 15. Weakness: Weaver decks shrug off the attack, and it's slower than plan 2.

**Against their best plan:**
- **Engine vs. Weaver money:** by turn 14, money has about 27 VP. The engine has about 25 VP from pre-Prophecy Triumphs plus 20 or more from its first post-Prophecy turn. It also hands money Curses, and money has only Change to remove them. The engine wins clearly.
- **Engine mirror:** decided by the Weaver split (10 cards, about 4 each), Triumph share (8 Estates) and Curse share. Grab Weavers early, and get Witch's Hut before the opponent's deck turns Action-dense.

**Challenged:**
- **Vineyard plan:** I re-tested a Stonemason-overpay Vineyard plan. It peaks at about 1 Vineyard (≈6 VP) per Potion per turn, which is below Triumph. It works as a supplement once the Estates run out, not as the plan.
- **How the engine loses:** it loses the Weaver split, or the opponent sits on River Shrine plays so the Prophecy stays off and you're limited to one Triumph per turn. Another way to lose: the opponent leads on Triumphs and closes out Estates + Curses + a third pile first.

**Recommendation:** Build the gainer engine. It beats any money plan and holds up in the mirror, provided you contest Weavers early.

**Open:**
- **4/3:** River Shrine and Silver. Alternative: Weaver and Silver, then use River Shrine's free gain on Weaver.
- **5/2:** Artificer, and buy nothing at $2. Stonemason is weak until there are Silvers to trash. This split is noticeably worse, so choose 4/3.

**Buy priorities:**
- **Shuffles 1–2:** Weaver (up to about 4), River Shrine ×2, Artificer. At $6–7, Stonemason with overpay.
- **Shuffle 3:** Hostelry to match your terminals, Witch's Hut ×1–2, Change.
- **Scoring:** buy Triumph whenever a turn gains 6 or more cards (from about turn 10). Before the Prophecy, prefer Triumph over Province. After it, use spare Actions for 2–3 Triumphs and gain Duchies with Weaver. Once the Estates are gone, switch to Duchies, Provinces and Vineyards.

**Ending:** most likely on three piles: Estates (Triumph), Curses, then Duchies or a contested Kingdom pile (Weaver or Stonemason). Provinces probably won't empty. Whoever leads on Triumph VP controls when the game ends.

**Watch out for:**
1. A money opponent also benefits from the Prophecy (Province $7, Gold $5). Against money, don't add River Shrines beyond what you need for trashing.
2. Footpad's attack against your Weavers mostly helps you, and yours helps them the same way.
3. If the opponent takes Weavers first, lean on Stonemason overpays and Artificer. Witch's Hut still curses when it discards Hostelries and Horses.

**Least sure of:** how soon the Prophecy fires. The engine's large numbers depend on Flourishing Trade's extra Buys. If only I play River Shrines, the 5 Sun take until about turn 13–14. To test it, re-run the build with one Triumph per turn up to turn 14. I expect it still beats money, but by less.

**Assumed:**
- Footpad's +1 Card works in any player's Action phase, including an opponent's Weaver played off my Footpad attack.
- "Use Action plays as Buys" means unused Actions can be spent as Buys.
- Triumph's Debt has to be paid off before your next buy, and it can be carried into the next turn to power Change.
- River Shrine's Clean-up gain doesn't count as a Buy-phase gain for Exploration.
- Overpaying with a Potion for Stonemason follows the Guilds rule; `rules.md` doesn't cover it.
- Discarding Horses for Witch's Hut counts them as Actions.
