**Kingdom analysis: Forts, Destrier, Ducat, Steward, Cabin Boy, Guide (Reckless), Wishing Well, Ratcatcher, Harbinger, Mystic, plus Exploration**

This is a 2-player game with no Colonies and no Shelters. You choose your opening split (4/3 or 5/2), and 4/3 is clearly better.

## Inventory
- **Costs**:
  - $2: Ducat, Ratcatcher
  - $3: Steward, Guide, Wishing Well, Harbinger, and Tent from the Forts pile
  - $4: Cabin Boy, Garrison, and the Exploration Project
  - $5: Mystic, Hill Fort
  - $6: Destrier (cheaper on turns you've gained cards), Stronghold
- **Forts**: a split pile of 4 each of Tent, Garrison, Hill Fort and Stronghold, in that order. Only the top card is available. **Playing a Tent is the only way to rotate the pile**, so Hill Fort needs two rotations (or all 4 Garrisons bought out) before anyone can buy it.
- **Reckless Guide**: the Trait makes Guide follow its instructions twice.
  - When played it gives +2 Cards and +2 Actions, then goes to your Tavern mat. It isn't discarded from play, so it doesn't go back to its pile.
  - If you later call it, it ends up in play, is discarded in Clean-up, and **returns to the Guide pile**.
  - So each Guide is a one-shot "village plus Lab" that then leaves your deck.
- **Tokens and Durations**: Coffers (Ducat, Exploration), Villagers (Exploration), Durations (Garrison, Stronghold, Cabin Boy) and Reserve cards (Guide, Ratcatcher). There are no attacks.
- **Price gaps**: nothing worth $5 is available on turns 1–2, because Hill Fort isn't exposed yet. That's why 5/2 wastes the extra $1.

## First read
1. **Scoring**: Provinces, Duchies, and Stronghold (2 VP, 4 copies; Cabin Boy can also gain it). There are no VP tokens. The game will almost certainly end on Provinces.
2. **Payload**: mostly cards worth +$2:
   - Tent, Garrison and Steward (terminal)
   - Mystic and Cabin Boy (non-terminal; Cabin Boy pays next turn)
   - Stronghold: +$3, terminal
   - Coffers from Ducat and Exploration
   - Ducat is the only +Buy.
3. **Draw and villages**:
   - Destrier is a Lab.
   - Hill Fort plus Guide adds cards (see below).
   - Steward and Stronghold draw, but they're terminal.
   - The only villages are Villagers (from Exploration) and one-shot Guides.
4. **Trashing**: Steward (2 cards), Ratcatcher (1 card per call, and it comes back), and Ducat (trashes a Copper when gained). Trashing is good.
5. **Attacks**: none, so a money deck isn't punished for being slow.
6. **Standout**: Tent puts itself back on your deck when discarded from play. That means **a Tent you play is in every hand**: a permanent +$2, at the cost of a hand slot and your one Action. It also controls the Forts rotation.

**Summary:** this is a low-power board with no attacks. The real questions are:
- whether a thin Destrier/Hill Fort deck beats Tent-powered money;
- who controls the Forts rotation;
- whether there's enough payload for double-Province turns.

## Key interactions
- **Tent loops itself.** Play Tent each turn, Clean-up puts it on your deck, and you draw it again. That's +$2 every turn from turn 3 or 4, which is worth roughly +$1 per turn more than a fifth random card. Two catches:
  - It uses your only Action, so it collides with Steward. Exploration's Villagers fix that.
  - An unplayed Tent is discarded from your hand, not from play, so it loses the loop until you draw and play it again. Calling a Guide discards your hand, so it breaks the loop too.
- **Hill Fort gains Guide.**
  - "Put it into your hand" mode: play the Guide straight away. Net effect is +1 card and no Actions lost, the same as a Lab.
  - "+1 Card +1 Action" mode: in a deck that reshuffles every turn, the Guide is played next turn for +1 net card and +1 net Action.
  - So 4 Hill Forts ≈ 4 Labs plus up to 4 villages each turn.
  - Guides pile up on the mat. When you draw your whole deck, calling them at the start of your turn costs about nothing and sends them back to the pile.
- **Ducat, Guide and Hill Fort make Destrier cheaper.** Each card you gain earlier in the turn cuts $1 off Destrier.
  - Ducat then Destrier costs $2 + $5 = $7.
  - Ducat, Destrier, Destrier costs $2 + $5 + $4 = $11.
  - Hill Fort gains in the Action phase make Destrier cheaper before you even reach your Buy phase.
- **Exploration rewards gaining outside the Buy phase.**
  - Hill Fort's gains (Action phase) and Cabin Boy's gains (start of turn) don't count against it.
  - Buying a Project isn't gaining a card, so buying Exploration pays out +1 Coffers and +1 Villager on that same turn.
  - Turns spent trashing with Steward are usually turns you wouldn't buy anything, so they also pay out.
  - Over turns 3–8 that's about 3–4 Villagers and Coffers.
- **Hill Fort or Cabin Boy as payload**: Hill Fort can gain Cabin Boy, a cantrip that gives +$2 next turn, so payload grows by $2 per Hill Fort per turn. Late, Cabin Boy can trash itself to gain a Stronghold (2 VP) whenever Stronghold is on top of Forts.

## Other cards
- **Ratcatcher**: a cheap, non-terminal trasher that fits beside a Tent. Good to pick up with spare $2 or a spare Buy.
- **Garrison**: draws next turn for each card gained, so it pairs with Ducat Buys or Hill Fort gains. It's terminal and competes with Tent; it's fine with Villagers.
- **Stronghold**: points late. +3 Cards next turn needs a spare Action. Gain it via Cabin Boy rather than buying it.
- **Mystic**: non-terminal $2, so it fits beside Tent, but Cabin Boy does the same job for $4. It's a fallback buy at $5 when Hill Fort isn't on top.
- **Harbinger and Wishing Well**: the only partner is Harbinger setting up a known top card for Mystic or Wishing Well to name. Two cards for about a Lab's worth of effect is weak. Skip them.

## Plans considered
- **A. Tent money (the baseline)**:
  - How it works: Tent/Silver opening, then Gold, Province, Cabin Boy at $4–5, and the odd Destrier, which is non-terminal and doesn't collide with Tent.
  - Pace: $6 by about turn 4–5, first Province about turn 7, 4 Provinces about turn 13–14.
  - Weakness: it's capped at one Province per turn.
  - Plain Steward money is about a turn slower, because Steward competes with Tent.
- **B. Thin Destrier/Hill Fort deck**:
  - How it works: Tent, Steward and Exploration, then Hill Forts gaining Guides and Cabin Boys, Destriers bought cheaply, and Ducats for the Buys.
  - Pace: building until about turn 11, then double Provinces from about turn 13.
  - Needs: 2–4 Hill Forts and Hill Fort kept on top of Forts.
  - Weakness: an opponent with a Tent can rotate Hill Fort away. It then takes 3 rotations to bring it back.
- **C. Bank Coffers with Exploration for one huge turn**: Coffers come in at about 1–2 per turn, and there's no cost reduction to make a big turn huge. This is rejected as a main plan, but banked Coffers do become the finishing turn of Plan B.

### Build lines (average draws)
- **A**:
  - Turns 1–2: Tent / Silver.
  - Turn 3: Tent + 3 Copper → $5 → Cabin Boy.
  - Turn 4: Tent + Silver + 2 Copper → $6 → Gold.
  - Turns 5–6: Silver or Gold; Tent is now in every hand.
  - Turn 7: first Province.
  - After that: about one Province every 1.5 turns, Duchies from 4 Provinces left.
  - Built turn: $8, 1 Buy.
- **B**:
  - Turns 1–2: Tent / Steward.
  - Turn 3: Tent + 3 Copper → $5. Play Tent and rotate (Garrison now on top). Buy Exploration → 1 Coffers, 1 Villager.
  - Turn 4: Tent + Steward. Spend the Villager, trash 2 Estates, rotate again (Hill Fort now on top). Buy nothing → 2 Coffers, 1 Villager.
  - Turns 5–6: Hill Fort ($5), Cabin Boy or Ducat.
  - Turns 7–8: second Hill Fort. Destrier for $5–7 after a Ducat or Guide gain. Hill Forts gain Guides and Cabin Boys.
  - Turns 9–11: 2–3 Destriers and a 3rd Hill Fort. Steward switches to drawing.
  - **Built turn (about turn 12–13)**:
    - Hill Fort gains Guides, giving +Actions.
    - Tent $2, 3 Cabin Boys $6, 2 Silvers $4, Gold $3.
    - 2 Ducats give +2 Buys and +2 Coffers.
    - Total about $15–17 with 3 Buys, i.e. Province + Province, or Province + Duchy.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Tent money | ~T7 | ~T19–20 alone | 1 Province | about half the Provinces before B's big turns |
| B Destrier/Hill Fort | ~T12 | ~T16–17 | Province + Province / Duchy (+ Stronghold via Cabin Boy) | Provinces + Duchies + 8 VP of Strongholds |

**Against their best plan:**
- **B against A**: A has about 3 Provinces by turn 12. B needs about 3 double turns, on turns 13, 15 and 16. That's a close race that B probably wins, because A doesn't choke but doesn't speed up either, and B has the Ducat Buys and Strongholds for the tiebreak points. Without Hill Fort, B is only about level with A.
- **A's best counter**: A is Tent money, so it can also rotate Hill Fort to the bottom almost for free. Watch the top of Forts every turn.
- **Mirror**: Hill Forts split 2–2 and Guides are contested. More Tent plays means more control of the rotation.

**Challenged:**
- **Re-testing Plan A**: Tent money is strong here precisely because nothing attacks it, and a permanent extra $2 is a real edge. If Hill Fort is denied, B degrades into A plus a few extra Destriers, and that still keeps pace. So B is the "free option" — it is never much worse than A.
- **How B loses**: Hill Fort gets denied early, and the building turns spent on Exploration and trashing never pay back.

## Recommendation
Play **Plan B, a thin Tent/Destrier deck that takes Hill Forts when it can**. Every piece of it is also good in money: Tent, Cabin Boy, Destrier and Ducat. Against a denying opponent it falls back to Plan A without wasted buys, and against plain money its double-Province turns arrive around turn 13.

## Opening
- **4/3**: Tent / Steward. Use the Tent you play on turns 3–4 to rotate toward Hill Fort.
- **5/2 (only if forced)**: Tent / Ratcatcher. No $5 card is available yet, and Ducat can't trash a Copper on a $2 hand.

## Buy priorities
1. **First shuffle**: buy Exploration on a $4+ turn where you'd otherwise buy nothing useful. Trash Estates, then Coppers, with Steward, using Villagers to play it alongside Tent.
2. **Second and third shuffles**:
   - Hill Fort whenever it's on top.
   - Destrier at its reduced price (Ducat first).
   - Cabin Boy at $4.
   - Ratcatcher or Ducat with spare $2.
   - Hill Fort gains: Guide while you need Actions, Cabin Boy once you're drawing your whole deck.
3. **Greening**: green once you reliably hit $16 with 2+ Buys. Use spare Cabin Boys to gain Strongholds from about 4 Provinces left. Hold Coffers for the final turn.

## Ending
The game ends on Provinces. A three-pile ending is unlikely. Ducats (10) could run out, and Guides could too if both players leave them on the mat uncalled. Forts has 16 cards.

## Watch out for
- **The top of Forts every turn.** Rotating Hill Fort away costs the opponent almost nothing, and bringing it back takes 3 rotations.
- **Calling a Guide while holding Tent** breaks Tent's return to your deck. Call Guides only when you'll draw your deck anyway.
- **Over-trashing Coppers** before Cabin Boys and Silvers replace them.

## With more players
With 3+ players, Tents and Hill Forts are contested even harder and the rotation gets chaotic. Lean toward Plan A plus Destriers.

## Least sure of
The B-over-A margin rests on hand-built turn estimates, not a simulation. A turn-by-turn solo run of each plan would test whether B's $16 turns really arrive by about turn 13.

## Assumed
- **Exploration pays out on the turn you buy it.** Buying a Project isn't gaining a card.
- **Played Reckless Guides stay on the mat; called ones go back to the pile.** A Guide played normally stays on the Tavern mat and isn't returned. One that's called is discarded from play and goes back to the pile.
- **Gains outside the Buy phase count for Destrier.** Cabin Boy's start-of-turn gain and Hill Fort's Action-phase gain both lower Destrier's cost that turn.
- **Ratings check**: community ratings put Steward (3.04) and Destrier (2.61) at the top of this board and Mystic and Harbinger near the bottom. That matches this analysis. I rate Tent (−2.17) far above its rating because here it starts on top of Forts from turn 1 and nothing competes with it.
