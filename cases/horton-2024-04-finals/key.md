## Expert summary
The kingdom merges two ideas. In the first deck, Taskmaster is the only
sustainable village, but the only $5 card to keep it going is Duchy, and there
is no Copper trashing. The second is a Stampede/Storeroom deck. After much
tweaking Horton is confident of the dynamic: the Taskmaster deck is stronger
than the Stampede/Storeroom deck, but the Stampede/Storeroom deck can win if it
aims to empty the Duchies before going for Provinces. In the final both players
committed to Taskmaster and the Duchies went very quickly; with both decks
over-terminaled, Behram pivoted toward the Stampede/Storeroom deck, which beat
what Horton saw as a more Madman-focused plan, and won convincingly.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (designer) The Taskmaster deck: Taskmaster is the only sustainable village and repeats when you gain a $5 card, which here mostly means Duchy.
- **R2** [core] (designer) The alternative is a Stampede/Storeroom deck (Stampede gains 5 Horses onto your deck when you have 5 or fewer cards in play).
- **R3** [core] (designer) Matchup: Taskmaster is stronger, but the Stampede/Storeroom deck wins if it empties the Duchies first, cutting off Taskmaster.
- **R4** [supporting] (result) When both players go Taskmaster and Duchies run out, pivoting to Stampede/Storeroom is the better adjustment (it won the final).
- **R5** [supporting] (designer) The board has no Copper trashing, and Duchy is the only $5 card.

## Traps
- **T1** (result) A Madman-focused pivot (Hermit) after Duchies run out; it lost to the Stampede/Storeroom pivot in the final.
- **T2** (result) Over-terminaling a Taskmaster deck once its $5 targets (Duchies) are gone.
