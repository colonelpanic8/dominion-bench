# Key: Making Luck ep. 30 review: City, Bridge, Ranger, Monastery, Salt the Earth

## Sources
- [Making Luck ep. 30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PN7hNBTXZJQ&t=170s),
  2018-06-26, 2:50-12:30. Adam Horton and Jake review the kingdom shown at
  the end of ep. 29, after games against each other and WanderingWinder.
  They point to WanderingWinder's forum posts as "pretty close" (not used
  here).

## Expert summary
Both hosts' preview read was City/Bridge, and the build they describe is:
open Jack of All Trades/Monastery (right whatever deck you play), thin
fast, add a second Monastery quickly, pick up an Ironworks, then a couple of
Rangers to help reach $5. At $5 add Cities, hope for City + Ranger, and
focus the City pile down (Stonemason helps empty it). A Mint at the right
moment clears the Treasures, Coppers and the Jack once Bridges are the
payload. The payoff is a mega-turn of Bridges with many Buys that piles
out or wins outright.

The complication is Salt the Earth: a Big Money player who buys +1 VP and
trashes Provinces from the Supply puts the builder on a clock. Adam came
away thinking a committed Salt-the-Earth money deck is at least as good,
and that his City/Bridge wins came only because opponents didn't salt hard
enough. Jake thinks no strategy dominates. The builder can keep building
and then pile out or outscore a money player whose point ceiling is low,
so the game becomes who keeps the flexibility to pivot between building,
money and tactical salting. Shepherd was bad (its Pasture even helps the
salt player, since Monastery can't trash Pasture from play). Groundskeeper
went unused because Salt the Earth leaves no time for it.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (consensus) Opens Jack of All Trades/Monastery, whatever the later plan.
- **R2** [core] (consensus) Identifies the City/Bridge mega-turn as the board's big-turn deck, with Ranger (+Buy, +5 Cards) for draw and Ironworks/Monastery to build.
- **R3** [core] (consensus) Recognizes Salt the Earth as the main threat or counter-plan: a money player trashing Provinces from the Supply forces a builder to finish quickly.
- **R4** [supporting] (consensus) Build order: thin fast, a second Monastery early, an Ironworks, a couple of Rangers to reach $5, then Cities, aiming for City + Ranger turns.
- **R5** [supporting] (consensus) Focuses down the City pile (Stonemason helps) so Cities get stronger and the game can end on piles.
- **R6** [supporting] (consensus) A Mint at the right time to trash Coppers and the Jack once Bridges are the payload.
- **R7** [supporting] (single expert) Stays flexible: pivots between building, money and tactical salting based on what the opponent commits to.
- **R8** [supporting] (result) Adam won a couple of games with City/Bridge but attributes them to opponents not committing to Salt the Earth; against a bot that never salts, one host's building deck scored about 110-120 VP in 16 turns.

## Traps
- **T1** (consensus) Shepherd: weak here, and its Pasture can't be trashed by Monastery from play.
- **T2** (consensus) Building slowly toward a huge turn while ignoring an opponent who salts.
- **T3** (single expert) Spending turns on Groundskeepers: Salt the Earth leaves no time for them.

## Disagreements
- Adam: committed Salt the Earth money (the acceptable range is going for
  it nearly all-out) is the stronger plan; the City/Bridge mega-turn is not
  easy to set up. Jake: no dominant strategy; the City/Bridge deck can build
  through salting and pile out, and it's about pivoting.
- Whether Stonemason's on-play trashing mattered only because of the Salt
  the Earth clock; they ended undecided.

## Grader notes
- Give full R3 credit to an analysis that sees Salt the Earth as the money
  deck's weapon against City/Bridge, whichever side of the disagreement it
  takes. An analysis that ignores Salt the Earth misses the board's key
  tension.
