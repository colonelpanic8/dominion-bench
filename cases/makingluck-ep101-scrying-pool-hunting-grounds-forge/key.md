# Key: Making Luck ep. 100/101: Scrying Pool, Hunting Grounds, Coin of the Realm, Forge, Noble Brigand

## Sources
- [Making Luck ep. 100](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RQk58SzbZd0&t=3622s), 2020-02-28,
  60:22-75:00: Adam Horton and Wandering Winder's first look ("the kingdom at the end").
- [Making Luck ep. 101](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KZk0j3QU2m4&t=380s), 2020-03-24,
  6:20-15:00: the same hosts report after playing "a decent number of games",
  trying Encampment vs none, Scrying Pool vs none, and Noble Brigand openings.
  The games were published on Adam's YouTube channel.

## Expert summary
First look: there is no +Buy and only Forge thins well (Trader can't remove
Estates profitably). After the probability check (double Silver hits $6 on
turn 3 or 4 about 40% of the time) both hosts open Silver/Silver to reach
Hunting Grounds and then Forge quickly. They build a deck with Coin of the
Realm as the village, Hunting Grounds as the draw, and Forge for thinning.
Forge is also the payload: Forging junk and spare cards is the only way to
gain more than one Province per turn.

After playtesting, Forge is important in every deck. Two decks proved
competitive with close win rates: Coin of the Realm/Hunting Grounds, and a
Scrying Pool deck (with Encampments, which suit it better than Coin of the
Realm). Scrying Pool's real strength here was its attack setting up Noble
Brigand to snipe Golds from the Hunting Grounds player, who needs Treasures
for Provinces. One host thinks the right counter to Scrying Pool/Noble
Brigand is a Trader Silver flood (or Beggar's reaction). Silvers usually
hurt a Scrying Pool deck, and Noble Brigand can't trash them all. The $5s
are weak (Scholar is a reluctant buy), which works in Scrying Pool's favour.
Noble Brigand openings didn't work, and Beggar/Trader combos were a joke.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (consensus + result) Forge is important in any deck here: the main thinning and the only way to more than one Province per turn.
- **R2** [core] (consensus) Opens Silver/Silver to hit $6 (Hunting Grounds/Forge) early.
- **R3** [core] (result) A Coin of the Realm (village) + Hunting Grounds (draw) deck is competitive. "I think they're both competitive." (a host, of this deck and the Scrying Pool deck)
- **R4** [supporting] (result) A Scrying Pool deck (with Encampments) is about equally good; its attack lines up Noble Brigand against the opponent's Golds.
- **R5** [supporting] (consensus) Notes there's no +Buy, so extra money beyond a Province is wasted without Forge.
- **R6** [supporting] (single expert) Counter to Scrying Pool/Noble Brigand: Trader-driven Silver flood (Beggar's reaction is a weaker option).

## Traps
- **T1** (result) Opening Noble Brigand: tried and "wasn't good".
- **T2** (consensus) Building around a Beggar/Trader combo.
- **T3** (single expert) Opening Trader/Silver over Silver/Silver: it doesn't help hit $6 on turns 3-4.

## Disagreements
- Encampment: one host thought it wasn't good; the other liked it, at least
  in the Scrying Pool deck.
- Scrying Pool: one host warmed to it, then off it on ep. 100. After testing,
  both rate it competitive, and one host calls it a 10/10 card that is hard
  to make bad.

## Grader notes
- Two near-equal decks were found. Full credit on the plan for either one,
  provided Forge is in it.
