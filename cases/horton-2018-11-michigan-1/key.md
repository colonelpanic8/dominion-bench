# Key: U-Con 2018 Set 1: Knights, Necromancer, Rogue, Feodum, Hermit

## Sources
- [Adam Horton, "Dominion: Michigan Tournament Summary"](https://adamhorton.com/flog/dominion-michigan-tournament-summary/),
  2018-11-12. Horton (a top in-person player) played this 3-player preliminary
  game at U-Con and won it. He didn't design the board. One game, first-person.

## Expert summary
Horton reads the board as a Knight war: Necromancer and Rogue let Knights be
replayed and re-gained, so no deck is safe from them, and every plan has to
defend against Knight attacks and track what opponents are getting away with.
He opened Hermit/Necromancer, picked up a Feodum and Squires, and "bought"
Madmen while keeping a Hermit or two. When an opponent's Knight hit the other
Knight (and his Feodum), he was effectively the only Knight player (he alone
had Necromancer), so he bought Knights and more Necromancers, used Rogue to
gain good cards from the trash, and finished on Feodum: 10 Feoda at 5 VP
each. He never found time for Cemetery, and Scouting Party was affordable
enough that Coppers didn't hurt.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (single expert) Knight attacks dominate the game: with Necromancer and Rogue, Knights recur, so the plan must defend against them and contest them.
- **R2** [supporting] (single expert + result) Opens Hermit and Necromancer.
- **R3** [supporting] (single expert + result) Uses Hermit to gain Madmen (Squires and cheap gains alongside).
- **R4** [supporting] (single expert + result) Rogue gains good cards out of the trash once Knights fill it.
- **R5** [core] (single expert + result) Feodum is the main VP source: Horton ended with 10 Feoda worth 5 VP each, fed by Silvers (Squire, Feodum trashing, Rogue).
- **R6** [supporting] (single expert) Necromancer replays the Knights (and Zombies) from the trash, which makes owning it a big Knight advantage.

## Traps
- **T1** (single expert) Spending early turns on Cemetery to thin Coppers: Horton always had better things to do, and Coppers didn't hurt much with Scouting Party affordable.

## Disagreements
- None recorded. One opponent opened Knight and lost the initiative when another Knight trashed it.

## Grader notes
- Horton calls the opponent's Knight-on-Knight trade a huge stroke of luck, so
  don't treat Hermit/Necromancer as proven. Credit any analysis that builds a
  Knight-resilient deck with Feodum or Necromancer as the key pieces.
- Wolf Den (-3 VP per singleton) wasn't discussed; credit noting it punishes
  one-off Knights and Castles as a beyond-key extra (my note).
- 3-player game.
