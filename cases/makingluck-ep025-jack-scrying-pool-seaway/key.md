# Key: Making Luck ep. 25 review: Jack of All Trades, Scrying Pool, Mining Village, Seaway, Wolf Den

## Sources
- [Making Luck ep. 25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eSySVIj5g7g&t=532s),
  2018-05-22, 8:52-21:30. Adam Horton and Jake review the kingdom shown at
  the end of ep. 24, after games against each other, against
  WanderingWinder and against bots.

## Expert summary
On 4/3 open Jack of All Trades/Lookout. Jack and Scrying Pool have poor
synergy but are each so strong that the deck plays both. Payload here can
only be Treasure, so the deck stays money-heavy rather than being a pure
Scrying Pool deck: Scrying Pool is a good card, not the crux. Magic Lamp
makes a "one of everything" build path correct for once: the three Wishes
give a big early tempo boost and pick up much of the kingdom, notably
Secret Cave, which works with Jack early (discard, then Jack refills) and
late (draw the deck, discard junk, Lookout is sure to trash something bad).
+Buy matters, so Seaway goes on Mining Village. Its +Actions help early,
and a stack of Mining Villages is an endgame threat: trashing them all for
$2 each with many Buys, so the opponent can't leave piles or the last
Provinces low. Engineers then gain Mining Villages, which tend to empty,
and Cities take over mid-to-late. Buy Potion right after the Seaway, not
before, to avoid a Potion-plus-$7 turn with one Buy. A second Jack is fine,
partly because of Wolf Den. Ironmonger was never the best choice, and
Idols only help against a money/Jack opponent.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (consensus) Opens Jack of All Trades/Lookout on 4/3.
- **R2** [core] (consensus) Builds a deck with both Jack and Scrying Pool despite their poor synergy; payload is Treasure, and Scrying Pool is a good card rather than the backbone.
- **R3** [core] (consensus) Seaway goes on Mining Village (for +Buy and +Actions), with Engineer focusing Mining Villages; the stack threatens a huge final turn by trashing them for $2 each.
- **R4** [supporting] (consensus) Magic Lamp's Wishes make taking one of many kingdom cards early correct; Secret Cave is the standout (Jack synergy early, Lookout setup late).
- **R5** [supporting] (single expert) Buys the Potion right after getting Seaway's +Buy, not earlier.
- **R6** [supporting] (consensus) Gets a second Jack; Wolf Den makes duplicates cheap points.
- **R7** [supporting] (consensus) Cities matter in the mid-to-late game once Mining Villages run low.
- **R8** [supporting] (consensus) Big Money (or money with Jack) is clearly worse than the build here.
- **R9** [supporting] (result) A Potion/Lookout opening with Jack afterwards beat one host once (played by WanderingWinder), but over many games he couldn't make it work: it rarely hits $4 and plays Jack only around turn 6-7.

## Traps
- **T1** (consensus) Seaway on Ironmonger, the hosts' own preview idea for 5/2: Ironmonger was never the best choice here.
- **T2** (single expert) Opening Potion (Potion/Lookout) and delaying Jack.
- **T3** (consensus) Idol as a main plan against a building opponent: the deck is too inconsistent to play Idols every turn; they help only against money/Jack.

## Disagreements
- 5/2: one host now leans to Seaway on Jack, the other refuses to accept
  that but has nothing better; both agree Seaway on Ironmonger was wrong.
- One host would reach the Seaway-on-Mining-Village plan later, valuing the
  endgame threat more than early Actions.
