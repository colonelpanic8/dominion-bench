# Key: Making Luck ep. 99 review: Procession, Steward, Triumph, Treasurer, Swindler

## Sources
- [Making Luck ep. 99](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rBM1ZSderQ&t=216s),
  2020-01-31, 3:36-14:45. Adam Horton and Wandering Winder review the
  kingdom shown at the end of ep. 98 (an episode on Treasurer). They played
  their two preview plans against each other, then both played the
  winner.

## Expert summary
In the preview Adam proposed a money-ish deck: Swindler to disrupt,
Treasurer (taking the Key early), Palace points and Provinces. Wandering
Winder proposed a thin Procession deck aimed at Triumph. Adam's money deck
won their one game, but only through luck and Wandering Winder's
misplays, and Adam came away convinced the Procession deck is "way
better". Its plan: open double Steward (thinning, and a partial defense if
the opponent Swindles), thin hard, then use Procession's exact-$1-more
gains to climb, Settlers into Stewards, Stewards into Processions or
Salvagers, $4s into Treasurers. The payoff is a mega-turn of chained
Processions with Steward and Settlers drawing, which gains many cards, and
Buys from Salvager let it take several Triumphs for huge points. Steward
is the best draw on the board, especially with Procession. Gains are the
limiting resource, so Treasurer gaining Treasures from the trash (such as
Swindled Potions) adds gains and money. Villages: Native Village early,
Settlers more in the middle, and another Native Village or two late, when
Processing leaves many terminals. A Swindler or two in the midgame is still
worth it, since hitting a Procession or Steward hurts this deck.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (consensus) Builds a thin Procession deck that gains many cards in one turn and buys several Triumphs; this beats the money/Swindler/Palace plan.
- **R2** [core] (consensus) Steward is the key early card and best draw (open Steward, likely double Steward); it thins and defends against Swindler.
- **R3** [core] (consensus) Uses Procession's exact-cost upgrades along the chain: Settlers to Steward, Steward to Procession/Salvager, $4s to Treasurer.
- **R4** [supporting] (consensus) Needs +Buy for multiple Triumphs (Salvager), and gains are the limiting factor.
- **R5** [supporting] (single expert) Treasurer adds gains at $5 and money, e.g. gaining Swindled Potions from the trash to Salvage (Wandering Winder).
- **R6** [supporting] (single expert) Native Village early, Settlers in the middle, extra Native Villages late as the deck becomes terminal-heavy (Adam).
- **R7** [supporting] (single expert) A little Swindler in the midgame to hit the opponent's Procession or Steward (Adam; Wandering Winder skipped Swindlers but agreed it matters).
- **R8** [supporting] (result) Adam's money/Swindler/Treasurer/Palace deck won the only head-to-head game, through luck and misplays; both hosts concluded the Procession deck is better, and Adam then played it too.

## Traps
- **T1** (consensus) A money deck with Swindler, Treasurer and Palace as the main plan (Adam's own preview plan).
- **T2** (single expert) Overrating Swindler as deck-wrecking here: it's hard to play enough Swindlers to stop the Procession deck.

## Disagreements
- Treasurer: Adam thinks it isn't very important in the Procession deck;
  Wandering Winder found it useful there (an extra gain price point, money,
  Potions from the trash). They called it a matter of perspective.
- Storing cards on the Native Village mat for the Triumph mega-turn: both
  find it plausible but unresolved.

## Grader notes
- Apothecary, Idol and Distant Lands went undiscussed, as did the Bustling
  Village half of the Settlers pile.
- The hosts stress the deck is far from optimized; credit sensible
  Procession/Triumph lines that differ in detail.
- (My note) Distant Lands is a $5 Action a Procession on a $4 can gain, and
  a plausible extra point source; not in the sources.
