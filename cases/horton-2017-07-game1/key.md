# Key: Horton Summer 2017 finals game 1: Crossroads, Gear, Prince, Duplicate/Duke

## Sources
- [Adam Horton, Summer 2017 Tournament Summary](https://adamhorton.com/flog/dominion-summer-2017-tournament-summary/):
  the designer's write-up of a 3-player finals kingdom he playtested for months. He reused it
  from his previous 2P tournament, where nobody built the deck he liked.

## Expert summary
Horton sees two directions: a Duplicate/Duke deck and a Crossroads engine, and he
prefers the Crossroads engine. Its core is Gear, Crossroads and Prince: about four Gears,
playing two a turn and setting aside four green cards, plus a Princed Crossroads. Each turn
then starts with the starting Estates effectively out of the way, nine cards in hand and
three Actions. Festival and Crossroads give huge draw, and the draw gets stronger as the
deck greens. Windfall adds payload very fast and pairs with Palace, Mill helps hit price
points and adds reliability, and Squire and Duplicate speed growth and scoring. Gear is "the
star of the show". Apothecary looks like it should fit (no Copper trashing, lots of support)
but proved unnecessary in playtesting. At the tournament, at most one player per game built
something like this, and each of them won easily.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (designer + result) Builds a Crossroads engine around Gear, Crossroads and Prince. "Really, it's Gear who is the star of the show here" (Adam Horton)
- **R2** [core] (designer) Gear sets aside green cards (about 4 Gears, 2 played a turn) so turns start with a big, Action-rich hand, and the deck's draw improves as it greens.
- **R3** [core] (designer) Princes a Crossroads for draw and Actions at the start of every turn.
- **R4** [supporting] (designer) Festival plus Crossroads is the draw/Action core; it tolerates Treasures and is reliable, unlike typical Crossroads decks.
- **R5** [supporting] (designer) Windfall adds payload quickly (Golds) and works with Palace for points; commits to multiple Windfalls.
- **R6** [supporting] (designer) Mill helps reach price points with Gear and adds reliability; Squire and Duplicate fit later for growth and scoring.
- **R7** [supporting] (designer) Names Duplicate/Duke (4-6 Duplicates stored on the Tavern mat, then a turn gaining many Duchies, then Dukes) as the main alternative.

## Traps
- **T1** (designer) Apothecary as a key card: it has support here but the deck is fast and reliable without it.
- **T2** (designer) Talisman, Crossroads or Squire in the Duplicate/Duke deck: Horton says they don't fit there.

## Disagreements
- None recorded. Only the designer's view; tournament players who built a weaker version of the
  Crossroads deck won their games.

## Grader notes
- 3-player game. Credit an analysis that chooses Duplicate/Duke only partially on R1; Horton
  calls it a real option but weaker than the engine.
- Mine: Gear's set-aside green and Princed Crossroads is a non-obvious interaction; an analysis
  that finds Crossroads + Gear without Prince deserves partial credit on R3.
