# Key: Horton 2025-10 Tiebreaker Kingdom: King's Court, Stowaway, Monkey, Alley, Credit

## Sources
- [Adam Horton, "October 2025 Tournament Summary"](https://adamhorton.com/flog/october-2025-tournament-summary/),
  Cincinnati tournament, October 2025. Horton designed and playtested this as a
  backup kingdom; it was used for a tiebreaker game in the bracket.
  [Kingdom sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1i-xyLMNrixItE8fQi91r3od4uZAdHhdTSi86wCznmnw).

## Expert summary
The idea is a board with no trashing where Loots (from Cutthroat) are the big
payload, and King's Court still works by multiplying duration draw. Monkey and
Stowaway are good enough when King's Courted to give some very good turns; the
deck is clumsy to set up but worth building. In playtesting Horton found that
King's Court played from hand can target a Shadow card played from the deck
(Alley here), which makes the deck more reliable and easier to set up than he
intended. A nice endgame trick: King's Court an Improve to turn three of your
King's Courts into Provinces.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (designer) Builds a King's Court deck despite no trashing, using King's Court on duration draw (Monkey, Stowaway).
- **R2** [core] (designer) Payload is Loot (from Cutthroat); there is no trashing on the board.
- **R3** [supporting] (designer) King's Court can choose a Shadow card (Alley) from the deck as its target, making the deck more reliable.
- **R4** [supporting] (designer) Endgame trick: King's Court an Improve to turn King's Courts into Provinces.
- **R5** [supporting] (designer) The deck is clumsy to set up but still worth going for.

## Traps
- **T1** (designer) Concluding that no trashing means King's Court can't work here.

## Grader notes
- Horton's description is brief; credit sensible use of Credit (gain an
  Action or Treasure up to $8 for Debt) to get King's Courts early, and the
  Inherited Stowaway starting card, as extras.
