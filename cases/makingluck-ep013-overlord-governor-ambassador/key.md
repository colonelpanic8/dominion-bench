## Expert summary
The Ambassador war decides the early game, and Governor is the main way to
win: there is no +Buy, so the only ways to take more than one Province a
turn are turning on City fully (two empty piles) or Governor, and the City
route is usually too dangerous because the opponent can win on their next
turn. After play, both hosts agree the right opening is Overlord. While the
Ambassador war is on you make only a dollar or two a turn, so its Debt
barely hurts. You pay the Debt off when you can and buy another Overlord
whenever you're debt-free. The only consistent pattern in who won their
games was who had more Overlords. Overlord is Ambassador early, City when
you draw Envoy, Procession when needed, and Governor late, so one card goes
from deck control to payload. Buying the real Ambassador, Lookout or
Governor to save money didn't matter much. Provinces mostly come from
Governor remodeling Gold into Province, so money matters mainly to pay off
Debt. Procession is underused: Processioning an Envoy into a Governor
rescues a bad shuffle. In the endgame the leader greens first, ideally
starting with a three-Province turn; Governor's Silvers clog the opponent's
deck late.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (consensus) Winning the Ambassador war (thinning and junking the opponent with Ambassador) is the early priority.
- **R2** [core] (consensus) Governor is the main payload; with no +Buy, it (remodeling Gold into Province) is the way to take several Provinces a turn.
- **R3** [core] (consensus) Opens Overlord; its Debt doesn't matter early because the deck makes little money during the Ambassador war.
- **R4** [core] (consensus) Keeps adding Overlords whenever debt-free; Overlord's flexibility (Ambassador early, City/Procession mid, Governor late) is the key to the board. "Overlord is just a really good card here." (a host)
- **R5** [supporting] (result) In their games, the player with more Overlords was the one who won.
- **R6** [supporting] (consensus) Money is needed mainly to pay off Debt; Provinces come from Governor's remodel, not from buying.
- **R7** [supporting] (consensus) Procession is strong here, e.g. Processioning Envoy into a Governor.
- **R8** [supporting] (consensus) Turning on City fully (emptying two piles) is too risky as the multi-Province route, since the opponent may win on their next turn.
- **R9** [supporting] (consensus) The player ahead greens first, ideally opening with a three-Province turn; late Silvers from Governor hurt the opponent.

## Traps
- **T1** (consensus) Opening double Ambassador or Ambassador/Lookout instead of Overlord: one host's pre-game choice, which he said after play was a miss.
- **T2** (consensus) Relying on emptying two piles to power City for multiple Provinces.
- **T3** (single expert) Buying the plain Ambassador, Lookout or Governor instead of more Overlords to save money.

## Disagreements
- Pre-game (ep. 12), one host liked Overlord as the opener because it is useful
  in every phase; the other wanted double Ambassador or Ambassador/Lookout.
  After play the second host conceded Overlord was best.

## Grader notes
- Royal Blacksmith, Wishing Well, Haggler, Arena and Triumphal Arch were not
  discussed. (My note) Both Landmarks reward Action-heavy decks (Arena pays
  for discarding an Action at the start of the Buy phase; Triumphal Arch
  scores the second most common Action), so remarks on them are plausible
  extras, not misses.
- The hosts also joked that this board shouldn't be played 3-player because
  of Ambassador; ignore.
