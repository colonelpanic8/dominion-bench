# Key: Horton Summer 2017 finals game 4: Hoard, Keep, Battlefield

## Sources
- [Adam Horton, Summer 2017 Tournament Summary](https://adamhorton.com/flog/dominion-summer-2017-tournament-summary/):
  the designer's short write-up of a 3-player finals kingdom; he doesn't commit to a best plan.

## Expert summary
Horton says the game tends to end very fast, so there isn't time to do everything. One
path is Fishing Village/Forager/Jack/Watchtower for a while, then pushing Treasures into the
deck quickly with Hoard. Other options include Journeyman for draw, Charm or Coin of the
Realm as support, and Counterfeit/Hoard or Fool's Gold as payload. These mix into many
decks, and which is right depends on the opponents. Keep is the complication, since in a
3P physical game there are eight Treasure piles to track. Battlefield matters too: Keep
tempts you to keep building, but Battlefield punishes that here. Horton calls it a really
tough board whose play varies with the opponents.

## Rubric
- **R1** [core] (designer) Expects a short game, so it doesn't plan a long build.
- **R2** [core] (designer) Treasure flooding with Hoard (after an early Fishing Village/Forager/Jack/Watchtower phase) is a main path.
- **R3** [supporting] (designer) Keep makes tracking and winning Treasure majorities a key decision.
- **R4** [supporting] (designer) Battlefield argues against over-building just to chase Keep points.
- **R5** [supporting] (designer) Notes other options: Journeyman draw, Charm/Coin of the Realm support, Counterfeit/Hoard or Fool's Gold payload.

## Traps
- **T1** (designer) Building for a long time to maximize Keep while ignoring Battlefield points.

## Disagreements
- None recorded; the designer himself leaves the plan open.

## Grader notes
- Quality C: grade mainly on whether the analysis reads the short clock, the Keep/Battlefield
  tension and Hoard as the Treasure-gain engine. Many concrete plans are defensible.
